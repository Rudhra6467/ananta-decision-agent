# Testable hypotheses (the register)

This register restates the teaching of Raunak A (Trade With Trend) as hypotheses that can be checked. It draws on 1,552 candidate statements extracted from his videos.

**None of these is validated.** Each is his claim or his method, written so it can be tested. Before any item could become a trading rule, it has to pass the research rules:

- Pre-register the exact definition, thresholds and horizon before looking at results.
- Count every variant tried, including threshold sweeps and alternative moving-average lengths, so the multiple-testing burden is known.
- Use realistic costs: Indian brokerage, STT and impact, and the equivalent costs in Canada and the US.
- Keep a sealed out-of-sample holdout that is opened once.

**How the table was built.**

- Duplicates were merged. He returns to the same ideas many times, especially breadth, sector relative strength (RS) and 50 DMA pullbacks. Merged rows list every source video.
- Thresholds that vary across videos are shown as a range, not averaged.
- Rows are ordered roughly by how central the idea is to his method and how codable it is.
- "Markets" means where the test could run. IN = India, CA = Canada, US = United States. "Adapt" means the instrument or threshold must be re-fitted.
- The notes carry no video dates. So this register cannot say how his teaching changed over time. Disagreements between videos are flagged instead.

## The 10 to test first, and why

1. **TWT-H01 (breadth regime for breakouts).** This is the most repeated idea in the corpus (12 videos) and the filter behind most of his sizing decisions. It needs only constituent prices with point-in-time membership. Sweep the 25–40% threshold as a counted family, not as free tuning.
2. **TWT-H02 (index below 50/200 DMA).** This is his main market filter and it is fully mechanical. hKnYYRpd63s makes a concrete claim (~75% of longs lose) that can be checked directly.
3. **TWT-H03 (regime-scaled risk).** This is what he actually prescribes when H01 and H02 fire: cut risk from 2% to 0.25–0.5%. It can be tested on the same trade list as H02, so the added data cost is zero.
4. **TWT-H04 (top-RS sectors).** This is the core of his sector-then-stock funnel. Sector indices are available in all three markets and the ranking rule is simple.
5. **TWT-H09 (50 DMA pullback in leaders).** This is his signature entry and it is precisely specified: rising 50 DMA, volume below the 50-day average, bullish close. The comparison arm (no volume or candle condition) is built into the claim.
6. **TWT-H10 (extension above the 50 DMA).** This is simple and widely cited. It also directly measures whether his "don't chase" rule earns its keep. Test the 5/8/10/15% thresholds as a pre-declared grid.
7. **TWT-H13 (RS confirmation at breakout).** This is cited in 9 videos, uses only price data, and is a clean two-arm test.
8. **TWT-H20 (25/75 staged entry).** This is his main breakout execution rule, and it makes a quantified claim (failed-breakout loss of about a quarter). It is testable on any breakout list once a pullback-confirmation rule is fixed.
9. **TWT-H22 (−3/−5/−7 staged stop).** This is fully numeric, appears in three videos, and is cheap to test against the single −7% stop and no-stop baselines.
10. **TWT-H28 (correction-RS stocks).** This drives his post-correction stock selection and appears in 14 videos. It needs only prices plus an objective correction-dating rule (e.g. index drawdown ≥10% from its high).

These ten share three traits: they are central to how he builds positions, they are mechanically defined or close to it, and they run on price data alone. H01, H02 and H03 can share one engine and one trade list, which keeps the look-count low. India-only flow hypotheses (H48) and intraday profile work (H59, H60) are deferred because they need more specialised data.

## What is not testable as taught, and what it would need

- **Elliott wave claims** [HcDysbOyn8A, JMZFlzfNOCQ, KvhIh2rU0R0]. These include the three impulse rules, wave 3 being longest, wave 5 equalling wave 1, and wave 2 sharp versus wave 4 sideways. Counting is subjective and the notes themselves mark the conceptual claim as untestable. A fixed, algorithmic swing-labelling procedure would be needed, applied without hindsight. Even then, it tests the algorithm, not his counts.
- **Judgment calls and single-episode forecasts.** Examples:
  - Leverage unwinds after news shocks [butE8gDPXxw], marked as needing judgment.
  - Nifty beating the S&P, Asia and metals over one specific 6-month window [butE8gDPXxw].
  - Episode-specific levels such as dollar index ~110 [zHzq3uAFJFs] or Brent 99 [Sb6KTt64YKE].
  - Tariff-period recoveries [B1ynZNvqesE], marked as not codable.

  A single episode is one observation. These need restating as general rules with thresholds defined in advance.
- **Undefined terms.** The following need numeric definitions before any test: "wide-range" and "narrow-range" Heikin Ashi candles [H0XAVFBdbzw, FtxgOtPYqVA, L99LgSdCDD8], "decisive" closes [xzR6bwNgb18, Vz5lqc_RO7M], a "prominent" HVN [p03ho_Rhc_E], the "base-tightness" criterion [sIo5XQqZ0b0], and his "strong stock" definition, which the video does not give [NYWbbvaJnPc]. Use, for example, ATR multiples, close-location fractions and point-of-control volume share. Each choice adds to the look-count.
- **Chart-angle rules.** The 45-degree and 60-degree slope rules [tTdy5srpW9Q, K06GqbJwEv4] depend on chart scaling. They must be converted to a rate of change, such as % per day.
- **Unnamed or unreconstructable indicators.** These include the breadth gauge in VCNsjVuGoWQ, the 40–45 metric in jcWYGNInYg8, and the ATR-scaled sharp-decline breadth in elpQ8wyLZLQ. The formulas are not in the notes and would need to be recovered from him.
- **Pattern and pivot subjectivity.** This affects cup-and-handle, VCP, "valid structure" watchlists [mim7ylyaN74] and stage boundaries. A coded detector with fixed parameters is needed, and results will be about that detector.
- **India-only or proprietary data.** These include delivery volume [jBYC0t30fz8], promoter holding [488FDla8kuc, WI0tdUjH_Vg], F&O-list eligibility [Tkx2xIboYsw, QWcb7yxYAxk], mutual fund sector holdings [Q-uCkMMTh5Q], and option-chain open interest [kkJ4m9JQ4pM]. They are testable in India with NSE and SEBI archives, but only proxies exist in Canada and the US.
- **Trader-outcome claims.** Examples: retail F&O traders do worse than cash traders [eeKRfAbbvZA, arFNgAPisHU]; "a trader who lost nothing in a down market" generalises better [6ZKLrl0z8jY]; 20–25% CAGR is sustainable [6ZKLrl0z8jY, wtWoGmPsvTY]. These need broker-level account data or a fully specified system. They cannot be tested from prices as stated.
- **Hindsight-built screens.** Studies of what 10x winners looked like beforehand [Bqp9jKQCs30, ag4pL-OONoo] suffer from survivorship bias. They need a forward-constructed universe that includes delisted names.
- **Thin samples.** The 8–14% washout band [6GqBBVt6fUs], multi-year bear spacing [hpbDmEK-FvM], and post-2003/2009/2020 small-cap leadership [JcxWhCO0hqI] rest on only a handful of Indian episodes. The 6GqBBVt6fUs claim is also ambiguous about whether the band refers to stocks above or below the 200 DMA. These can be described but not validated statistically. Pooling with Canadian and US history is the only route to a usable sample.
- **Third-party statistics.** Bulkowski's pattern statistics [_--GkDAomPk] are not his own claim. They would need to be re-run, not taken as given.
