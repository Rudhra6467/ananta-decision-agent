# Sectors and intermarket

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits

In his method the sector is the first filter. He picks it before any stock and judges it mainly by its relative-strength (RS) line against a broad index, usually the Nifty 500. He then checks price structure (higher highs and higher lows, position against a rising 50 DMA or 30-week MA), sector breadth (share of members above the 200 DMA), and whether "sister stocks" in the same group confirm. Money is concentrated in the few groups that lead, and he watches which groups hold up through corrections because he expects them to lead the next leg.

Around this sits an intermarket layer he uses to set how aggressive to be:
- the US as the "mother market"
- India's relative cycle against the world, emerging markets (EM) and China
- the dollar index (DXY) and the rupee
- crude oil
- US yields and central-bank stance
- foreign (FII) versus domestic (DII) flows

The intermarket layer rarely produces an entry. It tells him whether to be fully invested, nimble or defensive. Every statement below is his claim or method, a hypothesis to test, not established fact.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Sector selection by relative strength

**What it is.** Plot each sector index divided by a broad benchmark and keep only sectors whose RS line is making higher highs and higher lows. He deliberately reads the RS line before looking at the sector's own price [BxY2ofigO8I, Yxo-rYOCuAo, c-0bHPew1fg, JJEB-UZgK4E, LWoEC9OjajE].

**Conditions, as he states them across videos:**
- **Benchmark.** Nifty 500 in most videos [LWoEC9OjajE, c-0bHPew1fg, Yxo-rYOCuAo]. An older video uses Nifty 50 [JJEB-UZgK4E], and one allows "top 500 / top 100" [BxY2ofigO8I].
- **RS lookback.** 1 year, sometimes 2 [Yxo-rYOCuAo]. He says a sector RS trend takes about 6–7 months to develop (metals example), so chasing whatever led over the last 2–3 weeks misses the big trends [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- **Structure filter.** Higher highs and higher lows, price above a rising 50 DMA, and rising RS. Wait for pullbacks toward the 50 DMA rather than buying blindly [Oq4FjWEDbd0, DCG1GO9TGFU, yEKNcJ4EH0U, fFUPLUEhzVo, x2RbjJp1eCI].
- **Avoid.** Sectors whose RS line makes successive lower highs and lower lows with repeated pivot breaks; he reads this as distribution [BxY2ofigO8I, 6qExRlD11sE]. Do not trade a sector that keeps closing below its 50 DMA. Wait for it to reclaim the average, base and show a breakout [B8HZvUkY5sA, d3-OMTGOGx8].
- **How many sectors.**
  - "1 to 3" in the right structure at any time [JJEB-UZgK4E].
  - Top 3 [eeKRfAbbvZA].
  - 9–10 sectors cut to 2, roughly 900 stocks down to roughly 200 [BxY2ofigO8I].
  - "a couple" [d3-OMTGOGx8].
  - Later he keeps 6–8 sectors on radar [KDSbZ0oW2U0] and puts 85–90% of capital into 5–6 pockets [DCG1GO9TGFU].
  - So the number of sectors has widened over time.
- **Review cadence.** Every week or every 15 days. Do not buy a sector and sit on it, because leadership rotates every few months [LWoEC9OjajE].
- **Tools.** Three checks: RS rank or trend, sector breadth, market breadth [Jpu536_AmGc]. He also ranks sector ETFs, which carry volume, by trend plus RS [yEKNcJ4EH0U]. Another version scans sector or theme indices for breakouts, then the NSE constituent list for the same pattern [q26J58h5Qt8]. Repeating industry labels among his top 50–100 ranked stocks reveal the leading pockets [VCNsjVuGoWQ].
- **Index choice changed.** In 2017 he preferred exchange-defined sector indices over custom baskets, on the grounds that custom baskets pull in weak companies [488FDla8kuc]. Recent videos build custom indices on TradingView for niches with no official index: hospitals, insurance, rare earth, two-wheelers, small banks [DCG1GO9TGFU, lRUDp336l7Q, rbSj4pOg3_I, 0Hnffbj5pgE]. His stated reason is that parent indices are distorted by a few heavyweights, for example autos dragged by four-wheelers.

**Persistence claim.** Strong sectors keep getting stronger and weak ones weaker (credited to Livermore) [c-0bHPew1fg, Yxo-rYOCuAo]. A weak sector keeps weakening unless a massive catalyst arrives; IT is his example of "not a blind buy" [S--7ta4FmbY]. Once a sector turns, the move usually lasts many months, so there is no need to rush [NUDjIohACWs].

**How he teaches it.** High clarity, roughly 25 videos across 8 years [kkJ4m9JQ4pM, arFNgAPisHU, jlpd-YpqwiQ, zWYCYySQVNc, mim7ylyaN74, sIo5XQqZ0b0, rxqOUxO_vP4 among others]. This is his most consistent teaching in the chapter.

### 2.2 Sector breadth and snapshot tables

- **Breadth heat map.** For each sector, the percentage of members above the 200 DMA, taken in monthly snapshots. Sector strength "means little" unless most members are above the 200 DMA. He says it can be built in a spreadsheet or Chartink [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
  - Readings on 1 June 2025: defence about 85% (up from 19–25% in April), capital markets 71% (from about 16%), finance about 85%, banks about 80% (only fell to about 52% in the correction), IT about 30% [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
  - Other snapshots: capital goods 59%, banks 50%, PSU banks 17% [0nbQs92kYuY]. Aerospace and defence 90% above 50 DMA, 86% above 200 DMA, about 56% in Stage 2 [c-0bHPew1fg].
  - When a group is split near 50%, as financials were (small banks and NBFCs strong, large banks and insurance weak), he scores it "mixed" [0nbQs92kYuY].
- **52-week range table.** Each sector's position within its one-year range. When nearly all sectors sit at yearly highs, as in July 2024, he reads the market as extended [r11n7ztY2YY].
- **Sub-industry breadth.** If banks, insurance, capital markets, AMCs and finance companies all turn up together, he reads a sector-wide move [azzUmy2l_0U]. In capital markets, a breakout carried by only 2 of 10–15 stocks was treated as weak participation [6GqBBVt6fUs].

Clarity is high in WI0tdUjH_Vg, otherwise medium. He gives no explicit buy threshold; the ">75% after a trough below 25%" figure is the notes' test framing, not a stated rule.

### 2.3 Quadrant classification (RRG-style)

- **Two dimensions** [6K1LOJqZYEg]:
  - Near-term participation: up to 1 week.
  - Core trend: 3–6 months.
- **Four quadrants**, applied across a 56-sector universe:
  - Leading: strong on both.
  - Weakening: core strong, near term weak.
  - Improving: core forming, near term strong.
  - Lagging: soft on both.
- **RRG charts.** He also uses relative rotation graphs with his own sensitivity settings, read as dated snapshots. He notes that different software gives different readings, and his settings are not stated [c-0bHPew1fg, Oq4FjWEDbd0].
- Clarity is medium to low and the settings are unreproducible.

### 2.4 Leadership through corrections and at bottoms

His main idea here is that groups falling least in a correction, or holding their 50 DMA or higher lows, are the likely next leaders.
- **Examples:** IT, digital, pharma and private banks in the late-2024 correction [dERi8MUU5es, V41BUpPK3E4, s1ZCPQI5894]. Banks, finance and hospitals during a fear period [g3mcqf6rqoc]. CPSE broke out and rose about 15–20% while the market fell [NtpyZH5cfaE]. PSU banks, pharma, defence and metals held higher lows on weekly charts in a weak market [Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA, r63sarc_qV8].
- **Laggard variant.** Prefer groups that rose with the market in 2022–24 without blowing off; they are the ones holding now [iWa7MbGawvU].
- **Valuation and performance grid.** In a roughly 15-day correction [Pm_1kb8Fku0]:

  | Group | Approximate fall |
  |---|---|
  | Overvalued outperformers | 25–35% |
  | Undervalued outperformers | 15–25% |
  | Overvalued underperformers | 15–20% |
  | Undervalued underperformers | 5–7% |
  | Broad index | about 5% |

  He describes three phases: correction, consolidation, leadership. Resilient groups break out before the index. Leaders that had run to about 3 standard deviations above mean valuation fell most [dERi8MUU5es].
- **Confirmation sequence after a low** [B1ynZNvqesE]:
  1. Identify the groups with high RS that fell least.
  2. Those groups must also lead the rebound between day 4 and day 15.
  3. Days 10–15: they must pull back on lower volume, with no wide high-volume bearish bars. Sector volume is the sum of constituent volume.
  4. Compare ATR over the last 10–15 days across groups. Low clarity; no outcome is stated.
- **Supporting conditions.** Leaders holding above the 50 and 200 DMA after a big run are unlikely to retest their lows [B1ynZNvqesE]. He wants the leaders to show volume and good quarterly earnings [ywccnKeWXNo]. A sustainable rally needs new leaders that hold their swing lows when the index consolidates; prior-cycle leaders such as defence, railways and capital goods had broken [ywccnKeWXNo].
- **Pullback or correction.** Track only the leaders. If they stay intact it is a pullback; if they crack, raise cash [Qu49zHwEGwo]. Leaders must also be backed by second-tier groups setting up [g3mcqf6rqoc].
- **Tension.** In the first leg of a V-shaped recovery he expects the most beaten-down sectors (Bank Nifty, four-wheelers, PSU banks) to bounce hardest, while groups that held up (IT, CPSE) lag [-qQntPmfe-s]. He also warns that sharply beaten sectors can rally to the 50 or 200 DMA and then retest their lows [JcxWhCO0hqI]. The two views may be reconcilable by horizon (first 20–40 days versus months after), but he never reconciles them himself.

### 2.5 Pullback versus chase; crowding and consensus

- **Don't chase.** Do not buy a sector making headlines after a vertical run, for example realty up about 50% in two weeks. Look instead for sectors consolidating inside a larger uptrend; earlier leaders all consolidated before leading [S--7ta4FmbY]. Do not chase euphoric defence or capital-markets moves; buy them back on pullbacks to the trend base [fFUPLUEhzVo, p5O_uspLu9k]. Consensus sectors such as pharma and healthcare drift into sideways consolidation [6K1LOJqZYEg, SuYHqw5zA8U, lRUDp336l7Q].
- **Contrarian strand.**
  - Alpha comes from low-consensus pockets [6qExRlD11sE, Tkx2xIboYsw].
  - Buy a sector that is out of favour while its structure is still valid [rxqOUxO_vP4].
  - Start systematic accumulation when the street is negative: brokers bearish, mutual funds not buying, low delivery volume. His example is banks, which houses were bullish on near 32,000 and negative on after the fall to about 16,000–17,000 [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].
  - Follow charts, not news; PSU banks led for 3–4 months under bearish headlines [SbtFCxbIB1w, c-0bHPew1fg].
- **Momentum versus risk-reward as a style choice.**
  - "Momentum" sectors are above a rising 50 DMA near highs but offer poor risk-reward.
  - "Risk-reward" sectors have pulled back to or below the 50 DMA.
  - Pick one style [B8HZvUkY5sA, q26J58h5Qt8]. He gives continuation-theme risk-reward as only about 1:1 to 1:1.5 [q26J58h5Qt8]. He avoids momentum names whose trending pivot sits 20–25% or more below price because the stop cannot be defined [Y4TewQHVhvc].
  - Later the same idea is framed as momentum pockets versus perceived-value pockets; a balanced investor can hold both [0Hnffbj5pgE].
- **Paused leaders are not dead.** A leader pausing or retracing above its 50 or 200 DMA should not be read as finished [Jpu536_AmGc, S--7ta4FmbY, N2dUTcb9CzQ].
- **Tension.** "Strength gets stronger, weakness gets weaker" [c-0bHPew1fg, Yxo-rYOCuAo, S--7ta4FmbY] sits uneasily with "buy what is out of favour" [Q-uCkMMTh5Q, rxqOUxO_vP4]. The bridge he implies is that the out-of-favour sector must still have valid structure. Note also that "risk-reward" sectors below the 50 DMA [B8HZvUkY5sA] would be barred by his own rule in the same video against trading sectors sustaining below the 50 DMA.

### 2.6 Inside a strong sector: drill-down and size

- **Pick stocks that mirror the sector chart.**
  - Above a rising 50 DMA with a rising RS line [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
  - Weekly version: above a rising 30-week MA with rising RS and clean candles, his Weinstein-style drill-down, for example the roughly 104-stock IPO index [lRUDp336l7Q].
  - A strong-RS stock sitting at its 50 DMA in a clean pattern and turning up [SbtFCxbIB1w].
  - Stocks that break out before their sector index are treated as leaders (Axis Bank before Bank Nifty) [1jsLESmVLVU].
- **One stock per sector.** In an older large-cap method he took only one name per sector, the RS leader (for cement: ACC, Ambuja, UltraTech) [dV4MiNaAenI].
- **Smaller names inside the sector.** He prefers them because they give more alpha [0nbQs92kYuY, c-0bHPew1fg, kwbfXjRtEkk].
  - Within autos: 3,000–30,000 crore market cap [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
  - A market-wide "sweet spot" of 10,000–35,000 crore that held flat or made highs while large caps were sold [r63sarc_qV8].
  - The private bank index rose about 20% because small banks surged while marquee banks did not [kwbfXjRtEkk].
  - Large caps are still kept for portfolio stability [DCG1GO9TGFU].
- **Split sub-sectors.** Use equal-weight sub-indices, for example two-wheelers versus four-wheelers, on weekly and daily charts [rbSj4pOg3_I, lRUDp336l7Q]. He also notes Bank Nifty's rise was PSU-led while large private banks were flat for 2–3 years [6GqBBVt6fUs].
- **Sector context on positions.** He held Cadila partly because pharma was strong and cut Emami as FMCG lost momentum [eeKRfAbbvZA, 4CzLWJxP4UM]. In one review, sector-index support did not save two of the trades [uw5QQOenJnQ].

### 2.7 Sister stocks and group moves (long and short)

**Long side.**
- When one stock sets up, check its same-sector peers; if they show the same pattern, he treats the trade as high probability. Credited to Livermore [jlpd-YpqwiQ, _--GkDAomPk, uFFAAZNSozg].
- Numbers:
  - About 3–5 peers breaking out of consolidation in the same period before a Stage 2 investment (private banks example) [Bqp9jKQCs30].
  - The top 3 sector leaders sharing the same structure before starting a systematic plan [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].
- Skip a stock that looks fine alone if peers do not confirm. In 2009–10 he would have avoided SBI because most PSU banks did not confirm [2Yv0E57_bs4].
- Group breakouts, such as hotels, chemicals, Kirloskar group, Mahindra group, and the 2022 defence and shipbuilding names, are one of his "most powerful" signs [VET4JHrc2vU, uw5QQOenJnQ, mR0HMssJek8, T4849hhdwTo].
- Sector names often break out in sequence (metals: Jindal Steel, APL Apollo, Tata Steel), which gives several entry chances [mim7ylyaN74].

**Short and reversal side.**
- The same peer check is applied to short signals [uFFAAZNSozg], outside-bar reversals [_AiSzhs67J4] and kicker candles [QWcb7yxYAxk].
- Before shorting, the broad index and the sector index should both be in downtrends [G2gjQUJRfJM].
- Short-side detail is thin.

**How taught.** Many old videos (6–8 years ago) and recurrence in recent ones. Clarity is medium. "How many peers, within what window" is never fixed beyond "3–5".

### 2.8 Top-down alignment

- Market trend, then sector trend, then stock trend; trade only when all three agree [jlpd-YpqwiQ, G2gjQUJRfJM, arFNgAPisHU, sbHzNd2iA8k].
- An older live workflow screened sector indices by price above the 200 DMA, beta above a threshold, and stochastic in buy mode. Banking alone passed [2Yv0E57_bs4]. The beta threshold is not given.
- A watchlist spread across 6–7 or more sectors so no single sector dominates [CXJZMbE_d5o].

### 2.9 Banks as the lead sector; cyclical versus defensive leadership

- **Bank Nifty as India's Nasdaq.** Broad strength is hard to sustain if it falls. Bank Nifty above its 200 DMA pulls large caps into the leading quadrant and reduces FII selling [c-0bHPew1fg]. Its breakdown in January preceded the correction [c-0bHPew1fg]. Weekly gaps down are "never a good sign" for the broad index [Sb6KTt64YKE]. If banks fall, Nifty and Nifty 500 cannot hold [d3-OMTGOGx8, YFsvQXZZt1g].
- **Specific trigger.** A weekly Bank Nifty gain of about 3.5–4% is followed by 4–5 weeks of strong market momentum [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- **Rotation from banks.** Once banks strengthen, realty, autos, auto components, housing and consumption follow, then infrastructure [S--7ta4FmbY, lRUDp336l7Q]. When banks, metals and autos are all in good structure, broad indices rarely crack, though small, micro and theme stocks still can [Oq4FjWEDbd0]. Banks and autos doing well hints the economy is fine [mim7ylyaN74].
- **Contradiction.** One month ago he said Bank Nifty used to set the tone before about 2020, but a few lagging heavyweight private banks have removed that effect. He wants index weights rebalanced, and claims a 13–14% quarterly move would restore sentiment [CFJNeCPMQ1o]. That sits against the other recent videos that still treat it as the momentum source.
- **Rate-cycle sequence, also inconsistent.**
  - After cuts: BFSI, capital markets and IPO names first, then realty and autos, then metals [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
  - Elsewhere: fiscal and monetary expansion benefits realty, autos and consumption first, and "ultimately flows" to financials [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
  - Bank payoff lags policy announcements [L-PGsMh2vHw].
- **Cyclical versus defensive.**
  - A healthy rally should be led by cyclicals and financials (metals, autos, banks, capital markets, defence), not by IT, pharma or consumer durables [-qQntPmfe-s]. High-beta sectors set the tone [6K1LOJqZYEg].
  - Strength confined to pharma, healthcare and IT marks a "defensive undertone" [M4Z-Gi4gGag].
  - Yet in some phases he bets on defensives on purpose: the 2011–13 analogue [s1ZCPQI5894], after high beta has run for about 3 weeks [CXJZMbE_d5o, -SC5vcQ1NuE], and under currency weakness [JcxWhCO0hqI].

### 2.10 Size and index-segment rotation

- In corrections capital rotates from small, mid and micro caps into large caps. The prior momentum leaders fell about 18–27% [Y4TewQHVhvc].
- Avoid small, mid and micro caps when money is scarce; focus on mid-to-large caps with strong balance sheets [hwOw2HaLUcQ]. Micro and small caps lag when global liquidity is tight [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- Foreign money enters large caps first, so track Nifty 100, Nifty 200 and an FPI-holdings index of the top 150 stocks for signs of its return [6GqBBVt6fUs].
- Nifty 50 heavyweights (Reliance, HDFC Bank, ICICI, Kotak, Infosys, TCS) grow at roughly single digits. Nifty Next 50 and Next 100 have better structure [SuYHqw5zA8U, CFJNeCPMQ1o].
- **Direction changed over time.**
  - About a year ago: large caps held better, small caps to be avoided [Yxo-rYOCuAo, rocXNq3_1Zw, V41BUpPK3E4].
  - Most recent videos: a narrow bull in micro caps, new-age IPOs and data-centre or AI-linked names while benchmarks stay weak [rhiXs5Kh_0g, sIo5XQqZ0b0, CFJNeCPMQ1o, kwbfXjRtEkk].
  - These are regime reads rather than contradictions, but no rule tells you when the switch happens. The one stated trigger is Nifty 500 reclaiming and holding its 50 DMA, which would let mid and small caps surprise [d3-OMTGOGx8].

### 2.11 Policy-backed and secular themes

- Prefer pockets where the government spends: PLI, defence modernisation, green energy and EVs, logistics and digital infrastructure. He thinks in 3–7 year theme horizons and lists decade themes (infra and capital goods 2000–10, consumption 2010–15, defence, railways and IT after 2020) [Jpu536_AmGc].
- Defence sweet spot needs three legs together: government support, private capex, export orientation (a Peter Lynch idea) [p5O_uspLu9k].
  - Data he cites: India's FY25 defence budget about 6.2 lakh crore growing 6–8% a year; global spend about $2.5 trillion by 2030; a conflict cycle running to about 2040–45.
  - He gives a 10–15 year bullish horizon [yYwiSDVwgyA].
- Financialisation as a secular, decade-plus theme [azzUmy2l_0U]. Defence and capital markets as India's "own AI" [6GqBBVt6fUs, KUlNy1_Rk9M, yEKNcJ4EH0U]. Indirect AI plays such as data-centre suppliers [NtpyZH5cfaE]. Private-equity entry as a structural signal (low clarity) [rbSj4pOg3_I].
- These are beliefs, not trade signals. He repeatedly says to wait for proper entries [6GqBBVt6fUs].

### 2.12 The US as "mother market"

- **Lead indicators.** S&P 500, Dow and DAX lead India; a correction starting there means mid and small caps fall again [e34G88-BKI4]. When the S&P 500 falls with momentum, global markets follow "9 out of 10 times", so the priority becomes protecting capital [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- **Weekly check.** S&P 500, Nasdaq and Nikkei against their 50 DMA [B8HZvUkY5sA]. An S&P reclaim of its 50 DMA implies a further Indian leg up [q26J58h5Qt8].
- **Speed matters.** A gradual 10–15% US fall can be absorbed elsewhere. A fast 10–20% fall over a couple of months spreads everywhere within about a fortnight [hwOw2HaLUcQ]. Another version: about 20% over 24–36 months is absorbable (as in 2000–03), a 2008-style fall is not [LWoEC9OjajE].
- **No decoupling** [LWoEC9OjajE, r11n7ztY2YY, Qu49zHwEGwo, lRUDp336l7Q, 6qExRlD11sE].
- **Leader divergence.** S&P 500 at new highs without the Nasdaq is an early warning for AI trades [8QeR8FmYYg4]. Narrow, mega-cap-driven US gains are fragile [butE8gDPXxw, r11n7ztY2YY, LWoEC9OjajE].
- **US and China grid for India** [hwOw2HaLUcQ]:
  - Both strong: excellent.
  - US strong, China weak: good.
  - Both weak: terrible.
- **Contradictions.**
  - India would fall only 4–5% if the US fell 10% ("educated guess") [butE8gDPXxw].
  - A US correction could even help India by redirecting flows [iWa7MbGawvU, butE8gDPXxw].
  - India takes limited cues from the US [YFsvQXZZt1g].
  - A 10% Dow fall would mean 10–30% more downside in Indian small and micro caps [x2RbjJp1eCI].
  - These coexist with the "no decoupling" teaching.

### 2.13 India's relative cycle against the world, EM and China

- **Nifty 500 / S&P 500.** Alternates between underperformance and outperformance legs of about 4–12 months, visible back to about 1995–2000. His experience: underperformance legs last about 6–7 months, outperformance about 8–12 [YVGWKmpHStM].
  - He says timing cannot be predicted.
  - During an underperformance leg, good policy news is absorbed within a day and selling resumes [YVGWKmpHStM].
- **China.** Outperformance phases over India last about 12–18 months [iWa7MbGawvU]. Six-month return: China about +28%, Nifty about −6%. Bloomberg survey he cites: about 85% of funds raised China weights, about 65% cut India [iWa7MbGawvU]. Later he says China is no longer cheap and the catch-up has played out [YVGWKmpHStM].
- **EM and the world.** MSCI World and EM outperformed Nifty 500 from the September 2024 top [Oq4FjWEDbd0, rhiXs5Kh_0g]. India's EM rank is cyclical:
  - About 85–90% of EM peers beat India in 2025–26, as in 2011–13.
  - India's EM weight fell from about 20–24% to 11–12% [SNrzU-z3RkE]; about 8% is quoted in a later video [8QeR8FmYYg4].
  - Country allocation rotates by theme: Brazil 4%→16%→3%, China 17%→43%→19%, India up to about 24% [dpsU7JvHiMA].
- **Is under-ownership protective? Tension.**
  - Yes: under-owned markets are safer when crowded trades unwind [SNrzU-z3RkE].
  - No: "weak gets weaker", so capital leaving EM hits the weakest relative-strength market first [8QeR8FmYYg4].
  - Partly: India would be hit by a dollar rally but less [8QeR8FmYYg4].
- **Long run.** India beats China over 5–15 years because of lower debt and higher ROE and margins [YVGWKmpHStM]. Indian mid and small caps in USD terms are beaten only by Nasdaq over 5–20 years [0Hnffbj5pgE]. He tells viewers to verify this.

### 2.14 Dollar index and rupee

- **Dollar index (DXY).** A rising DXY hurts EM and India; a falling DXY with stable US 10-year yields helps [bhTEw6wD_CU, zHzq3uAFJFs, M4Z-Gi4gGag, sIo5XQqZ0b0, rhiXs5Kh_0g, 8QeR8FmYYg4]. DXY and yields rising together usually hurts stocks, though he says this is a tendency, not a rule (citing Murphy) [bhTEw6wD_CU]. Levels are episode-specific and drift:

  | Video | Level or range | His reading |
  |---|---|---|
  | V41BUpPK3E4 | 99–105/106 range | Supportive for EM |
  | zHzq3uAFJFs (early 2025) | Below about 110 for 2–3 weeks | Relief rally odds rise |
  | zHzq3uAFJFs | 120–121 | New Indian low |
  | zHzq3uAFJFs | Below 105–103 | New EM bull cycle |
  | zHzq3uAFJFs | EM index 52–53 support, 54–56 breakout | Breakout only persists with dollar below support |
  | JcxWhCO0hqI | 100–107, at top of range | A pullback gives only a short EM rally |
  | M4Z-Gi4gGag | 100 to 110 (about 10%) | Pain for EM; prior 90→114 move was the painful phase |
  | hwOw2HaLUcQ | 95–105 | Expected range for EM |
  | sIo5XQqZ0b0, rhiXs5Kh_0g (recent) | 101.9–102 range top | A break above means India struggles most |

- **Rupee.**
  - USDINR breaking above 72.5–73 on the weekly chart is an equity warning [e34G88-BKI4].
  - A move from 83–84 to 87–88 costs foreign investors about 4.5% before any market move. FIIs may return near 88.5–90.5 (also quoted 89–91) once it flattens [zHzq3uAFJFs].
  - Nifty's 2025 return of 8.9% in rupees was about 4.3% in USD [SNrzU-z3RkE].
  - He calls rupee weakness the single biggest damage to India's sentiment; it went from about 74 to about 95 and he guesses the cycle is near its end [dpsU7JvHiMA].
  - **Tension:** gradual depreciation is normal and only gets noisy at 90–92 [SbtFCxbIB1w], versus "biggest damage" [dpsU7JvHiMA].

### 2.17 FII and DII flows

- **Don't obsess over daily FII data.** FIIs hold about $800 billion of Indian equity, and net selling of about $25–35 billion since September 2024 is a small share of that [YVGWKmpHStM].
- **Sensitivity has fallen.**
  - 2010–14: outflows of 3,000–4,000 crore a month could cut the index 8–12%; now similar selling causes 2–3% [YVGWKmpHStM].
  - Old era: about $1 billion of outflow could knock Nifty 5–11% [dpsU7JvHiMA].
  - Past episodes (2011–13, 2015–16) brought 10–15% index declines [sIo5XQqZ0b0].
  - Domestic SIP and mutual-fund money absorbs the selling: about 19–21.5 thousand crore a month [dpsU7JvHiMA, B8HZvUkY5sA], and about 33,000 crore of MF inflow in one August [r63sarc_qV8].
  - Look where DIIs buy [YVGWKmpHStM].
- **Contradiction.** India still needs FII money for momentum, and domestic flows alone are not enough [x2RbjJp1eCI, KUlNy1_Rk9M, YFsvQXZZt1g]. Separately, the "SIPs give FIIs exit" idea is a misunderstanding, since FIIs can only sell because DIIs buy [SNrzU-z3RkE, dpsU7JvHiMA].
- **Why FIIs sell.**
  - Indian large caps grow at high single digits versus S&P 500 expected growth of 18–22%.
  - India has no AI play.
  - The rupee [0nbQs92kYuY, sIo5XQqZ0b0, dpsU7JvHiMA].
  - Taxes [KUlNy1_Rk9M, NtpyZH5cfaE].
  - India gets only about 2–4% of global allocation [LWoEC9OjajE].
- **Where FIIs are active.** In IPOs and select mid caps. Check quarterly FII shareholding changes on screener.in [r63sarc_qV8, kwbfXjRtEkk].
- **What he wants to see.** More days of net FII buying (one day of about 1,300 crore cited) before expecting momentum in high-beta sectors [lRUDp336l7Q]. FII buying turning up marks a sentiment shift [ywccnKeWXNo].
- **Low-clarity extras.** India's beta to EM fell to 0.56 [0nbQs92kYuY]. India's valuation relative to EM is at a multi-year low, so FIIs may return, lifting large caps and private banks first [6qExRlD11sE].

## 3. How a program would test it

1. **Sector RS rotation.**
   - Each week (and every 15 days as a variant), rank NSE sector indices by the trend of sector/Nifty 500.
   - Score a sector "in" if its RS line is in a higher-high/higher-low structure (fixed swing-pivot definition) and price is above a rising 50 DMA.
   - Hold the top 1–3 (variant: 5–6) sectors, or their constituents above their own rising 50 DMA with rising RS.
   - Compare 1, 3 and 6-month returns against equal-weight Nifty 500 and against stocks from sectors with lower-high/lower-low RS.
   - Separate test: 6–7 month RS selection versus 2–3 week RS selection.
   - *Data:* daily NSE sector and thematic index history, constituent lists with point-in-time membership, Nifty 500.
   - *Sources:* BxY2ofigO8I, JJEB-UZgK4E, LWoEC9OjajE, Oq4FjWEDbd0, yEKNcJ4EH0U.

2. **Sector breadth.**
   - Monthly percentage of each sector's members above the 200 DMA.
   - Test whether sectors crossing about 75% after a trough below about 25% beat the index over 3–6 months.
   - Test whether top-decile breadth groups beat bottom-decile groups.
   - *Data:* point-in-time constituents, daily prices, an industry taxonomy.
   - *Sources:* WI0tdUjH_Vg, c-0bHPew1fg, 0nbQs92kYuY.

3. **Fell-least versus fell-most after a low.** These are competing hypotheses.
   - For each index drawdown of at least 10% (Nifty 500), rank sectors by peak-to-trough drawdown.
   - Measure relative returns over days 1–40 after the trough (his V-bounce claim favours the deepest fallers) and over 3–12 months (fell-least leaders).
   - Add his day 4–15 rebound-leadership confirmation and the condition of a low-volume pullback in days 10–15.
   - *Data:* sector indices, summed constituent volume.
   - *Sources:* dERi8MUU5es, Pm_1kb8Fku0, B1ynZNvqesE, -qQntPmfe-s.

4. **Sister-stock confirmation.**
   - For each breakout from a defined base (or Stage 2 entry above a rising 30-week MA), count same-industry peers breaking out within ±3 weeks.
   - Compare forward 1/3/6-month returns and hit rates for 0, 1–2 and 3 or more confirming peers.
   - Mirror the test on the short side with sector and index downtrend filters.
   - *Data:* industry classification, daily OHLCV for the full universe.
   - *Sources:* Bqp9jKQCs30, jlpd-YpqwiQ, 2Yv0E57_bs4, G2gjQUJRfJM.

5. **Bank Nifty as lead.**
   - Event study A: weeks with a Bank Nifty gain of at least 3.5%; Nifty, midcap and smallcap returns over the next 4–5 weeks against the unconditional rate.
   - Event study B: Nifty 500 forward 1–3 month returns and breadth when Bank Nifty is above versus below its 200 DMA.
   - Test whether Bank Nifty base breakouts precede realty, auto and housing outperformance over 4–12 weeks.
   - Split the sample before and after 2020 to check his claim that the effect has weakened.
   - *Sources:* 6K1LOJqZYEg, c-0bHPew1fg, S--7ta4FmbY, CFJNeCPMQ1o.

6. **Dollar and yields regime.**
   - Classify weeks by DXY trend (above or below its 50 DMA or 200 DMA, multi-month range breakouts) and US 10-year trend.
   - Compare 1–3 month forward returns of Nifty, the Nifty midcap/smallcap/Nifty 50 ratio, and EM equities across regimes, especially "DXY and yields both rising".
   - *Data:* DXY, US 10-year and 30-year yields, Nifty indices, an MSCI EM proxy.
   - *Sources:* bhTEw6wD_CU, zHzq3uAFJFs, M4Z-Gi4gGag, hwOw2HaLUcQ.

7. **Crude thresholds.**
   - Nifty and Bank Nifty forward 1–3 month returns when Brent closes above versus below 100, above versus below its 50 DMA, and after a breakout from a multi-year weekly base.
   - Use a rolling beta of Nifty to crude to check his claim that sensitivity is falling.
   - Check USDINR response above 100.
   - *Sources:* Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA, r63sarc_qV8, YFsvQXZZt1g.

8. **Flows and the US link.**
   - (a) Regress monthly Nifty returns on FII net flows scaled by FII holdings, for 2006–14 and for post-2016, to test the claim that sensitivity has declined.
   - (b) For each S&P 500 drawdown of 10–20%, compare Nifty drawdown and lag when the fall took 2 months or less versus 6 months or more; test his "about 2 weeks" contagion lag.
   - (c) Measure the duration distribution of relative legs in Nifty 500/S&P 500 against his 6–7 month underperformance and 8–12 month outperformance figures.
   - *Data:* NSDL/NSE FII and DII daily flows, FII holding value, S&P 500 history.
   - *Sources:* YVGWKmpHStM, dpsU7JvHiMA, hwOw2HaLUcQ.

## 4. Transfer to Canadian (TSX) and US markets

**Works largely as is (method, not names):**
- Sector RS lines against a broad benchmark (S&P 500 or S&P/TSX Composite) with structure and 50 DMA filters.
- Sector breadth (percentage above the 200 DMA), quadrant classification, and top-down alignment.
- Sister-stock confirmation.
- Fell-least leadership after corrections.
- Smaller names within leading sectors, and custom equal-weight sub-industry baskets.
- Crowding and extension filters, including distance from the 50 DMA.
- Risk-on/risk-off competition.
- Central-bank stance as a regime filter.
- Leader/average divergence such as Nasdaq versus S&P.

For the US use GICS sectors and industry groups with ETFs (XL*, equal-weight RSP-type). The TSX has fewer and more concentrated sectors (financials, energy, materials dominate), so RS ranking will rest on few members and sub-industry baskets matter more.

**Needs adapting:**
- **Bank Nifty as lead.** Test the TSX financials index or US financials/KBW banks. In the US the Nasdaq or semiconductors may be the true analogue, as he himself says [c-0bHPew1fg].
- **Crude.** The sign likely flips for Canada as an exporter, with energy-heavy TSX and CAD. In the US the effect is mixed.
- **Dollar.** For Canada use USDCAD. For the US, DXY works through multinationals and foreign earnings rather than an EM flow channel.
- **Flows.** FII/DII data has no direct equivalent. Use ETF and mutual fund flows, 13F holdings changes, or Canadian foreign-purchase data.
- **Market-cap bands.** Crore bands must become percentile buckets.
- **Costs and instruments.** Indian STT and charges (he cites about 55,000–65,000 rupees per side on 5 crore [dpsU7JvHiMA]) differ from North American costs. Sector ETFs are far more liquid in North America than the thin Indian ETF universe [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
- **US contagion test.** For Canada, test TSX against the S&P 500 with the speed-of-fall rule.

**India-only:**
- Specific sector calls and dated leader lists.
- PSU/CPSE groupings, government-scheme themes (PLI, Atmanirbhar defence), RBI and budget events.
- Rupee levels, the SIP absorption thesis, MSCI EM weight cycles for India, the India–China rotation, and the screener.in workflow.
- Defence and financialisation theses.

## 5. Gaps and doubts

- **Undefined mechanics.** He never defines a swing-pivot rule for "higher high on the RS line", the RS lookback beyond "1–2 years", the beta threshold in the old screen [2Yv0E57_bs4], his RRG settings [c-0bHPew1fg], or how many confirming sister stocks within what window (only "about 3–5" [Bqp9jKQCs30]). Detail is said to sit in paid material [sbHzNd2iA8k].
- **Shifting levels.** DXY, crude and rupee levels are episode-specific and move between videos: DXY 95–105, 99–106, 100–107, 103–110, 101.9; crude 97–100 or 116 as the trigger. Treat them as illustrations, not fixed thresholds.
- **Contradictions to resolve in testing:**
  - V-bounce of the most beaten sectors [-qQntPmfe-s] versus fell-least leadership [dERi8MUU5es].
  - Strength-persists versus buy-out-of-favour.
  - "Risk-reward" sectors below the 50 DMA versus his rule not to trade sectors below the 50 DMA [B8HZvUkY5sA].

  - Domestic money is enough versus FII money is needed.

- **Evolution.** The benchmark moved from Nifty 50 to Nifty 500. Exchange indices gave way to custom baskets. Focus widened from 1–3 sectors to 5–8. The size preference swung from large caps (about a year ago) to micro caps and new-age names (recent). He gives no rule for the size switch.
- **Unverified figures.** Several numbers are his recollection or third-party: the Bloomberg fund survey, about 80% of US gains from AI, yen carry size, Fed odds, a SEBI "CAS" item garbled in captions.
