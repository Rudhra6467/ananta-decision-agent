# Exits and selling

## 1. What he teaches and how it fits

Raunak A's exit teaching rests on one idea. Losers are frequent, so the account grows only if losers are cut quickly and winners are allowed to run. He links patience, trend following and loss cutting as one formula [eeKRfAbbvZA, g3mcqf6rqoc].

In his more recent positional teaching (roughly the last 1–3 years), he prefers trailing over targets:
- He trails with structure (trending pivots, lower-high/lower-low breaks) or with moving averages (the 50 DMA and its "pivot", the 200 DMA, the 52-week weekly average).
- He scales out partially into large gains.
- He runs portfolio-level sell rules tied to the index's 50 DMA and 200 DMA [Lw6h6T30VvE, LWoEC9OjajE, mR0HMssJek8, 8QeR8FmYYg4].

His older pattern videos (about 6–8 years ago) do give fixed targets: measured moves, 8–20% swing targets and R-multiples [K06GqbJwEv4, VqoLpEvre04, R6QjIoYHoFE, QWcb7yxYAxk]. He also taught an Ichimoku and Heikin-Ashi exit toolkit in that period [xzR6bwNgb18, 59vJPo6oiNA, uqEFppHC4Jw].

The exits sit on top of his Stage 2 / relative-strength / base-breakout entry method. The initial stop below the base is covered in another chapter. This chapter covers what happens after a trade is working, or is drifting.

Everything below is his claim or method. None of it is established fact; each item is a hypothesis to test.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Trail, don't target (later teaching), versus targets (earlier teaching)

**What it is.** After entry he says to trail rather than set a price target. He dismisses targets in blunt terms, calling them "for fools", and says professionals follow price [Lw6h6T30VvE].

**Rules and conditions.**
- Hold until the structure deteriorates, not until a target is reached or noise appears [mim7ylyaN74].
- Do not exit early on a sharp counter-move. Exit only when the predefined trailing stop is actually triggered, and aim to ride the whole trend [MXXsv0RSek8].
- Do not exit because the market is "at a high". Use trailing and protective stops and let the market signal the end of the trend [kkJ4m9JQ4pM].
- The holding horizon he aims for is 6–12 months, long or short [6ZKLrl0z8jY].
- His timeframe for pivot trailing is daily or weekly. He calls anything below roughly 75-minute or hourly charts a waste of time for this approach [Lw6h6T30VvE].

**Why exits are hard.** He says exits are harder than entries. Tops rarely form an orderly Stage 3 range; price often collapses vertically as volatility rises [Bqp9jKQCs30].

**Contradiction over time.** Videos 6–8 years old teach explicit targets (see 2.2). The "no targets" stance appears in a video about 2 years old. Even his early Kumo video calls exits "subjective" and promises to cover them later [2crnCCz4Y3s]. The research program should treat the target rules and the trailing rules as separate systems from different eras.

**Clarity.** The philosophy is taught often and clearly. The precise trailing mechanics vary from video to video.

### 2.2 Target rules (mostly earlier videos)

**Measured move from a range** [K06GqbJwEv4]
- Expect at least 2× the range height as reward, and often 3–4×.
- With no volume expansion at the breakout, the base case is about 1:1 to 1:2.
- With volume and candle range expanding after the breakout, and a rise angle above 60°, he expects 1:3 to 1:4.
- Longer ranges (2–3 months) and a prior momentum-driven decline are said to improve outcomes.
- The angle is chart-scale dependent; a slope measure would be needed to test it.

**A-to-B measured move** [VqoLpEvre04]
- Target = breakout level + the A–B distance. Example: 5550 + 350 ≈ 5900.
- In strong trends he says the move can reach 3–4× A–B.
- Alternative: a fixed 10–15% target if the market is momentum-driven.

**TD supply line target** [Vz5lqc_RO7M]
- Measure the vertical distance from the true low of the supply region to the TD supply line.
- Add that distance to the point where the breakout candle meets the line. Example: 320 pts + 10,220 = 10,540, reached in 7–8 days.
- Aggressive variant: add the distance to the breakout candle's close or high instead.

**Fixed-percentage swing targets**

| Setup / context | Target | Video |
|---|---|---|
| Divergence signals (treated as swing trades) | +15–20% | R6QjIoYHoFE |
| Swing entries | 8–10% conservative, 15–18% aggressive; scale out bit by bit past 10–12% | 2Yv0E57_bs4 |
| Kicker pattern | 8–10% conservative, often 15%+; reward:risk usually 2:1–4:1, rarely 5:1+ | QWcb7yxYAxk |
| Weekly outside bar | Exit "left to judgment"; typical 20–30% in 2–4 weeks (about 3:1–5:1) | _AiSzhs67J4 |
| Kicker (as compared in the same video) | 5–15% over a few days | _AiSzhs67J4 |

**IBD rules he quotes but does not follow.** He cites the IBD rules: take profit at about +10% from the buy point, and sell automatically at −7%. He says he does not follow the 7% rule himself [mR0HMssJek8].

### 2.3 Structure and pivot trailing (long side, core of current method)

**Basic rule.** Mark major and minor trending pivots. Hold while the latest major pivot low is intact, and do nothing during corrective moves above it. A break of the major pivot is the exit signal (example: exit near 240) [mR0HMssJek8].

**Closing basis, set in advance.**
- Example, Anant Raj: bought at 120–125, trading near 155. Sell all on a daily close below the nearby pivot at about 132–133 [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- Members' practice he describes: trail until recent pivot lows break [T4849hhdwTo].

**Staged structural exit** [mR0HMssJek8]
- Sell about 50% at the first lower-high/lower-low.
- Exit fully at the second lower-high/lower-low.
- He loses interest in a stock whose later candles after a decline are mostly narrow-range.

**Valuation does not override structure.** Exit a growth leader once its daily chart forms lower-highs/lower-lows, even with a high PE and strong fundamentals [KRoie4cj5TQ].

**Volume-profile variant** [p03ho_Rhc_E]
- If price goes sideways after the breakout and a new high-volume node forms, put the stop below the most recent pivot.
- Either the range resolves up and the trade continues, or the stop is hit in profit.

**What not to do**
- Do not react to weak candles that leave the stop untouched. His own past mistake was exiting a range breakout on a small dip below entry and then watching the stock make new highs. For range breakouts the stop belongs below the range low [U7YrYjIS9B8].
- Hold through strong wide bearish candles while the higher-high/higher-low structure and the pivot are intact [IHqkSPVCxko].
- Example of holding through a flat period: Tata Consumer, bought around 650 and flat after 10 days, is held inside the demand zone, with an aggressive cut planned below about 640–645 [eeKRfAbbvZA].

**Short side (thin).**
- Exit shorts on market structure, i.e. the end of the lower-high/lower-low sequence [2crnCCz4Y3s].
- Reduce or exit when the first higher-high and higher-low in a downtrend is followed by sideways action [IHqkSPVCxko].
- No short-specific trailing parameters are given.

**Clarity.** Taught repeatedly and clearly at the rule level. "Major" versus "minor" pivot is never defined in the notes.

### 2.4 Moving-average trailing

**50 DMA scale-out ladder (O'Neil-style)** [LWoEC9OjajE]
1. After a 30–50% rally, sell 25–30% of the position.
2. Trail the rest with the 50 DMA.
3. On a break of the 50 DMA, sell another 33–40%.
4. If the stock then recovers, the low of the candle that broke the 50 DMA becomes the "50 DMA pivot".
5. Exit fully if that pivot breaks.
6. Repeat on a fresh Stage 2 setup with improving relative strength.

Context and limits:
- He frames this as nimble positional management using the 50 DMA, the pivot and the 200 DMA. It is not buy-today-sell-tomorrow trading.
- He recommends it for roughly the next 12–18 months in a narrow market.
- He says it suits traders who tend to give back large profits.

**52-week average on the weekly chart (8 years ago)** [Bqp9jKQCs30]
- On a weekly close below the 52-week MA, sell 50% and mark the relevant weekly pivot low.
- On a break of that pivot, sell the remaining 50%.
- For fundamentally strong companies reporting good results, do not sell all at the first MA break. Treat the full exit as confirmed only by the pivot break or a proper Stage 3 breakdown, which he calls rare.
- **Re-entry:** if the stock instead builds a new base and breaks out, buy back the sold 50% and resume trailing (he walks through a 2010–2012 example).

The weekly 52-week MA (older) and the daily 50 DMA ladder (recent) are different speeds. Read this as an evolution toward faster exits in a narrow market, not as one rule.

**Ichimoku exits (6–7 years ago)**
- Two-stage exit [xzR6bwNgb18]:
  1. First exit when Chikou moves below price.
  2. Second exit when price is clearly below both Tenkan and Kijun.
  - Mirror logic for shorts is implied but not spelled out.
- Kijun as stop: closing basis only. An intraday breach that closes back above does not count [153pJZk-ixo].
- Kijun versus Senkou B as trail [59vJPo6oiNA]:
  - Kijun exits earlier; in his ICICI example it gave about 475 versus about 470–471 for Senkou B, roughly 1% better.
  - Senkou B exits later.
- Cloud re-entry against the trade as an exit, for long or short [2crnCCz4Y3s].

### 2.5 Partial profits into strength

Sizes and triggers vary a lot across videos.

| Trigger | Amount sold | Video |
|---|---|---|
| Swing high or consolidation in a leading group; hold rest until consolidation or LH/LL | 20–30% | eeKRfAbbvZA |
| After a 30–50% run, especially if seeing 40% fall back to cost would upset you | 25–40% | rocXNq3_1Zw |
| After a 30–50% run (part of the 50 DMA ladder) | 25–30% | LWoEC9OjajE |
| +15–20% above the breakout level (volume profile) | 50% | p03ho_Rhc_E |
| Each strong wide-range candle in the trade's direction, instead of predicting the top | 5–15% per candle | 59vJPo6oiNA |
| Fixed reward:risk levels: +100 (1:2) and +150 (1:3) on a 50-rupee stop | Not specified | 1klATqC7oks |
| Monthly-timeframe resistance (swing/positional) | Scale out or exit | dV4MiNaAenI |
| Wide-range candles after an opening-range entry (day trading; low clarity) | Part of position | hk4d8AxbQ3A |

Other details:
- In [rocXNq3_1Zw] he adds that someone deliberately holding a few multi-year winners can do nothing, but should know which type of participant they are.
- In [59vJPo6oiNA] he notes many swing traders take everything around 1.5R. He judges his own exits by his R history (whether he tends to exit at 2R, 3R or 4R).

**Using the strategy's own R distribution** [Wk4NVr226t8]
- If an open trade reaches a multiple well above the strategy's average winner (example: 3R versus an average of 1.5–2.2R), book at least part, even while price is still rising.

**Tension.** [DCG1GO9TGFU] says do not touch winners during dips. The partial-profit rules trim winners on strength. He partly reconciles the two by trader type [rocXNq3_1Zw], and by timing: trims happen on strength, not on dips.

### 2.6 Warning signs and momentum fade (discretionary)

**Fading momentum before the breakdown** [eeKRfAbbvZA]
- Signs: repeated long wicks, range expansion, wide bearish candles, falling volume. He cuts before the full breakdown.
- Example, HUL: entry 2420–2430, cut below about 2320–2330.

**Heikin-Ashi**
- Ignore isolated indecision candles (small body, both shadows) within a strong trend [uqEFppHC4Jw].
- A long upper shadow after a prolonged advance calls for caution. Confirm over the next 2–3 sessions, e.g. strong red candles, before acting [uqEFppHC4Jw].
- In an expansion-pattern uptrend, do not sell on a pause; sell only on a clear bearish HA candle [L99LgSdCDD8].
- Act only on wide-range reversal candles with confirmation, or on a structure break [IHqkSPVCxko].

**Maturing trend** [tTdy5srpW9Q]
- Retracements deepening below 38.2% and toward 50% mean it is time to consider booking full or partial profits.
- So does a cluster of strong bearish candles with rising volatility.
- Re-enter if strong-trend traits return.

**Other signs**
- Repeated RSI lower lows followed by a price lower low (failed divergence) signals weakness [R6QjIoYHoFE].
- Price "excess", such as an island-reversal-like structure, signals trend termination. In bear markets it often ends counter-trend rallies. Focus on the concept, not the textbook pattern [Nkp_wwGZFNQ].

**Expected noise inside a trend.** Strong trends carry 5–8% drawdowns over many months; whether to act on them is up to the trader [lRUDp336l7Q]. Leaders consolidating can give back 5–8% easily [Qu49zHwEGwo].

### 2.7 Managing losers beyond the initial stop

- **Cap losses at the strategy's average loss.** Do not let a loser exceed its historical average. Example: average loss −0.6 to −0.9R; at −1R, cut size or exit at once [Wk4NVr226t8].
- **Trade off plan.** If a trade is not working to plan, exit at least half, then review [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- **Premature entry.** If an early entry fails, exit fast (Kotak Bank flag, exited the next day) [mim7ylyaN74].
- **No revenge trades.** After a loss on a counter, he generally does not trade it again, even on a similar new setup [VET4JHrc2vU].
- **Stops protect against catastrophe.** His worst loss of the year came from entering a realty stock early in a failed base; it fell 12–15% [rxqOUxO_vP4].
- **Fixed percentage stop.** With equal-value sizing he uses a fixed stop of about 15% [aUgaUeCmSOQ]. The caption is garbled ("falls above 15%"), so the exact figure is uncertain.

### 2.8 Portfolio and regime sell rules (recent, 3 weeks to 2 years ago)

**Order of selling on an index 50 DMA break** [8QeR8FmYYg4]
1. Trigger: the index (he uses the Nifty Smallcap 250) breaks its 50 DMA pivot, and the break is confirmed. This marks the end of the up-leg.
2. Sell losers first.
3. Then, depending on how comfortable you are with chart structure, exit or book partial profits.
- He cites O'Neil for this sequence.

**Pullbacks** [KDSbZ0oW2U0, DCG1GO9TGFU]
- Cut losers, keep winners, and add to winners.
- If a winner turns into a loser, cut it fully.
- Repeat with each pullback.
- He says most people do the reverse.

**At a correction start** [elpQ8wyLZLQ]
- Cut everything except the leaders of the prior up-leg.
- The portfolio itself is the best indicator; no chart indicator can time these turns.

**BCG framing** [Qu49zHwEGwo]
- Hold and add to "stars" (top risers that are only consolidating).
- Cut "dogs" (negative on the holding) if the market moves below its 50 DMA.
- Slow "cash cows" can be cut too.
- Good sector and RS selection means little churn is needed.

**Index 50 DMA as a trailing reference** [bhTEw6wD_CU]
- In the worst case, exit if the index pulls back to its 50 DMA.
- In the best case, the cushion lets holders ride a dip to or slightly below it.
- Low clarity.

**200 DMA rule, proposed for the next cycle** [hpbDmEK-FvM]
- When the index is sustained below its 200 DMA, sell weak holdings that are decisively below their own 200 DMA, especially if that average is turning down.
- Move into higher-quality names or cash.
- He concedes selling during the fall itself may not have been prudent. This is a rule he says he wants, not a rule with a track record.

**Extended market** [r11n7ztY2YY]
- When the market feels too easy, tighten stops, avoid leverage and preserve profits.
- Do not exit everything. In July 2024 he noted Nifty at 24,000 could still reach 26,000.

**Late cycle** [hKnYYRpd63s]
- Options listed: fixed deposits, ETFs, selective shorts or cash. He says he does not know which is best.

**Systematic plans** [Q-uCkMMTh5Q]
- For systematic investment plans, a review zone means pausing contributions or redirecting them, not a binary sell.

### 2.9 Time

There is no explicit time stop anywhere in these notes. Holding-period statements are descriptive:
- 6–12 months positional [6ZKLrl0z8jY].
- 2–4 weeks for the outside-bar pattern [_AiSzhs67J4].
- "Weeks" for 8–15% swings [2Yv0E57_bs4].
- A flat trade after 10 days is held while structure holds [eeKRfAbbvZA], which argues against time stops.

### 2.10 Short side, beyond exits

Short-specific exit material is limited to the structure and cloud exits in 2.3 and 2.4 [2crnCCz4Y3s, IHqkSPVCxko, xzR6bwNgb18].

The related sizing rule [LahSe1vu1kY] concerns entries:
- Take the first two short signals in a down move at full size.
- Cut the third and later signals to at least 50% smaller size.
- His reasons: falls are shorter than rises, and counter-rallies can be large.

## 3. How a program would test it

Data needed for all tests:
- Survivorship-free daily and weekly OHLCV, adjusted for splits and dividends.
- Index series for the regime rules.
- A breakout-entry generator consistent with his base/Stage 2 method, so that every exit rule is compared on identical entries.
- Realistic costs and slippage, including gap fills through stops.

1. **50 DMA ladder versus alternatives.** Applied to breakouts that reach +30% (also test +40% and +50%):
   - Sell 27.5% at the trigger.
   - Sell 36.5% of the original on the first daily close below the 50 DMA.
   - Sell the rest on a close below the low of the 50 DMA-break candle, after any recovery.
   - Compare against a full exit at the 50 DMA, a full exit at the 200 DMA, and buy-and-hold to 12 months.
   - Metrics: CAGR, average R, giveback from peak open profit, max drawdown.
   - Split the sample by market breadth (narrow versus broad), since he claims the ladder suits narrow markets [LWoEC9OjajE].

2. **Weekly 52-week MA half/half with re-entry.**
   - Sell 50% on a weekly close below the 52-week SMA; sell the rest on a break of the most recent weekly swing low (define it, e.g. a 2-bar fractal).
   - Variant A: no re-entry.
   - Variant B: buy back the 50% on a breakout from a new base.
   - Compare with a full exit at the first MA close [Bqp9jKQCs30].
   - Optional fundamental filter (e.g. positive EPS growth) to test his "strong fundamentals: sell only half" condition.

3. **Pivot trailing versus fixed targets on identical breakouts.**
   - Trailing exit: daily close below the most recent swing low (fractal widths 2/3/5 bars as a stand-in for "major" pivots).
   - Target exits: measured-move targets of 1×, 2×, 3× range height and the A–B distance; fixed +10%, +15%, +20%.
   - Also record maximum favourable excursion in multiples of range height, conditioned on breakout volume expansion (e.g. volume above 1.5× the 50-day average) and post-breakout slope (ATR-normalized, replacing the 60° angle) [K06GqbJwEv4, VqoLpEvre04, Lw6h6T30VvE, mR0HMssJek8].

4. **Two-violation structural exit.**
   - Sell 50% at the first confirmed lower-high/lower-low on the daily chart, and the rest at the second.
   - Compare with a full exit at the first violation and at the second [mR0HMssJek8].

5. **Portfolio regime rule: sell losers first.**
   - Simulate a 10–20 stock momentum portfolio.
   - On the first confirmed close of a small-cap index below its 50 DMA (confirmation e.g. 2 closes, or the low of the break candle taken out):
     - (a) sell positions below cost;
     - (b) then trim 25–50% of winners;
     - versus holding everything, versus selling winners first.
   - Include the 200 DMA variant: index sustained below its 200 DMA (e.g. 10 days) and stock more than 5% below a declining 200 DMA [8QeR8FmYYg4, KDSbZ0oW2U0, DCG1GO9TGFU, hpbDmEK-FvM].
   - Metrics: drawdown, recovery time, 6- and 12-month forward return.

6. **Partial profit at a +30–50% gain.**
   - Sell 25–40% at the trigger, the remainder on the base trailing rule.
   - Compare giveback from peak and final return against holding fully [rocXNq3_1Zw, eeKRfAbbvZA].

7. **R-distribution exits.**
   - Using rolling in-sample strategy statistics:
     - partial exit when open profit exceeds about 1.5× the historical average winner R;
     - cut when open loss passes the historical average loss, e.g. −1R.
   - Test out of sample [Wk4NVr226t8].

8. **Heikin-Ashi long upper shadow plus confirmation.**
   - After an extended advance (e.g. more than 20% above the 50 DMA), measure the 5–10 day forward return following a long-upper-shadow HA candle confirmed by 2 strong red HA candles within 3 sessions, versus baseline and versus unconfirmed candles [uqEFppHC4Jw].

## 4. Transfer to TSX and US markets

**Usable as is**
- Pivot and structure trailing.
- 50/200 DMA and 52-week MA rules.
- The partial-profit ladders.
- R-distribution exits.
- Ichimoku and Heikin-Ashi exits.
- Loser-first portfolio rules.

All are price-only and market-agnostic. Rupee figures in examples (Tata Consumer, HUL, Anant Raj, ICICI) are illustrations, not parameters.

**Needs adapting**
- **Regime index.** The Nifty Smallcap 250 maps to the Russell 2000 or S&P 600 in the US. For Canada, the S&P/TSX SmallCap or Completion index; the TSX Composite is heavy in financials and energy, so breadth behaves differently.
- **"Narrow market" condition.** Needs a defined breadth metric for each market.
- **Percentage targets and the 5–8% "normal" drawdown.** These come from Indian mid/small-cap volatility. Rescale by ATR or volatility rank, especially for TSX large caps, which tend to be calmer, and for TSX juniors, which tend to be wilder.
- **75-minute minimum timeframe.** This fits India's 6h15m session (5 bars). For the 6.5h North American session, use 65- or 78-minute bars, or simply daily.
- **Thin TSX names.** Gaps through pivots make closing-basis exits fill worse; costs and slippage need explicit modelling.
- **Taxes.** Canadian taxable accounts, TFSA and RRSP accounts, and US wash-sale rules change the economics of frequent partial exits and buy-backs, such as the 52-week MA re-entry.

**Largely India-specific**
- The fixed-deposit alternative (closest local analog: GICs or T-bills).
- Systematic investment plan pausing [Q-uCkMMTh5Q] (analog: pre-authorized contribution plans).
- Election-day volatility exits [T4849hhdwTo].
- **Positional shorts.** He claims 6–12 month shorts [6ZKLrl0z8jY]. In India, cash-equity shorts are intraday only, so positional shorts mean futures.
  - In the US and Canada, stocks can be shorted directly, but borrow cost and availability must be added.
  - His observation that falls are shorter than rises [LahSe1vu1kY] is untested in any market.

## 5. Gaps and doubts

**Conflicting rules across eras**
- Fixed targets (6–8 years ago) versus "targets are for fools" (2 years ago).
- The 52-week weekly MA (8 years ago) versus the daily 50 DMA ladder (10 months ago).
- It is unclear whether the older rules are abandoned or just setup-specific.

**Partial-exit sizing is inconsistent**
- Sizes range across 5–15%, 20–30%, 25–30%, 25–40% and 50%.
- Triggers range across 2R/3R, +15–20%, +30–50%, strength candles and monthly resistance.
- No single rule is stated as his current default.

**Undefined terms**
- "Major" versus "minor" pivot.
- "Decisive", "confirmed" and "sustained" breaks below averages.
- "Strong" or "wide-range" candles.
- "Prolonged advance".
- Measuring "rising volatility".
- The 60° angle is chart-scaling dependent.

**Thin or low-clarity notes**
- The index 50 DMA exit [bhTEw6wD_CU] and the opening-range scale-out [hk4d8AxbQ3A].
- The 2Yv0E57_bs4 parameters do not fully match the text (8–15% versus the 8–10% / 15–18% fields).
- Caption error on the 15% stop [aUgaUeCmSOQ].
- The 200 DMA portfolio rule is aspirational, with his own doubt about selling into a fall [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- Late-cycle options are explicitly undecided [hKnYYRpd63s].

**Contradictions to resolve in testing**
- "Don't touch winners on dips" [DCG1GO9TGFU] versus trimming 25–40% after big gains [rocXNq3_1Zw, LWoEC9OjajE].
- Cutting before the full breakdown on wick and volume signs [eeKRfAbbvZA] versus not reacting to weak candles that leave the stop untouched [U7YrYjIS9B8, MXXsv0RSek8].
- Quoting O'Neil's 7% rule while rejecting it, yet using a 15% fixed stop elsewhere [mR0HMssJek8, aUgaUeCmSOQ].

**Not covered**
- No time stop.
- Almost no short-side trailing or target parameters.
- No rules for gaps through exit levels.
- No guidance on intraday versus closing exits beyond the Kijun and pivot "closing basis" rules.
- No numbers on how his own exits have performed.
- Two entry-side notes about not buying extended stocks [rbSj4pOg3_I, NtpyZH5cfaE] are only indirectly relevant here. They imply exits should happen before such extension, but no extension-based sell rule is given.
