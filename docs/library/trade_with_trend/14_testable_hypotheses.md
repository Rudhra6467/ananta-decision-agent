# Testable hypotheses (the register)

This chapter turns Raunak A's teaching into a register of hypotheses. Each row is his claim or method restated as a question a backtest can answer. **None of them has been validated.** Many come from one or two worked chart examples in a video. The thresholds (40% breadth, 8% extension, −7% stop, 16 weekly tranches) are his teaching numbers, not fitted results.

Before any row can become a trading rule, the research program's rules apply:

- **Pre-register** the exact definition, threshold, universe and horizon before looking at results.
- **Count every look.** Each threshold or variant tried counts as a test.
- **Use real costs.** That means Indian STT, brokerage, slippage and impact for small caps, and the equivalent in Canada and the US.
- **Keep a sealed holdout period.** Nothing is tuned on it.

Survivorship-free, point-in-time index membership is required for every breadth and constituent test.

**How the register was built**

- The source notes contained about 1,500 candidate statements. Many repeat across videos, so they are merged here into 60 rows.
- Where his videos give different numbers for the same idea, the range is shown and every source is cited.
- The notes carry no reliable dates. I therefore cannot say which version of a threshold is earlier or later, only that the videos differ.
- Lower-priority candidates (IPO behaviour, gap types, kicker and coil patterns, TD lines, R-multiple and risk-of-ruin arithmetic, Renko) remain in the full candidate list and are not lost. They are simply not in the first 60.

**Market codes:** "IN/CA/US" means it can be tested in all three with local data. "IN (CA/US adapted)" means the inputs must be substituted (local VIX, a local financials index, local flow data). "IN only" means the data exists only in India.

### Where his videos disagree (test both sides; do not pre-pick his favourite)

- **Low breadth: avoid or buy?** H01 says weak breadth means stand aside from longs. H04 says extreme lows are buying zones. Jpu536_AmGc says long portfolios do worse at ~10% above the 200 DMA, while V41BUpPK3E4 says <10% precedes better index returns. Both can be true (index versus breakout returns, different horizons), so the test must separate the index-return and breakout-return outcomes.
- **Wait for the reclaim or accumulate early?** H37 (M4Z-Gi4gGag, hKnYYRpd63s) says wait for the index to reclaim its averages. H36/H38 say buy in tranches below the 200 DMA.
- **Resilient or beaten-down sectors after a low?** Most videos say the sectors that fell least lead (H11). -qQntPmfe-s says the sectors with the largest prior drawdown outperform in the first 20–40 days.
- **Prior-cycle leaders.** V41BUpPK3E4, iWa7MbGawvU and ywccnKeWXNo say last cycle's leaders lag after a correction. 8QeR8FmYYg4 says leaders that reclaim highs first outperform.
- **Valuation.** BhQ_YnaRqa0 and uFFAAZNSozg prefer cheap stocks. N5bb0Cu8RAs and KRoie4cj5TQ say high-P/E breakouts do no worse or do better (H46).
- **Beta.** zWYCYySQVNc, LahSe1vu1kY and QWcb7yxYAxk favour beta above 1.2–1.3. uFFAAZNSozg says low-beta signals are better risk-adjusted.
- **Stochastic setting.** H0XAVFBdbzw says (8,3,3) is worse than (15,3,3). 2Yv0E57_bs4 says (8,3,3) beats (14,3,3).
- **Exits.** His positional videos say exit on structure (H29). His swing videos use fixed targets: 8–12% (2Yv0E57_bs4), 15–20% (R6QjIoYHoFE), +8–10% (QWcb7yxYAxk). These may be different strategies rather than a contradiction, so test each within its own style.
- **Breakout retest rate.** About 40% (mR0HMssJek8) versus more than 65% (-SC5vcQ1NuE).
- **Golden/death cross.** M4Z-Gi4gGag suspects it adds nothing. 20BxPDdAZAE uses the golden cross as confirmation.
- **IPOs.** Several videos favour recent IPOs breaking out after lows. iWa7MbGawvU and 20BxPDdAZAE say IPOs underperform in drawdowns and tight liquidity.
- **FII selling and small caps.** Small caps outperform during FII selling (0nbQs92kYuY, SNrzU-z3RkE) versus heavy FII selling being followed by weak small caps (JcxWhCO0hqI).

## The 10 to test first, and why

These ten were chosen because they are the spine of his repeated process: market regime, then sector, then stock, then entry, then management. They recur across many videos, can be stated precisely, and need only price and volume data that exists for India, Canada and the US.

1. **H01, breadth gate on long entries.** His single most repeated regime filter (13 videos). It needs only constituent closes. Test one pre-registered threshold (40%) with a 25–50% sensitivity band.
2. **H02, index below its 50/200 DMA hurts breakouts.** The second regime pillar. It is trivially codable and decides whether the rest of his system is switched on.
3. **H03, regime-scaled risk.** This is his practical answer to H01/H02. It is the cheapest rule to adopt if H01/H02 hold, and it can be tested on the same breakout backtest.
4. **H08, sector RS line structure.** Every stock-selection video starts here. It needs a fixed swing-pivot rule, which also serves H06 and H26.
5. **H09, picking from the top 2–3 sectors.** This is the direct payoff of H08 and is cited in 12 videos.
6. **H12, stock resilience in corrections.** This is his main "what to buy after a low" rule. It needs objective correction dating, and that dating can be reused for H11 and H11's contradiction.
7. **H13, RS confirmation at breakout.** It is precise (ratio new high on the breakout day), price-only, and cited in nine videos.
8. **H18, low-volume 50 DMA pullback in leaders.** His core entry. Every condition is codable except "strong candle", which needs one numeric definition (e.g. a close in the top 25% of a bar whose range exceeds 1.2× the 20-day average).
9. **H27, staged breakout entry (25/75 versus 100%).** It is central to his risk teaching, and C8IZozIkPJY gives a specific claim (failed-breakout losses fall to about a quarter of full size) that the test can falsify.
10. **H29, structural exit versus fixed target.** It decides how all the entries above are scored. Test it on breakout and pullback entries separately, because his swing videos argue the opposite.

## What is not testable as taught, and what it would need

- **Elliott wave counts** (HcDysbOyn8A, JMZFlzfNOCQ, KvhIh2rU0R0). The counts are subjective. Testing needs a published, rule-based wave labeller fixed in advance. Only the three "rules" (wave 2 not beyond wave 1's origin, wave 3 not shortest, no wave 4/wave 1 overlap) are checkable, and they are near-tautological once counts are chosen to satisfy them.
- **Heikin Ashi "initiation" and "confirmation" candles** (e34G88-BKI4, FtxgOtPYqVA, L99LgSdCDD8, IHqkSPVCxko). The notes say the definitions sit in a separate series not covered here. Testing needs numeric definitions (range versus ATR multiple, body/shadow ratios, number of confirming bars).
- **"Wide range", "decisive", "tight", "extended", "prominent"** appear across H18, H22, H24, H56 and H58 (e.g. xzR6bwNgb18, p03ho_Rhc_E, SbtFCxbIB1w). Each needs a pre-registered numeric proxy. Treat each proxy as one counted look.
- **Unstated indicators.** These are the RS formula in NYWbbvaJnPc, the breadth indicator shown but not named in VCNsjVuGoWQ, the ATR-scaled decline indicator in elpQ8wyLZLQ, the composite index score in c-0bHPew1fg, and the RRG settings (c-0bHPew1fg, Pm_1kb8Fku0). They must be reconstructed, and a reconstruction tests our version, not his.
- **Chart-angle rules** (45°/60° slopes in tTdy5srpW9Q and K06GqbJwEv4). These depend on axis scaling. They would need converting to a percent-per-day rate of change.
- **Stage and pivot calls.** These are partly discretionary in his use. They are testable only through a fixed classifier (30-week MA slope over N weeks plus a swing rule). Results then speak for the classifier, not for his eye.
- **Single-episode or dated calls** (butE8gDPXxw on a 6-month Nifty versus S&P call; the 2025 dollar-index level of ~110 in zHzq3uAFJFs; tariff-shock analogies in B1ynZNvqesE). Each is one observation and cannot be tested statistically. Only the generic rule behind it can be.
- **Retail outcome claims.** Examples are that F&O traders do far worse than cash-equity traders (eeKRfAbbvZA, arFNgAPisHU), or that a trader who never lost capital through a bear market will do better out of sample (6ZKLrl0z8jY). These need account-level broker or regulator data that we do not have.
- **Leverage-unwind and "judgment" items** (butE8gDPXxw; profile day-type filters in Nkp_wwGZFNQ, marked NO in the notes). These need positioning data (margin debt, open interest, funding) or an agreed day-type classifier before any test.
- **Hindsight screens.** "What 10× winners looked like before their run" (Bqp9jKQCs30) is built from known winners and is biased by survivorship. A valid test must screen forward on point-in-time data, delisted names included.
- **Small-sample breadth extremes** (H04, the 8–14% band in 6GqBBVt6fUs; long-cycle claims in x2RbjJp1eCI and hpbDmEK-FvM). India has only a handful of independent episodes. Results will be suggestive at best unless pooled with Canadian and US histories, and the thresholds re-fitted there count as additional looks.
