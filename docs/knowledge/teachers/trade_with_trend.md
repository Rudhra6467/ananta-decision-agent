# Trade With Trend (Raunak A, India): what the channel teaches

287 videos, about 253K subscribers, 2011-2026. Indian stocks and indices first; daily and weekly charts; the speaker repeatedly
warns against 5-30 minute trading for most people. Inspirations he names: Stan Weinstein, William O'Neil, Mark Minervini,
Peter Lynch, Wyckoff, Dow theory.

Madhav's summary: Raunak gives **regime and context**: regime quality -> relative strength -> volume -> contraction -> breadth.

## 1. Regime (is the market allowed to be long?)
- Traders use the 50-day average, investors the 200-day. No aggressive buying below the 50-day; cut position size and risk
  75-90% when the index is below both the 50- and 200-day averages. Exit or reduce after 2+ closes below the 50-day.
- Weekly checks of world markets (S&P 500, NASDAQ, Nikkei, crude) against their 50-day averages; S&P 500 falling with momentum = do not trade for income.
- No-trade zone: a congestion band on the index where neither longs nor shorts are taken.
- Structure first, then breadth, then momentum. Never enter on momentum alone.

## 2. Stage analysis and relative strength (stock selection)
- Weinstein: weekly 30-week average (he uses 50 weeks), buy only stage 2 (rising average, higher highs and lows),
  never stage 4; avoid stocks stuck below the 30-week average. Best case: price and the RS line break multi-year resistance together.
- Relative strength line = price / benchmark. Long only when price and RS both make higher highs; RS often turns first;
  never chase rising price with falling RS. Sector RS first, then stocks inside the strongest sectors; 'sister stocks' confirming.
- Leadership checklist: above the 50 and 200-day, 50-day above 200-day, RS beating both the sector and the index, volume up on rises and down on dips.

## 3. Bases and breakouts
- Wyckoff accumulation: selling climax (highest volume) -> automatic rally -> secondary test on low volume -> spring -> last point
  of support -> jump on expanding volume. Stop at the spring or climax low; at least 1:2 reward to risk.
- VCP / build-up / coil-spring: each swing smaller, volume drying up, 1-3 months; breakout volume above the prior 20 sessions;
  stop below the base; first base breakouts, not stocks already up 120-130% from the base.
- False-breakout filter (3 rules): wide-range breakout candle closing near its high with little wick; volume clearly above recent
  sessions (ideally the highest in 2-3 months); relative strength rising.
- 'Over 65% of breakouts initially fail' and retest; buy the retest of the old resistance rather than the first push.

## 4. Pullbacks
- Buy pullbacks to a rising 50-day average in stage-2 leaders (investors near the 200-day); stop below the pivot under the 50-day.
- Do not chase price 8-10% (or 8-15%) above its averages.
- Tennis-ball action: a leader that dips for 1-5 days after a breakout on low volume and bounces is healthy.

## 5. Breadth and market bottoms
- Healthy market: more than 50% of stocks above the 200-day and 70% above the 50-day; below 35-38% above the 200-day = more downside;
  17-25% = minimum position size.
- Real recovery vs bear-market rally: price holds above the 50-day, then breaks the 200-day on rising volume, led by the sectors
  that resisted the fall. Bear-market rallies can be 30-50%.
- Bottom confirmation (4-15 day rule): a broad recovery day, a wide-range up day on higher volume in days 0-5, no break of the first
  rally day's low in days 4-10, leaders consolidating quietly in days 10-15.
- 'Amazing opportunities coincide with horrible news'; buy broad ETFs in tranches during deep declines, not single collapsing names.

## 6. Risk and position sizing
- 25% at the breakout, 75% after the pullback holds and a wide-range candle with volume confirms (pyramiding). Other versions:
  30/30/30, 30-50/30/20. Test positions of 10-15% first.
- Risk about 1% per trade (1-1.25% in thin markets, 2% only in strong bull markets); no single stock above 7-8% of the portfolio.
- Exit everything when open positions are down about 7-8% in total. Cut losers in thirds (-3/-5/-7%).
- R-multiples: at least 100 trades before judging; keep losses under 1R; risk of ruin below 2%.
- Take 25-35% off after a 30-50% run; more on a break of the 50-day.

## 7. Older indicator courses (vocabulary)
Heikin Ashi (initiation and confirmation candles), Ichimoku (Kumo breakout), Market Profile (value area, POC, opening types,
rotation factor), Elliott Waves, Renko, ADX + 20 EMA (Raschke), stochastic, DeMark trendlines, gap trading with RSI, opening range.
Mostly discretionary or intraday; kept as vocabulary.

## Contradictions inside the channel (to resolve with data, not opinion)
'Buy pullbacks, not breakouts' vs many breakout entries; 'never trade 5/15/30-minute' vs the 5-15 minute Ichimoku systems;
different scale-in splits; 50% vs 33% cut when price returns to the buy point.
