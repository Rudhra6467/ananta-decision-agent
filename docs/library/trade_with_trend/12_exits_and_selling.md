# Exits and selling

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits his method

Raunak's exit teaching rests on one idea: **let price tell you when the trend is over.** You do not predict tops, set targets, or sell because the market "feels high." Instead, you trail positions with structure: pivot lows, lower-high/lower-low sequences, or a moving average. You take partial profits into strength. You cut losers fast and leave winners alone [eeKRfAbbvZA, Lw6h6T30VvE, kkJ4m9JQ4pM, MXXsv0RSek8].

This fits his larger approach, which is stage 2 breakouts in strong sectors with high relative strength, held for months. The exit has to be wide enough to survive the normal 5–8% shakeouts inside a trend [lRUDp336l7Q, Qu49zHwEGwo, 6ZKLrl0z8jY].

He also applies exits at the **portfolio level**. When the index breaks its 50 or 200 DMA, sell losers first and keep the leaders [8QeR8FmYYg4, DCG1GO9TGFU, KDSbZ0oW2U0].

His teaching has visibly shifted over time:
- **7–8 years ago:** many pattern-specific fixed targets (measured moves, 8–20% swing targets).
- **About 2 years ago:** he says targets are for fools [Lw6h6T30VvE].
- **Last year or so:** structured 50 DMA scale-outs and portfolio rules.

Treat all of what follows as his claims and hypotheses, not established results.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Core stance: trail, don't target; hold while structure holds

**What it is.** Hold as long as daily structure is unchanged and no supply appears. Do not exit on a single sharp counter-move, a pause candle, or a small dip below entry. Exit only when the planned trailing stop is actually hit [eeKRfAbbvZA, MXXsv0RSek8, mim7ylyaN74].

**Supporting points:**
- Patience, trend following and loss cutting are one linked formula. Losers are frequent, so small profits must not be booked early [eeKRfAbbvZA].
- Exits are harder than entries. Tops rarely form a clean stage 3 range; price often collapses vertically. So the exit must be mechanical rather than pattern-timed [Bqp9jKQCs30].
- Do not fear that the market is at a high. Use trailing and protective stops and wait for the market to signal the end [kkJ4m9JQ4pM].

**Numbers:**
- Intended positional holds are 6–12 months, long or short [6ZKLrl0z8jY].
- Trends run many months with 5–8% drawdowns along the way [lRUDp336l7Q].
- Leaders can give back 5–8% easily [Qu49zHwEGwo].

**Timeframe.** Use trailing pivots on daily or weekly charts. He calls anything below roughly 75-minute or hourly charts a waste of time for this approach [Lw6h6T30VvE].

**Personal lesson** [U7YrYjIS9B8]:
- He used to exit range breakouts on a small dip below entry, then watch the stock make new highs.
- For range breakouts, the stop belongs below the range low.
- Ignore weak candles that do not hit that stop.

**When not to use it.** He frames the 52-week-MA and buy-and-hold styles as wrong for narrow markets. In those conditions he prefers a nimbler 50 DMA approach (see 2.3) [LWoEC9OjajE].

**Clarity and frequency.** High and repeated across about 8 videos spanning 2 to 8 years.

### 2.2 Structural trailing: pivots and lower-high/lower-low

**What it is.** Mark major and minor trending pivots. Hold while the latest major pivot low is intact, and do nothing during corrections above it. A close below the major pivot is the exit. Once a stock is profitable, a break of the current trailing level is the sell trigger [mR0HMssJek8].

**Conditions and numbers:**
- **Closing basis, set in advance.** Example: Anant Raj, bought at about 120–125 and trading near 155. He would sell everything on a daily close below the pivot at about 132–133 [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- **Two-step lower-high/lower-low rule.** Sell about 50% at the first lower-high/lower-low violation and the rest at the second [mR0HMssJek8].
- **Valuation is no reason to hold.** Exit a held growth stock when its daily chart turns lower-high/lower-low, even with a high PE and strong fundamentals [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- **After a breakout goes sideways.** Once a new high-volume node forms, trail below the latest pivot. If the range resolves down, the trade ends in profit [p03ho_Rhc_E].
- **Ignore single bars.** Hold through strong bearish candles if the higher-high/higher-low structure and pivot are intact [IHqkSPVCxko].
- **Use the demand zone.** Tata Consumer: entered about 650, flat after 10 days. He held while price stayed in the demand zone, with an aggressive cut below 640–645 [eeKRfAbbvZA].
- **Members' version.** Members trail under recent pivot lows [T4849hhdwTo]. This is a member's method, not his own.
- **Older video.** Market structure (lower highs and lower lows for shorts) is offered as one exit option, which he called subjective at the time [2crnCCz4Y3s].

**Clarity and frequency.** High, about 7 videos. "Major" versus "minor" pivot is never defined numerically.

### 2.3 Moving-average staged exits (two versions; his preference changed)

**Older version: 52-week MA on the weekly chart** [Bqp9jKQCs30, about 8 years ago]
1. On a weekly close below the 52-week MA, sell 50%.
2. Mark the relevant weekly pivot low.
3. Sell the other 50% if that pivot breaks.

Supporting points:
- For fundamentally strong companies reporting good results, never sell everything on the first MA break. Treat the full exit as confirmed only by the pivot break or a proper stage 3 breakdown, which he calls rare.
- **Re-entry:** if the stock instead builds a new base and breaks out, buy back the sold 50% and resume trailing. He walks through a 2010–2012 example.

**Newer version: O'Neil-style 50 DMA scale-out** [LWoEC9OjajE, about 10 months ago]
1. After a 30–50% rally, sell 25–30%.
2. Trail the rest with the 50 DMA.
3. On a 50 DMA break, sell another 33–40%.
4. If the stock recovers, the low of the candle that broke the 50 DMA becomes the "50 DMA pivot."
5. Exit fully if that pivot breaks.
6. Repeat on a fresh stage 2 setup with improving RS.

Supporting points:
- Use the 50 DMA, the 50 DMA pivot and the 200 DMA together rather than holding blindly until the bull market ends.
- This is still positional holding, not buy-today-sell-tomorrow.
- He recommended this nimble style for roughly the next 12–18 months, and especially for traders who tend to give back big profits.

**Index-based variant.** Use the index's 50 DMA as the trailing reference for positions built earlier. In the worst case, exit when the index pulls back to it [bhTEw6wD_CU, low clarity].

**Clarity and frequency.** High in each source. The shift from a 52-week weekly MA to a 50 DMA reflects a regime view (narrow market), not a declared replacement.

### 2.4 Partial profits into strength

| Trigger | Size | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Swing high or consolidation in a leading group | 20–30% | eeKRfAbbvZA |
| +30–50% gain | 25–40% (25–30% in the 50 DMA scheme) | rocXNq3_1Zw, LWoEC9OjajE |
| +15–20% above breakout level | 50% | p03ho_Rhc_E |
| Each strong wide-range candle in the trade direction | 5–15% | 59vJPo6oiNA |
| Wide-range candles after an opening-range-breakout entry (day traders) | unspecified | hk4d8AxbQ3A |
| 2R and 3R (example: ₹50 risk, so +₹100 and +₹150) | unspecified | 1klATqC7oks |
| Open profit well above the strategy's average winner (example: 3R vs an average of 1.5–2.2R), even while price still rallies | at least part | Wk4NVr226t8 |
| Monthly-timeframe resistance | exit or scale | dV4MiNaAenI |

**Conditions:**
- After a partial sale, resume trusting the trend until a new consolidation or a lower-high/lower-low structure appears [eeKRfAbbvZA].
- The 30–50% rule is mainly for people who would be upset seeing a 40% gain return to cost. Those deliberately holding a few multi-year winners can do nothing, but they must know which type they are [rocXNq3_1Zw].
- He judges exits against his own R history (does he usually exit at 2R, 3R or 4R?). He notes many swing traders exit fully around 1.5R [59vJPo6oiNA].

**Clarity and frequency.** Medium to high, about 8 videos. Sizes vary widely (5% to 50%) with no unifying rule.

### 2.5 Indicator and candle exits (mostly older videos, 5–7 years ago)

**Ichimoku:**
- **Two-stage exit:** stage 1 when Chikou crosses below price; stage 2 when price is clearly below both Tenkan and Kijun [xzR6bwNgb18].
- **Kijun stop counts only on a decisive close.** An intraday dip that closes back above it is not a trigger [153pJZk-ixo].
- **Trailing choice:** exit on a close below Kijun (earlier) or Senkou B (later). In his ICICI example, Kijun gave an exit at about 475 versus 470–471 for Senkou B, roughly 1% better [59vJPo6oiNA].
- **Cloud breakouts:** exit on re-entry into the cloud, or on structure [2crnCCz4Y3s].

**Heikin-Ashi:**
- Do not sell on a pause; sell only on a clear bearish HA candle [L99LgSdCDD8].
- Ignore isolated small-body indecision candles inside a strong trend [uqEFppHC4Jw].
- A long upper shadow after a long advance is a caution signal. Wait 2–3 sessions for strong red follow-through before acting [uqEFppHC4Jw].
- Reduce only on wide-range reversal candles with confirmation, or on a structure break [IHqkSPVCxko].

**Momentum and volatility:**
- Cut before the full breakdown on fading momentum: repeated long wicks, range expansion, wide bearish bars, and falling volume. Example: HUL, entered about 2420–2430, cut below about 2320–2330 [eeKRfAbbvZA].
- Book profits when strong bearish candles cluster while volatility rises. Re-enter if strong-trend traits return [tTdy5srpW9Q].
- Repeated RSI lower lows followed by a price lower low signal weakening [R6QjIoYHoFE].

**Retracement depth.** As a trend matures, pullbacks deeper than 38.2% and toward 50% are a cue to take partial or full profit [tTdy5srpW9Q].

**Clarity.** High for the Ichimoku and HA rules; medium for the volatility reading, which is discretionary.

### 2.6 Fixed targets (older, pattern-specific; later disowned in principle)

**Measured moves:**
- **Range breakouts:** expect at least 2× the range height, often 3–4× [K06GqbJwEv4].
  - With no breakout volume expansion, expect about 1:1 to 1:2 risk/reward.
  - With expanding volume and range, plus a rise angle above 60°, expect 1:3 to 1:4.
  - Ranges of 2–3 months and a prior momentum-driven decline improve the odds.
- **A-B legs:** breakout level plus the A-B leg. Example: 5550 + 350 ≈ 5900. Strong trends can extend 3–4× the A-B distance [VqoLpEvre04].
- **TD supply line:** take the distance from the true low of the supply region to the line, and add it at the line-contact point. Example: 10,220 + 320 = 10,540, reached in 7–8 days. The aggressive variant adds the distance to the breakout candle's close or high instead [Vz5lqc_RO7M].

**Fixed percentages:**
- 10–15% if the market is momentum-driven [VqoLpEvre04].
- 15–20% for hidden-divergence swings [R6QjIoYHoFE].
- 8–10% conservative, 15–18% aggressive, scaling out past 10–12% [2Yv0E57_bs4].
- 8–10% conservative for kicker patterns, often 15%+, at 2:1 to 4:1 [QWcb7yxYAxk].
- 20–30% over 2–4 weeks (3:1 to 5:1) for weekly outside bars, versus 5–15% over days for kickers [_AiSzhs67J4].

**IBD rules.** He quotes the IBD rules (take ~10% gains, 7% automatic stop) but says he does not follow the 7% rule [mR0HMssJek8].

**Contradiction.** This whole block conflicts with "targets are for fools" [Lw6h6T30VvE] and with the trail-only stance in 2.1. A plausible reading is that targets belong to short swing setups and trailing belongs to positional trades. He never states that reconciliation himself.

### 2.7 Loss-side exits beyond the initial stop

- **Average-loss guardrail.** Use the strategy's historical R distribution. If the average loss is −0.6R to −0.9R and an open trade passes −1R, immediately cut size or exit [Wk4NVr226t8].
- **Off-plan trades.** If a trade is not working per plan, exit at least 50%, then review [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- **Premature entries.** Exit a failed early entry fast. Example: a Kotak Bank flag bought early and exited the next day [mim7ylyaN74].
- **Fixed percentage stop.** About 15% below entry for long-term equal-value positions. The caption reads "falls above 15%," which is likely a mishearing [aUgaUeCmSOQ].
- **Stops protect against catastrophe** even when buying dips. His biggest loss that financial year came from a realty stock entered early in a failed base, which fell 12–15% [rxqOUxO_vP4].
- **No revenge trades.** After a loss in a counter, he generally does not trade that same counter again, even on a similar setup [VET4JHrc2vU].
- **Narrow-range declines.** He loses interest in re-entering a stock whose candles are mostly narrow-range after a decline [mR0HMssJek8].

### 2.8 Portfolio-level selling in pullbacks and corrections (recent, last 1–2 years)

- **Order of action on an index 50 DMA break** (Nifty Smallcap 250, breakdown confirmed) [8QeR8FmYYg4, about 2 weeks ago]:
  1. Sell the losers first.
  2. Then, depending on comfort with chart structure, exit positions or book partial profits.
  
  He says, citing O'Neil, that the breach marks the end of the up-leg.
- **Losers first, never winners first.** In dips, do not touch winners; cut losers first. He says most people do the reverse [DCG1GO9TGFU].
- **The repeating cycle** [KDSbZ0oW2U0]:
  - In each pullback, cut losers, keep winners and add to winners.
  - If a winner turns into a loser, cut it completely.
  - Repeat with every pullback.
- **"Water your flowers, cut your weeds"** [g3mcqf6rqoc].
- **Keep only the leaders.** When a mini swing-down seems to start, cut everything except the best performers of the prior up-leg. The portfolio itself is the best indicator; no chart indicator times these turns [elpQ8wyLZLQ].
- **BCG framing** [Qu49zHwEGwo]:
  - "Stars" (biggest gainers, merely consolidating): hold and add.
  - "Dogs" (negative on the holding): cut if the market moves below its 50 DMA.
  - "Cash cows" (slow growers): can be cut.
  - Good sector and RS selection means little churn is needed.
- **200 DMA rule for the next cycle.** When the index sustains below its 200 DMA, sell holdings decisively below their own 200 DMA, especially if that average is turning down. Move into better stocks or cash. He admits selling during the fall itself may not have been prudent [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- **When the market feels too easy:** tighten stops, avoid leverage, preserve profits. Do not exit everything; Nifty at 24,000 could still reach 26,000 (July 2024) [r11n7ztY2YY].
- **Late-cycle options:** FDs, ETFs, selective shorts or cash. He says he does not know which is best [hKnYYRpd63s].
- **Systematic investment plans.** Hitting a review zone means pausing contributions and redirecting money, not making a binary sell decision [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].

### 2.9 Time

There is **no time stop.** He holds a flat position after 10 days if structure is intact [eeKRfAbbvZA]. His only holding periods are descriptive:
- 6–12 months for positional trades [6ZKLrl0z8jY].
- 2–4 weeks for weekly outside bars [_AiSzhs67J4].
- Weeks for 8–15% swings [2Yv0E57_bs4].

He also treats extension as a reason not to buy rather than a sell rule:
- Stocks up 35–89% in a month offer no risk-reward [rbSj4pOg3_I].
- Stocks 15–25% above the 50 DMA are poor risk-reward [NtpyZH5cfaE].

### 2.10 Short side

The notes on short exits are thin. What exists:
- Mirror the lower-high/lower-low and cloud exits [2crnCCz4Y3s, xzR6bwNgb18].
- Ride the whole trend down and exit only on the trailing stop [MXXsv0RSek8].
- 6–12 month horizon also applies to shorts [6ZKLrl0z8jY].
- A price excess (island-reversal-like) typically ends a counter-trend rally in a bear phase. This is useful for timing short re-entry [Nkp_wwGZFNQ, about 13 years ago].
- Sizing on successive short signals: trade the first two signals in a down move at full size, then at least 50% smaller. His reasons are that prices fall for shorter periods than they rise and counter-rallies can be large [LahSe1vu1kY]. This is a sizing rule, but it implies shorter short holds.

## 3. How a program would test it

**Data for all tests:**
- Survivorship-free daily (and weekly) OHLCV for NSE stocks, adjusted for splits and bonuses.
- Index series: Nifty 50, Nifty Smallcap 250.
- Delivery-cost model (STT, stamp duty, brokerage, slippage).
- A reproducible stage 2 breakout entry definition for the entry universe. For example: a close above a base high after a base of at least 6 weeks, with price above a rising 200 DMA.

1. **50 DMA scale-out vs alternatives** [LWoEC9OjajE, rocXNq3_1Zw]
   - Rule: on breakout entries reaching +30%, sell 27.5% at +30%; sell 36.5% of the original on the first daily close below the 50 DMA; record that candle's low as the pivot; sell the remainder on a close below the pivot.
   - Compare against: buy-and-hold to a fixed horizon; full exit on a close below the 50 DMA; full exit on a close below the 200 DMA.
   - Metrics: CAGR, average giveback from peak, MAR ratio.
   - Split results by breadth regime (% of stocks above their 200 DMA).

2. **52-week weekly MA half-exit with re-add** [Bqp9jKQCs30]
   - Rule: sell 50% on a weekly close below the 52-week SMA; sell the rest on a weekly close below the most recent weekly swing low (define a fractal, e.g. 2 bars each side); re-buy the 50% on a new base breakout.
   - Compare against: a full exit at the MA, and no re-add.

3. **Pivot trailing vs fixed targets** [mR0HMssJek8, KRoie4cj5TQ, Lw6h6T30VvE vs R6QjIoYHoFE, 2Yv0E57_bs4, VqoLpEvre04]
   - Same entries, three exits: close below the last major swing low (n-bar fractal, test n = 3, 5, 10); fixed +15% target with a range-low stop; measured move of 1× and 2× range height.
   - Metrics: expectancy in R and capture ratio of the maximum favorable excursion.

4. **Lower-high/lower-low half/half** [mR0HMssJek8]
   - Rule: on daily fractal swings, exit 50% at the first confirmed lower-high plus lower-low, and the rest at the second.
   - Compare against: a full exit at the first, and a full exit at the second.
   - Metrics: drawdown versus return.

5. **Portfolio losers-first rule** [8QeR8FmYYg4, DCG1GO9TGFU, Qu49zHwEGwo]
   - Construct a simulated momentum portfolio. On the first confirmed close of the Smallcap 250 below its 50 DMA after a 3-month or longer uptrend, sell all holdings with negative P&L and hold the rest.
   - Compare against: holding everything; selling the bottom-RS half; trimming winners first.
   - Measure forward 1-, 3- and 6-month returns and drawdown.

6. **Partial at the historical average winner** [Wk4NVr226t8, 1klATqC7oks]
   - Rule: compute the strategy's rolling average winning R. Sell 33% when open profit exceeds it; separately test a 2R/3R ladder.
   - Compare against: a single trailing exit.

7. **Heikin-Ashi long-upper-shadow confirmation** [uqEFppHC4Jw]
   - Event: after a 20%+ advance, a long-upper-shadow HA candle followed within 3 sessions by 2 strong red HA candles.
   - Measure the forward 5–10 day return against baseline, and the cost of exiting on it versus the pivot trail.

8. **Index 200 DMA weak-holding cull** [hpbDmEK-FvM]
   - Rule: when the index has 10 or more closes below its 200 DMA, sell stocks more than 5% below their own declining 200 DMA. "Decisively" must be parameterized; test 3–10%.

## 4. Transfer

**Usable as-is on TSX and US markets:**
- Pivot and lower-high/lower-low trailing.
- 50 DMA, 52-week and 200 DMA staged exits.
- Ichimoku and HA rules.
- R-distribution guardrails.
- Partial profits after 30–50% gains.
- The losers-first portfolio logic.

All of these are pure price or portfolio rules. The 50 DMA scale-out is explicitly derived from O'Neil, a US source.

**Needs adapting:**
- **Index triggers.** Replace the Nifty Smallcap 250 with the Russell 2000 or S&P 600 (US) and the S&P/TSX SmallCap or Completion index (Canada). The TSX small-cap universe is thinner and resource-heavy, so breadth-regime tests will be noisier.
- **Fixed % targets** (8–20%) and the "5–8% normal pullback" figure depend on Indian mid/small-cap volatility. Rescale by ATR or historical volatility.
- **Timeframes.** The 75-minute chart floor is tied to NSE session length (5 bars per day). Use 65- or 78-minute (US) or hourly bars elsewhere.
- **Costs.** Indian delivery costs (STT, stamp duty) penalize frequent partial exits more than US or Canadian commission-free brokers do. Re-evaluate the 5–15% per-candle scaling under each cost model.
- **Shorts.** Shorts in India are positionally constrained to F&O stocks (context, not his claim). US and Canadian borrow costs and availability change the short-side exit economics.
- **Fiscal-year and tax framing** of losses differs by country.

**India-only:**
- The specific rupee and level examples (Anant Raj, Tata Consumer, HUL, Nifty 24,000→26,000).
- His 12–18 month regime call for a "narrow market."
- The systematic investment plan pause framing, which is tied to Indian mutual fund and stock SIP practice.

## 5. Gaps and doubts

- **Targets vs trailing contradiction.** Older pattern videos (7–8 years ago) give fixed targets. Later he says targets are for fools [Lw6h6T30VvE]. He never explicitly maps target exits to swing setups and trailing to positional trades.
- **Moving-average choice changed.** The 52-week weekly MA [Bqp9jKQCs30] gave way to the 50 DMA [LWoEC9OjajE], justified by market breadth. There is no rule for when to switch back.
- **Undefined terms:**
  - "Major" versus "minor" pivot, "decisively" below the 200 DMA, and "confirmed" index breakdown have no numbers.
  - "Strong" wide-range candle and "clear" bearish HA candle are discretionary.
  - "Cash cow" is vague.
- **Inconsistent partial sizes.** 5–15%, 20–30%, 25–40% and 50% appear across videos, with no stated logic for choosing among them.
- **Re-entry is inconsistent.** He re-buys after a half exit [Bqp9jKQCs30] and re-enters when trend traits return [tTdy5srpW9Q]. Yet he avoids re-trading a counter after a loss [VET4JHrc2vU]. Whether the "no re-trade" rule applies to profitable exits is unclear.
- **Selling during the fall.** He wants a 200 DMA cull rule but says selling during the fall may not have been prudent [hpbDmEK-FvM]. This tension with the losers-first rules is unresolved.
- **Caption quality.** The 15% stop example is likely mis-transcribed [aUgaUeCmSOQ]. The index 50 DMA rule [bhTEw6wD_CU] and the ORB scale-out [hk4d8AxbQ3A] are low clarity.
- **Not his own methods.** Some examples are members' methods [T4849hhdwTo] or quoted IBD rules he does not follow [mR0HMssJek8].
- **Not covered:**
  - Time stops.
  - Short-side profit-taking in any detail.
  - Exits around earnings or events (one member exited on election day for volatility) [T4849hhdwTo].
  - Gap-through-stop handling.
  - Intraday versus closing execution, beyond the Kijun closing rule [153pJZk-ixo].
  - How partial exits interact with position-size or risk budgets.
  - Any evidence or backtest of his own. All numbers are examples or claims.
