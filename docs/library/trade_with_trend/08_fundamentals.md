# Fundamentals

## 1. What he teaches and how it fits

In Raunak A's method, fundamentals are a **filter that comes before the chart**. A short list of business checks cuts the universe down: sales and profit growth, margins, ROE/ROCE, debt, and ownership. Then stage analysis, relative strength and price structure decide timing [NUDjIohACWs, zWYCYySQVNc, hpbDmEK-FvM, 488FDla8kuc, T4849hhdwTo].

He says repeatedly that basic fundamentals are enough and need not be complicated [BhQ_YnaRqa0, tTdy5srpW9Q, ag4pL-OONoo]. The depth of the filter depends on holding period:

- **Swing trades:** a couple of light filters [zWYCYySQVNc].
- **Long-term, SIP and bear-market watchlists:** a fuller checklist (his "SPREAD" check) [ag4pL-OONoo, hpbDmEK-FvM, Q-uCkMMTh5Q].

His sources are O'Neil, Minervini and David Ryan for the growth-first view, and Peter Lynch for "buy what you use", PEG and insider selling.

Valuation is the least settled part of his teaching:

- Sometimes he dismisses P/E in favour of EPS growth [KRoie4cj5TQ, N5bb0Cu8RAs].
- Elsewhere he calls valuation plus stage analysis most of the decision [uFFAAZNSozg].
- In 2024 he favoured cheap laggards [Pm_1kb8Fku0].

He also discusses fundamentals at market level (earnings seasons, India's P/E band, India's premium to emerging markets) to frame his overall bias. Everything below is his claim or method, to be treated as a hypothesis.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Order of operations: fundamentals screen first, chart second

**The method**
- Run the fundamental scan first, then a volume filter, then a high-beta filter (swing workflow) [zWYCYySQVNc].
- Fundamentals pick the candidate; "chart structure then handles the timing" (paraphrase) [NUDjIohACWs].
- Combining basic fundamentals with technical patterns narrows 3,000–4,000 stocks to 50–75 [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- His stated aim is to pair a strong sector, strong price performance and a reasonably good business [488FDla8kuc].
- Simple patterns work in good companies. Trading names with no revenue or profit is speculation [T4849hhdwTo].
- Valuations should be "combined with price action". The reasoning is in an earlier video that is not in these notes [kkJ4m9JQ4pM].

**Why fundamentals matter to him**
- Lasting trends need a fundamental or macro cause, such as reforms, elections, central-bank policy or company events.
- Only general awareness is needed, not deep analysis [tTdy5srpW9Q].

**How he flexes it**
- The fundamental scan matters more than rigid technical numbers. A 20–30% volume shortfall or a beta near 1.1 is tolerated [2Yv0E57_bs4].

**Clarity and frequency:** taught clearly and across many years, from about 8 years ago to 1 year ago. There is no explicit rule for what happens when a strong chart fails the fundamental screen.

### 2.2 Growth: sales, profit, margins (core long-term checklist)

Most of the detail comes from one video [ag4pL-OONoo]. Other videos echo it [Jpu536_AmGc, NUDjIohACWs, hpbDmEK-FvM, elpQ8wyLZLQ, Bqp9jKQCs30].

**Sales growth**
- He looks for consistent double-digit YoY sales growth and tracks "about 18%" upward. The "133%" upper figure is probably a caption error.
- 3-year sales CAGR should be above 20–25%. He also checks 5- and 7-year CAGR, crediting O'Neil.
- YoY growth matters more than QoQ, because seasonality can distort a single quarter. Quarterly acceleration is still a plus.
- Organic growth is preferred over acquired growth [ag4pL-OONoo, Jpu536_AmGc].
- His model example (unnamed company): sales CAGR of 20% over 10 years, 31% over 7, 36% over 5 and 69% over 3, with recent quarters up 50–75%.

**Margins**
- Gross margin above about 22–23%, and better if rising.
- Operating margin in the "higher double digits" and expanding.
- Margins are judged year over year [ag4pL-OONoo].
- Operating-margin trend appears again in [NUDjIohACWs] and in the new-age screen (double-digit operating margin) [elpQ8wyLZLQ].

**Profit versus sales**
- Profit should outgrow revenue, for example profit up 30% on revenue up 20%.
- If profit rises on flat revenue, check whether it is only cost cutting.
- Profit up while margins shrink is a warning. His example is an unnamed large-cap paint company.
- He suggests tracking revenue, profit and margins in a spreadsheet [ag4pL-OONoo].

**History check**
- Look at 5–10 years of sales, operating profit, margin, net profit, EPS and P/E. A real company shows steady growth [hpbDmEK-FvM].

**Multibagger traits (hindsight list)**
- Aggressive, growth-focused management.
- Strong sales growth in a market that is still expanding.
- Low debt and low volatility.
- Multi-year relative-strength outperformance [Bqp9jKQCs30].

**Growth-share matrix**
- Stars (high growth, high share) are the top candidates.
- Question marks (high growth, low share) are acceptable.
- Cash cows are optional.
- Dogs (low growth, low share, often penny or small caps) should be avoided or sold.
- He claims simple PE, PEG and earnings-growth checks remove 85–90% of stocks [Jpu536_AmGc].

**Clarity:** high in [ag4pL-OONoo], where specific thresholds are given. Elsewhere it is mostly qualitative, and [Jpu536_AmGc] defers the details to another video.

### 2.3 Returns on capital (ROE/ROCE) and earnings quality

**His thresholds have moved over time:**

| Period (approx.) | Context | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~8y ago | Long-term checklist | ROE/ROCE ≥ ~6–7%, profit CAGR ≥ ~6–7% | [488FDla8kuc] |
| ~8y ago | Swing screen | ROCE > 20% and ROE > 20% | [zWYCYySQVNc] |
| ~8y ago | Swing screen | ROE/ROCE "covered automatically" by growth stocks | [2Yv0E57_bs4] |
| ~6y ago | Swing (Ichimoku) universe | ROE ≈ 25%, profit growth > 10% | [59vJPo6oiNA] |
| ~1y ago | Long-term | ROE > 15% "efficient"; growth sectors > 20%; stable sectors 8–12% | [ag4pL-OONoo] |
| ~3mo ago | Earnings-season screen | Profit growth > 25%, ROE and ROCE > ~13–14% | [lRUDp336l7Q] |

**Context rules** [ag4pL-OONoo]
- Compare ROE to peers, for example 18% versus a peer average of 10–12%.
- A rising ROE trend is good. A falling trend is a red flag.
- Great compounders (about 25% a year) usually have ROE of 15–18% or more, sustained or rising.

**ROE quality** [ag4pL-OONoo]
- ROE should be backed by positive free cash flow and steady operating cash.
- High ROE with negative FCF may mean manipulated earnings.
- Check that ROE is not inflated by debt or by one-time gains.
- Prefer ROE driven by rising net margin.
- No numeric FCF threshold is given.

**Positive view of Indian majors**
- He rates Indian large banks and IT companies among the world's best on ROE/ROCE [rxqOUxO_vP4].

### 2.4 Debt

**Absolute screens**
- Debt-to-equity below 0.5 [2Yv0E57_bs4, 59vJPo6oiNA].
- "No or very little debt" [488FDla8kuc].

**Bands** [ag4pL-OONoo]
- Below 0.5: low risk, typical of FMCG and pharma.
- 0.5–1.5: moderate. Acceptable for capital-intensive firms only with strong operating cash flow.
- Above about 2: high risk, which strong cash flow can offset.
- He admits the caption bands are loose.

**Industry context**
- D/E must be judged against the industry. Banks and NBFCs are structurally levered.
- He dislikes raw screeners because an absolute debt filter wrongly removes banks, finance firms and some growth firms [ag4pL-OONoo].
- He relaxed the D/E filter for banks back in [2Yv0E57_bs4].

**Good versus bad debt** [ag4pL-OONoo]
- Good debt funds expansion, R&D or capacity, and shows up later as profit and cash-flow growth.
- Bad debt funds losses, interest or dividends. He says it is common in small and mid caps.

**Where he applies it**
- Avoid heavily indebted firms for long-term SIP stocks [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].
- Bear-market watchlists [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- New-age picks, because he does not expect interest rates to help [elpQ8wyLZLQ].
- Tight-liquidity periods (avoid little-revenue, high-debt SME and IPO names) [20BxPDdAZAE].
- He says low-debt firms do better long term even if leverage helps short term [hpbDmEK-FvM].

**Clarity and frequency:** the most consistent fundamental rule across his whole archive.

### 2.5 The combined "SPREAD" checklist (sales, profit, ROE, debt)

[ag4pL-OONoo, echoed in hpbDmEK-FvM]

**Core condition:** high sales growth, high profit growth, high ROE and low debt equal strong financial health.

**Diagnostics**
- High sales and profit but low ROE: investigate inefficient use of capital.
- High sales but low profit: suspect cost leakage.
- High debt with good profit: risky if the economy, cash flow or margins turn.

**When he uses it**
- In bear markets, to avoid hyped names, because some stocks recover after a bear market while others collapse.
- He builds a watchlist of 5–10 such companies.
- He says these four metrics worked for him but are not the only ones that matter.

### 2.6 Ownership and governance

**Institutional ownership**
- At least 15% for large caps and about 10% for mid and small caps.
- His reasoning: institutions do research, so high ownership suggests decent management. He admits there are exceptions [zWYCYySQVNc].
- 15% or more again in [2Yv0E57_bs4].
- "High institutional ownership" in [488FDla8kuc].

**Promoter (insider) behaviour**
- High promoter holding is a plus [488FDla8kuc].
- Heavy promoter or insider selling is generally negative, per Lynch. He said he was seeing it at the time and would cover it later. No follow-up is in these notes [WI0tdUjH_Vg].

**Smart-money tracking**
- Check portfolios of top small/mid-cap mutual funds and scanners of marquee investors [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- Members combined charts with results, governance and institutional accumulation, CANSLIM-style. His example is a Zaggle-type pick [T4849hhdwTo].

**Clarity:** thresholds are stated clearly in the 8-year-old swing videos and rarely revisited.

### 2.7 Valuation: where his teaching conflicts

**Strand A — growth over P/E (O'Neil)**
- Dividends and low P/E matter far less than EPS growth.
- High payouts can signal weakness, and a low P/E usually reflects a poor past record.
- High P/E, P/S and P/B are fine if growth is visible and the chart sets up well [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- His top-25 holdings at the time:

  | Measure | His portfolio | Comparison |
  |---|---|---|
  | Average P/E | ~40s (top few ≈ 115, 80, 78, 70) | Nifty ~21 |
  | P/S | ~3 to 20+ | — |
  | P/B | mostly 4–23 | — |

  [KRoie4cj5TQ]
- Ignore noise about P/E above about 70–80. High P/E plus little debt and strong management is "excellent". O'Neil, Darvas and Minervini made money in stocks with P/E above 100.
- Stick with "pedigree" companies and ignore analysts who chase price [N5bb0Cu8RAs].

**Strand B — valuation matters**
- About 8 years ago he said valuation plus stage analysis is underrated and makes up 60–70% of the decision once a stock is cheap and in a favourable stage [uFFAAZNSozg].
- Simple P/E and P/B versus industry and market, used only as a rough guide. His examples:
  - HDFC Bank: P/E 23.6 vs industry 12.4 and market 30.4; P/B 3.1 vs 1.6 and 2.4.
  - SBI: P/E 8.4, P/B 1.5.
  - Automated fair-value estimates are reference only [BhQ_YnaRqa0].
- PEG (P/E divided by growth, from Lynch): a high P/E is fine if growth is high enough [p5O_uspLu9k, Jpu536_AmGc].
- The defence checklist includes "valuation versus growth" [p5O_uspLu9k].
- The swing screen lists "PEG > 2" as a growth measure. This looks inverted relative to normal use and is unresolved [2Yv0E57_bs4].
- Four-box grid of valuation (over or under) versus past performance (out- or underperformer). His 2024 stance favoured **undervalued underperformers** with good businesses, for example oil marketing companies at single-digit P/E [Pm_1kb8Fku0].

**Where Strands A and B meet**
- He warns against very high P/E (80–120) **with negligible sales**, often in micro and small caps [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- Reading his material together: a high P/E is acceptable when backed by real growth and quality, and suspect when it is not. He never states this reconciliation explicitly.

**Contrarian valuation pockets** (framed as perceived value, not advice)
- Value is perceived and differs by person. Buying an out-of-favour sector gets a good price for tomorrow, but price can fall further [0Hnffbj5pgE].
- IT fell about 36–40% from its 2024 top and trades at trailing valuations like March 2020. Private banks are near March 2020 valuations too [rbSj4pOg3_I].
- IT is near GFC-era multiples but can de-rate further. Some private banks trade at low P/B and P/E versus global peers [dpsU7JvHiMA].
- PSU banks are among the cheapest pockets, despite government risk. Defence and railways faced similar objections before they ran [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
- A Nifty 500 top-50 value index in a higher-high/higher-low structure with rising RS suggests money moving toward valuation comfort [Oq4FjWEDbd0].
- In practice he pairs cheapness with RS or price structure rather than buying cheapness alone [rbSj4pOg3_I, Oq4FjWEDbd0, uFFAAZNSozg].

### 2.8 Sector-specific checks

**Banks**
- Track ROE, ROCE, NIM, CASA share and NPA, reviewed every quarter, to confirm the chosen banks are still sector-best [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].
- SBI: clean balance sheet, NPAs within norms, deposit growth, solid NIM, ROE about 17% at P/E about 8.4.
- HDFC Bank: 3-year ROE about 16–17% [BhQ_YnaRqa0].
- Sector backdrop:
  - Gross NPA about 3.2%, down from an 11.2% peak.
  - Better provision coverage.
  - Credit growth 14–16% YoY [azzUmy2l_0U].
- Risk he flags: rising lending to 24–34-year-olds and EMI stress [rxqOUxO_vP4].
- The D/E filter is waived for banks [2Yv0E57_bs4, ag4pL-OONoo].

**Defence**
- A six-question qualitative screen, run by feeding annual reports into an AI assistant:
  1. Policy alignment.
  2. Order book and revenue visibility.
  3. Financial health (ROCE, ROE, profit growth, cash flow).
  4. Export and private-sector opportunity.
  5. Valuation versus growth.
  6. Execution and innovation.
- It cut about 14 names to about 5 [p5O_uspLu9k].

**Themes without stock-level rules**
- Capital markets: demat accounts above 15 crore; monthly SIP about ₹20,000 crore.
- Insurance: penetration 4.2%, with 6% expected by 2030.
- Asset management: AUM rose from about ₹10 lakh crore to ₹50 lakh crore or more [azzUmy2l_0U].
- IT margin expansion from AI over 2–3 years, but only after price confirms [NUDjIohACWs].
- AI as a net positive for earnings, with work at under 20–25% of the cost [8QeR8FmYYg4].

### 2.9 Newly listed and new-age companies

**The bullish case**
- Recent listings with low float and exciting models are fast movers in momentum phases, per Minervini and Ryan. Companies around 10 years old or more mostly move with the market [-qQntPmfe-s].
- He prefers companies listed after September 2024, many down 30–60% and some below issue price. 2020–24 IPOs that already rose 5–20x offer poor reward-to-risk.
- His filter: businesses and products you understand and use, from Lynch [-qQntPmfe-s, NtpyZH5cfaE, yEKNcJ4EH0U].
- Example: new-age names that corrected 45–50% then rebounded 25–30% from the bottom [NtpyZH5cfaE].
- New-age screen: double-digit growth, double-digit operating margin, low debt. He expected these to beat stagnant large caps in 2025–26 [elpQ8wyLZLQ].
- He sees the next ~5 years of opportunity here. Watch the IPO pipeline, listing gains, and new listings that break out with rising RS [kwbfXjRtEkk].

**Cautions**
- Valuations look exorbitant. Don't chase every IPO. A 7–12-month hold for a decent return is a fine outcome [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
- When liquidity is scarce (he estimated it at only about 20–25% "on"), avoid SME and new IPOs with little revenue and high debt. Large and mid caps should lead [20BxPDdAZAE].
- An IPO frenzy is mostly unfavourable to retail buyers [LWoEC9OjajE].

**How his view changed**
- About 1 year to 10 months ago he was cautious, with risk tied to the liquidity regime.
- About 5 months to 1 month ago he was clearly bullish on post-correction new listings.
- The quality conditions stayed the same throughout: real revenue, margins and low debt.

**Contrarian sentiment**

### 2.11 Market-level fundamentals (used for bias, not stock selection)

**India's valuation**
- India's forward P/E of about 22–23x is neither cheap nor dear against a 12x to 26–30x band [yYwiSDVwgyA].
- India's premium to EM fell from about 88% to the high 20s or low 30s. In his experience, stabilisation comes near 18–20% [SNrzU-z3RkE].

**Foreign investors and growth**
- FIIs return when top-300 companies deliver double-digit earnings growth.
- Nominal GDP of 11–13% is "good" for large caps [SNrzU-z3RkE].

**Small/mid-cap valuations — his view shifted**
- About 10 months ago he called them "still inflated", a risk [LWoEC9OjajE].
- About 2 months ago he said they are "not extremely expensive" given growth [0nbQs92kYuY].

**General bias calls**
- Disagrees that Indian valuations are too high; buying gradually [YVGWKmpHStM].
- Good quarterly results and macro support the market [CFJNeCPMQ1o, SuYHqw5zA8U, YFsvQXZZt1g].
- A genuine recovery needs improving fundamentals plus confidence, confirmed over 2–3 months [20BxPDdAZAE].

**Counterpoint he raised himself**
- Real inflation of 7–9% may exceed CPI of 4.3–4.8%, which could hurt demand and results. This is his own guess, unsupported by data [YFsvQXZZt1g].

### 2.12 Short side

He gives **no short-selling rules** from fundamentals. Negative signals are used only to avoid or exit:

- "Dogs" (low growth, low share) [Jpu536_AmGc].
- Profit up while margins fall [ag4pL-OONoo].
- High ROE with negative FCF [ag4pL-OONoo].
- Bad debt [ag4pL-OONoo].
- Heavy promoter selling [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- No-revenue, high-P/E names [hpbDmEK-FvM, T4849hhdwTo].

## 3. How a program would test it

**General data needs**
- Point-in-time fundamentals with reporting lags. Screener-style restated data creates look-ahead bias.
- A survivorship-free universe (Nifty 500 or wider).
- Index and sector benchmarks.
- A stage-2 / breakout definition from the chart chapters.

**Hypotheses**

1. **Earnings-season quality screen.** Stocks with trailing profit growth above 25% and ROE and ROCE above 13%, formed about 45 days after quarter end, beat the Nifty 500 over 6 and 12 months. Also test as a filter on stage-2 breakouts versus unfiltered breakouts [lRUDp336l7Q, NUDjIohACWs].
   - Data: quarterly profit, ROE, ROCE, filing dates, prices.

2. **SPREAD versus fails-one.** Test stocks passing all four checks:
   - 3-year sales CAGR ≥ 20%,
   - profit growth > sales growth with rising operating margin,
   - ROE > 15% and above the industry median,
   - industry-relative D/E below median (absolute < 0.5 outside financials).

   Compare them with stocks failing exactly one check, on 1–3-year returns and maximum drawdown. Run once from all months and once from bear-market lows [ag4pL-OONoo, hpbDmEK-FvM].
   - Also test absolute versus industry-relative D/E.

3. **High P/E is not a handicap at breakouts.** Among stocks making 52-week-high breakouts from stage-1 bases, compare the top and bottom P/E terciles on 6-month returns. Repeat with PEG terciles within sector, and exclude stocks with tiny revenue [KRoie4cj5TQ, N5bb0Cu8RAs, p5O_uspLu9k, hpbDmEK-FvM].

4. **Valuation × performance boxes.** Each month, sort stocks by 12-month return and by P/E percentile versus sector into four boxes. Compare forward 6–12-month returns and drawdowns. His 2024 claim is that undervalued underperformers have the best reward-to-risk; add an RS-turning-up condition as variant B [Pm_1kb8Fku0, uFFAAZNSozg].

5. **Recent-IPO base.** Stocks listed within 1–3 years that fell 40% or more from listing-period highs, then form a base and break out with positive 3-month RS versus the Nifty 500. Compare against older stocks with the same breakout signal over 3–12 months. Optionally condition on double-digit growth and operating margin with low D/E [-qQntPmfe-s, kwbfXjRtEkk, elpQ8wyLZLQ].
   - Data: listing dates, free float.

6. **Liquidity-regime quality tilt.** In tight-liquidity regimes, low-debt, profitable large and mid caps beat small caps, recent IPOs and loss-making stocks [20BxPDdAZAE].
   - Needs a liquidity proxy, for example a banking-liquidity or credit-spread measure. Define it ex ante.

7. **Results reaction by tape.** Measure 1- and 5-day returns after earnings beats, split by index above or below its 50-DMA. Separately, measure the size distribution of falls after guidance misses, with ~15% as his claim [DU5Y5AsCMZM].

8. **Institutional-ownership filter.** Swing entries in stocks with ≥15% (large) or ≥10% (mid/small) institutional ownership versus lower-ownership stocks. Compare the frequency of drops of 30% or more within 60 days [zWYCYySQVNc].

## 4. Transfer to TSX and US markets

**Usable as is**
- The ratio logic: sales and profit growth, profit outgrowing sales, margin trend, ROE level, trend and quality via FCF, industry-relative D/E, good versus bad debt.
- The SPREAD diagnostics.
- PEG, and P/E or P/B versus industry.
- The valuation × performance grid.
- The order of operations: screen, then chart.
- Earnings-reaction-by-tape tests.
- He names Finviz for US data [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].

**Needs adapting**
- **Thresholds.** The ROE, margin and growth cutoffs are tuned to India's high-growth, high-ROE large caps. Re-derive them as sector percentiles. His own "stable sector" ROE band of 8–12% shows how much context matters.
- **TSX sector mix.** The TSX is heavy in banks, insurers, energy, materials and REITs. Absolute D/E and ROE filters would gut it, so use industry-relative filters, as he himself recommends. Resource firms' ROE is cyclical, so trailing ROE can mislead near commodity peaks.
- **Bank metrics.** CASA becomes deposit mix or core-deposit share; NPA becomes impaired-loan and PCL ratios.
- **Size filters.** Market-cap cutoffs in crore (5,000 and 10,000) should be rescaled by percentile of the local universe, not by currency conversion.
- **Ownership.** Promoter holding has no direct equivalent; use insider ownership. Promoter selling becomes Form 4 / SEDI insider filings. Institutional ownership levels are far higher in the US, so 10–15% would screen out almost nothing.
- **New listings.** The IPO and low-float idea transfers (Minervini/Ryan originate in the US), but IPO cycles and lock-up structures differ. Canada's IPO flow is thin.
- **Value index.** The Nifty 500 value index becomes a Russell 1000 Value / S&P 500 Value or TSX value-factor ETF.
- **Fund holdings.** India's monthly mutual-fund disclosure becomes quarterly 13F data in the US, with a 45-day lag.

**India-only**
- GST reform [YVGWKmpHStM].
- RBI policy calls.
- India's premium to MSCI EM and the 18–20% "stabilisation" zone [SNrzU-z3RkE].
- FII flow thesis.
- PSU banks [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
- SME segment [20BxPDdAZAE].
- Demat, SIP, insurance-penetration and AMC theses [azzUmy2l_0U].
- Atmanirbhar defence policy criteria [p5O_uspLu9k].
- CPI-versus-real-inflation argument [YFsvQXZZt1g].

## 5. Gaps and doubts

**Inconsistent thresholds**
- ROE/ROCE ranges from 6–7% to ~25% depending on video and era (see the table in 2.3).
- Profit growth ranges from 6–7% CAGR to 10% to 25%.
- There is no single canonical screen. Testing should treat each as a separate variant and note the era.

**Caption problems**
- "PEG > 2" as a growth filter is probably inverted or misstated [2Yv0E57_bs4].
- The "133%" sales-growth figure is probably an error [ag4pL-OONoo].
- The upper D/E bands are loose [ag4pL-OONoo].

**Valuation contradiction**
- "Ignore P/E" [KRoie4cj5TQ, N5bb0Cu8RAs] conflicts with "valuation is 60–70% of the decision" [uFFAAZNSozg] and "favour undervalued underperformers" [Pm_1kb8Fku0].
- He never states when each applies. One possible reading is growth leaders in bull phases versus contrarian cheapness after sector drawdowns, but that is an inference.

**Views that changed**
- Small/mid-cap valuations moved from a risk to justified [LWoEC9OjajE → 0nbQs92kYuY].
- New listings moved from avoid-in-tight-liquidity to the main opportunity [20BxPDdAZAE → kwbfXjRtEkk].

**Missing rules**
- No rule for how a fundamental fail overrides a chart pass, or vice versa.
- No guidance on fundamentals and position sizing.
- No exit rule tied to fundamental deterioration, beyond quarterly bank reviews [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].
- No FCF threshold.
- No definition of "low float".
