# Sectors and intermarket

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits

Raunak A works top-down. He first reads the global and macro backdrop: the US market, the dollar index, crude, yields, central banks, and foreign flows. He then reads the Indian index and its breadth. Next he narrows to the few sectors or "pockets" showing relative strength (RS) and healthy structure. Only then does he pick stocks inside those pockets, preferring stocks that mirror the sector chart and that are confirmed by "sister stocks."

Sector selection is the most consistent and most codable part of his teaching. It runs from 8-year-old videos (RS lines, sister stocks) to the most recent ones (sector breadth tables, RRG quadrants, custom sub-sector indices).

His intermarket views are mostly regime filters rather than entry triggers. When the US falls fast, the dollar breaks out, or crude holds above about 100, he turns cautious, raises cash, or waits. When the central bank eases and leaders hold structure, he looks only for longs. Many of the macro statements are dated opinions tied to one episode. The levels he names (dollar index, crude, rupee) shift from video to video.

Everything below is his claim or method, to be tested, not established fact.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Top-down alignment: market → sector → stock

**What it is**
- Long side: when the broad index trends up, look only at sectors trending up, and within them only at stocks trending up [jlpd-YpqwiQ, arFNgAPisHU].
- Short side: the same order applies before shorting. Check the broad index (Nifty 500 or S&P 500), then the sector index, then the stock. A short is better when the index and the sector are both down [G2gjQUJRfJM].
- He calls sectors "always" his first step of analysis [sbHzNd2iA8k]. Narrowing to a few sectors comes before scanning stocks [Jpu536_AmGc, q26J58h5Qt8, SbtFCxbIB1w].
- Sector strength is checked three ways: RS ranking or trend, sector breadth, and overall market breadth [Jpu536_AmGc].

**Workflow**
- Scan sector and theme indices for breakouts.
- Pull the NSE constituent list.
- Pick constituents showing the same breakout. The sector is filter one; the stock is filter two [q26J58h5Qt8, lRUDp336l7Q].
- An older variant (7 August, 8 years ago) also required the sector to be above its 200-day average, beta above a threshold, and stochastic in buy mode. In that example banking was the only sector to pass [2Yv0E57_bs4].

**How taught:** high clarity, taught repeatedly over 8 years.

### 2.2 Sector relative-strength line and price structure

**Definition**
- RS line = sector index ÷ broad benchmark. He usually uses the Nifty 500 [BxY2ofigO8I, Yxo-rYOCuAo, c-0bHPew1fg, LWoEC9OjajE]. One 5-year-old video uses the Nifty 50 [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- Use the same time window for all sectors. Judge the RS line's structure first, deliberately before looking at sector price [BxY2ofigO8I].
- Lookback is usually 1 year, sometimes 2 [Yxo-rYOCuAo].

**Long condition**
- The RS line makes higher highs and higher lows [BxY2ofigO8I, Yxo-rYOCuAo], or breaks out to a new high and heads up [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- Later videos add price conditions: price above a *rising* 50 DMA, and a higher-high/higher-low price structure. The preferred entry is a pullback toward the 50 DMA rather than a blind buy [Oq4FjWEDbd0, DCG1GO9TGFU, fFUPLUEhzVo].
- Weekly variant: above the 30-week MA with rising RS, in Weinstein Stage 2 [lRUDp336l7Q, CFJNeCPMQ1o].

**Avoid condition**
- The RS line makes consecutive lower highs and lower lows, with repeated pivot violations. He reads this as distribution [BxY2ofigO8I].
- Do not trade a sector sustaining below its 50 DMA. Wait for it to get back above, base, and show a breakout [B8HZvUkY5sA, d3-OMTGOGx8].
- A Stage 4 sector rejected at the 50 DMA is avoided [rxqOUxO_vP4].

**How many sectors**
- "One to three" at any time [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- Two out of 9–10, which cut about 900 stocks to about 200 [BxY2ofigO8I].
- Three [eeKRfAbbvZA].
- "A couple" [d3-OMTGOGx8].
- Seven ranked pockets [Yxo-rYOCuAo].
- Five or six pockets holding 85–90% of capital [DCG1GO9TGFU].
- Six to eight on radar for redeploying cash [KDSbZ0oW2U0].

The count has drifted upward in recent years.

**Review cadence**
- Re-rank weekly or every 15 days. Do not buy and sit on a sector, because leadership rotates every few months [LWoEC9OjajE].
- Against chasing: an RS trend takes about 6–7 months to develop (his metals example). Chasing the best sector of the last 2–3 weeks misses the big trends [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- Once a sector turns, the move usually lasts many months, so there is no rush [NUDjIohACWs].

**Persistence claim**
- "Strong get stronger, weak get weaker," attributed to Livermore [c-0bHPew1fg, Yxo-rYOCuAo, S--7ta4FmbY].
- A weak sector stays weak unless a massive catalyst arrives. His example is IT, which is not a blind buy even if it looks cheap [S--7ta4FmbY].

**Instruments**
- Where index volume is missing, he ranks sector ETFs by price trend and RS. He frames this as a medium-to-long-term view [yEKNcJ4EH0U, VCNsjVuGoWQ].

**RRG and quadrants**
- He uses relative rotation graphs with his own, unstated sensitivity settings, and notes that different software gives different readings [c-0bHPew1fg, Oq4FjWEDbd0].
- A four-quadrant scheme across 56 sectors [6K1LOJqZYEg]:
  - near-term window: up to 1 week;
  - core-trend window: 3–6 months;
  - leading = strong on both;
  - weakening = core strong, near-term weak;
  - improving = core forming, near-term strong;
  - lagging = soft on both.

**How taught:** the most frequent, highest-clarity theme in this chapter.

### 2.3 Sector breadth

- **Heat map:** for each sector, % of constituents above the 200 DMA, as monthly snapshots. He says sector strength means little unless most of its stocks are above the 200 DMA. It can be built in a spreadsheet or Chartink in a couple of hours [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- **Example readings (1 June 2025):**
  - defense about 85% (from 19–25% in April);
  - capital markets 71% (from about 16%);
  - finance about 85%;
  - banks about 80% (only fell to about 52% in the correction);
  - IT about 30% [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- **Other readings:**
  - capital goods 59%, banks 50%, PSU banks 17%, which he treated as "pulling back, not broken" [0nbQs92kYuY];
  - aerospace and defense 90% above the 50-day, 86% above the 200-day, about 56% in Stage 2 [c-0bHPew1fg].
- **Stage-2 count:** rank sectors by the number of stocks in Stage 2 and above the 200 DMA and the 30-week MA [DU5Y5AsCMZM].
- **Mixed sectors:** a sector near 50% above the 200 DMA with a size split is scored "mixed." In financials, microfinance, NBFCs and small private banks were strong while large banks, insurance and PSU banks lagged [0nbQs92kYuY].
- **Industry labels:** he reads the industry labels of the top 50–100 ranked stocks to see which groups repeat [VCNsjVuGoWQ].
- **Sub-industry breadth:** when most sub-industries of a sector turn up together, he reads a sector-wide move. His example is financials after February 2025: banks, insurance, capital markets, AMCs and finance companies [azzUmy2l_0U].
- **Participation warning:** the capital-markets breakout came from only about 2 of 10–15 stocks [6GqBBVt6fUs].
- **Extension warning:** when most sector indices sit at the top of their 52-week range at once, he reads the market as extended (July 2024) [r11n7ztY2YY].

### 2.4 Sister stocks and group moves

**What it is**
- He credits the idea to Livermore [jlpd-YpqwiQ, _--GkDAomPk]. When one stock shows a setup, check same-sector or same-industry peers. If they show the same pattern, the trade is "higher probability," because money flows into whole groups [uFFAAZNSozg, jlpd-YpqwiQ].
- Long side: breakouts [Bqp9jKQCs30, VET4JHrc2vU, uw5QQOenJnQ, mR0HMssJek8, T4849hhdwTo].
- Short or both sides: reversal patterns such as outside bars [_AiSzhs67J4] and kickers [QWcb7yxYAxk]. He explicitly says it applies to long or short signals [uFFAAZNSozg].

**Numbers**
- About 3–5 peers breaking out of consolidation in the same period. His example is private banks: Yes Bank, IndusInd, Kotak, HDFC Bank [Bqp9jKQCs30].
- For investing: the top three sector stocks should share structure and direction. His example is HDFC Bank, ICICI and Kotak [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].

**Negative use**
- Skip a stock that looks fine alone if most peers do not confirm. His example: he would have avoided SBI in 2009–10 because most PSU banks were not confirming [2Yv0E57_bs4].

**Related ideas**
- **Sequential breakouts:** stocks in a sector often break out in sequence and stay valid for the next cycle, giving multiple entry chances. His examples are metal names [mim7ylyaN74].
- **Early leaders:** a stock breaking out before its sector index is treated as a leader. His example is Axis Bank ahead of Bank Nifty [1jsLESmVLVU].
- **Business groups:** he applies the idea to corporate groups too (Kirloskar, Mahindra) [uw5QQOenJnQ].
- **Sector support is not a guarantee:** he notes that sector support did not save two trades [uw5QQOenJnQ].

**How taught:** frequent in 2016–2018 videos, still used recently. Medium-to-high clarity, but N (peer count) and the time window are rarely fixed. Only [Bqp9jKQCs30] gives 3–5.

### 2.5 Picking stocks inside a strong sector

**Mirror the sector chart**
- Pick only the handful of stocks that are above a rising 50 DMA with a rising RS line [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
- Or stocks at the 50 DMA, forming a pattern, and turning up [SbtFCxbIB1w].
- Or stocks above the 30-week MA with rising RS [lRUDp336l7Q].

**Smaller beats larger (recent teaching)**
- Prefer smaller, faster-growing names over sector heavyweights [0nbQs92kYuY, c-0bHPew1fg, kwbfXjRtEkk].
- Market-cap bands he names:
  - 3,000–30,000 crore, with autos as his example [yEKNcJ4EH0U];
  - 10,000–35,000 crore as a "sweet spot" while large caps are sold [r63sarc_qV8].
- His example: the private bank index rose about 20% even though marquee banks did not, because smaller banks surged [kwbfXjRtEkk].
- Mix both: small names for alpha, large caps for stability [DCG1GO9TGFU].

**Change over time**
- Five years ago he picked one stock per sector from a large-cap list, choosing the RS leader, and stated a preference for large caps [dV4MiNaAenI]. This shifted toward smaller names.

**Custom indices vs exchange indices (a reversal)**
- Eight years ago: prefer exchange-defined sector indices, because custom baskets let weaker companies in [488FDla8kuc].
- Recently: he builds custom or equal-weight indices on TradingView where no official index exists, or where heavyweights distort the parent. Examples are insurance, rare earth, two-wheelers and hospitals [lRUDp336l7Q, DCG1GO9TGFU, rbSj4pOg3_I, 0Hnffbj5pgE].
- He gives two-wheelers vs four-wheelers as the standard case: split autos into equal-weight sub-indices and favour the group strong on both weekly and daily RS [rbSj4pOg3_I, lRUDp336l7Q].

### 2.6 Leadership through corrections and recoveries

**Pullback or correction?**
- Track only the current leaders. If they crack, turn conservative and raise cash [Qu49zHwEGwo].
- If banks, metals and autos are all in good structure, broad indices "rarely crack," though small caps and themes can still fail [Oq4FjWEDbd0].

**Next leaders = groups that fall least**
- Sectors with the smallest drawdown in a correction are the likeliest next leaders [dERi8MUU5es, V41BUpPK3E4, s1ZCPQI5894, NtpyZH5cfaE].
- This holds even if those sectors are still making lower highs [V41BUpPK3E4].
- So do sectors whose RS holds while headlines are bad [g3mcqf6rqoc, SbtFCxbIB1w].
- Variants:
  - Laggards that did not blow off in 2022–24 hold up best [iWa7MbGawvU].
  - Three phases: correction (crowded, richly valued themes fall most), consolidation, then leadership (resilient groups break out first) [Pm_1kb8Fku0].
  - In a falling market, a sector that bases, contracts and breaks out continues. His example is CPSE, up about 15–20% off its low while the market fell [NtpyZH5cfaE].

**Counterpoint**
- In the *first leg* of a V-shaped recovery, the most beaten-down sectors rebound hardest (Bank Nifty, autos, PSU banks), while those that held up lag [-qQntPmfe-s].
- This is a short-window exception that is not reconciled with the "fell least leads" rule.

**Confirmation steps after a low** [B1ynZNvqesE]
1. High-RS sectors should also lead the rebound between day 4 and day 15.
2. Between day 10 and day 15, leaders should consolidate on lower volume, with no wide-range high-volume bearish bars. Sector volume = the sum of constituent volume.
3. Compare 10–15 day ATR across groups. Clarity is low on what to expect.
- Leaders holding above the 50 and 200 DMA after a big run are "unlikely to retest lows" [B1ynZNvqesE].
- He also wants leaders to rise on strong volume and show strong quarterly earnings [ywccnKeWXNo].
- A rally is sustainable only if new leaders hold their structure while the index consolidates [ywccnKeWXNo].

**A leader's pause is not the end**
- Leaders retrace to shake out short-term traders, then set up again [Jpu536_AmGc].
- Former leaders consolidating above long-term averages are not "weak." His examples are metal, PSU banks and power [S--7ta4FmbY, DU5Y5AsCMZM].
- Within a leading group, sub-sectors rotate [N2dUTcb9CzQ].

**Rally quality**
- He prefers rallies led by cyclicals and financials (metals, autos, banks, capital markets, defense) over defensives (IT, pharma, durables) [-qQntPmfe-s].
- Leaders must be backed by "second-best" groups for a healthy market [g3mcqf6rqoc].
- High-beta sectors set the tone, so he weights them more [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- At tops, leaders stall first while rotation goes into new names [dERi8MUU5es].

### 2.7 Crowding, consensus, and the momentum-vs-risk-reward choice

**Do not chase**
- Do not chase a vertical, headline sector. His example is realty up about 50% in 2 weeks [S--7ta4FmbY].
- Do not chase euphoric defense or capital markets; buy after pullbacks [fFUPLUEhzVo, p5O_uspLu9k].
- Consensus leaders drift sideways [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- Look for what can move next, not what already has [lRUDp336l7Q].

**Contrarian tilt**
- Alpha comes from low-attention pockets [6qExRlD11sE].
- Buy sectors out of favour while their structure is valid [rxqOUxO_vP4].
- For investing, begin accumulating when brokers are negative, mutual funds are not buying, and delivery volume is low. His example is banks: bullish near 32,000, negative near 16,000–17,000 [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].
- Exponential, leveraged one-sided trades reverse sharply [SNrzU-z3RkE, butE8gDPXxw].

**Pick one style**
- "Momentum" sectors have strong structure but poor risk-reward. "Risk-reward" sectors have pulled back to or below the 50 DMA. A trader must pick one [B8HZvUkY5sA].
- Same split elsewhere:
  - continuation themes have only about 1:1–1:1.5 reward-to-risk [q26J58h5Qt8];
  - momentum vs value pockets [0Hnffbj5pgE];
  - he declined to add to momentum names whose trending pivot sat 20–25% or more below price [Y4TewQHVhvc].

**Valuation grid**
- He sorts sectors and stocks into four boxes: over- or under-valued × out- or under-performer [BhQ_YnaRqa0].
- In a roughly 15-day correction (broad index about −5%), the boxes fell:

| Box | Fall |
|---|---|
| Overvalued outperformers | −25% to −35% |
| Undervalued outperformers | −15% to −25% |
| Overvalued underperformers | −15% to −20% |
| Undervalued underperformers | −5% to −7% |

[Pm_1kb8Fku0]

**Tension:** "strength gets stronger" (2.2) sits uneasily with "avoid consensus or crowded leaders" here. He does not give a rule for when a leader becomes "crowded."

### 2.8 Bank Nifty as the lead sector, and rotation sequences

**The claim**
- Bank Nifty is to India what the Nasdaq is to the US, because of foreign holdings, derivatives participation and sentiment weight [c-0bHPew1fg].
- Bank Nifty above its 200 DMA pulls the market into the leading quadrant [c-0bHPew1fg].
- Its January breakdown preceded the correction [c-0bHPew1fg].
- Weekly gaps down in Bank Nifty are "never a good sign" [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- If banks fall, Nifty cannot hold. He cites a bullish engulfing candle at the 50 DMA as a stabilisation sign [d3-OMTGOGx8].
- After a weekly Bank Nifty gain of about 3.5–4%, he expects 4–5 weeks of strong market momentum [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- A Bank Nifty trend should improve breadth [YFsvQXZZt1g].

**Rotation sequences**
- Banks (mainly private banks) strengthen first, then realty, autos, auto components, housing and consumption follow, then infrastructure [S--7ta4FmbY, lRUDp336l7Q].
- Rate cuts with government and central bank aligned: BFSI, capital markets and IPOs break out first, then real estate and autos, then metals [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- Fiscal and monetary expansion helps realty, autos and consumption, and ultimately flows to financials [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
- The bank payoff lags the policy data [L-PGsMh2vHw].
- Banks and autos doing well hints the economy is fine [mim7ylyaN74].

**Contradictions**
- One month ago he said Bank Nifty *used to* set the tone before about 2020, and that underperforming heavyweight private banks have removed that effect [CFJNeCPMQ1o].
- He also notes the Bank Nifty rise was PSU-led while large private banks were flat for 2–3 years [6GqBBVt6fUs].

### 2.9 Size segments, flows, and new-age pockets

**Small/mid vs large, by regime**
- Avoid small, mid and micro caps when money is scarce. Stay in mid-to-large caps with sound balance sheets [hwOw2HaLUcQ].
- Capital rotates into large caps during corrections. Prior small/mid momentum leaders fell about 18–27% [Y4TewQHVhvc].
- Small caps fall 5–8% in single days in corrections [Yxo-rYOCuAo].
- The reverse case: when FIIs sell and DIIs buy, small/mid/micro caps are supported while large caps lag [0nbQs92kYuY, NtpyZH5cfaE, sIo5XQqZ0b0].

**Old economy vs new age**
- Nifty 50 heavyweights grow in single digits. Nifty Next 50 or Next 100 and the IPO index have better structure [SuYHqw5zA8U, CFJNeCPMQ1o].
- Recent leadership is narrow: new-age listings and themes such as data centers [rhiXs5Kh_0g, sIo5XQqZ0b0].
- IPO stocks (low ownership, small float) do well near market bottoms [S--7ta4FmbY].

**Foreign money enters large caps first**
- Track the Nifty 100, Nifty 200 and an FPI-holdings index of the top 150 stocks as a gauge of FII return [6GqBBVt6fUs].

### 2.10 Themes and policy

- Prefer pockets where the government spends: funding, regulation, project visibility [Jpu536_AmGc].
- Decade themes [Jpu536_AmGc, zWYCYySQVNc]:
  - infrastructure and capital goods in 2000–10;
  - consumption in 2010–15;
  - defense, railways and IT after 2020.
- His year-by-year favoured groups to 2018 [zWYCYySQVNc].
- **Peter Lynch-style test:** government support + private capex + export orientation = a multi-year sweet spot. Applied at length to defense, with budget, R&D and export figures [p5O_uspLu9k].
- **Long-horizon views:**
  - defense for 10–15 years [yYwiSDVwgyA];
  - defense, capital markets, hospitals and insurance for 5–7 years [YVGWKmpHStM];
  - financialization as a secular, not cyclical, theme [azzUmy2l_0U].
- **Other inputs:** private-equity entry as a signal [rbSj4pOg3_I]; indirect AI plays [NtpyZH5cfaE].
- **Supply drain:** large IPO or follow-on supply drains liquidity from existing stocks [SNrzU-z3RkE, x2RbjJp1eCI]. An IPO can be a sector-top "final nail," though he defers to the chart (Hyundai/autos) [q26J58h5Qt8].
- **Cyclical vs secular:** these are mostly judgment calls, not rules.

### 2.11 The US as "mother market"

**Lead indicators**
- The S&P 500, Dow and DAX lead India. A correction there means mid and small caps fall again [e34G88-BKI4].
- Weekly check: the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Nikkei vs their 50-day averages. With two below, he is cautious on India [B8HZvUkY5sA].
- When the S&P 500 falls with momentum, "9 out of 10 times" global markets follow, so protect capital [JJEB-UZgK4E].

**Speed matters more than size**
- A gradual US fall can be absorbed; a fast one spreads to almost every market:

| Scenario | Size and speed | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gradual | 10–15% | Absorbed by other markets | [hwOw2HaLUcQ] |
| Gradual | About 20% over 24–36 months | Absorbed, as in 2000–03 | [LWoEC9OjajE] |
| Fast | 10–20% in a couple of months | Spreads to nearly all markets within about 2 weeks | [hwOw2HaLUcQ] |
| Rapid (unspecified) | Not quantified | Takes down every market | [lRUDp336l7Q, 6qExRlD11sE] |

- No decoupling. If the US consolidates for 6 months, India cannot keep rising [r11n7ztY2YY, Qu49zHwEGwo].

**Other US signals**
- Leader divergence: the S&P 500 at a new high while the Nasdaq lags is an early warning [8QeR8FmYYg4].
- Narrow US gains (a few AI mega-caps) are fragile [LWoEC9OjajE, butE8gDPXxw, r11n7ztY2YY].
- Leverage amplifies unwinds [butE8gDPXxw]. He also discusses the yen carry trade [B8HZvUkY5sA].
- **US–China asymmetry for India:**
  - strong US + strong China = excellent;
  - strong US + weak China = good;
  - both weak = terrible [hwOw2HaLUcQ].

**Shifting views**
- A US correction could *help* India later by redirecting flows [iWa7MbGawvU, butE8gDPXxw].
- His educated guess: India falls 4–5% if the US falls 10% [butE8gDPXxw].
- Recently he says India takes "limited cues" from the US [YFsvQXZZt1g], which contrasts with the older "cannot decouple."
- He frames a US accident as the main 2026 risk [6qExRlD11sE].

### 2.12 Relative cycles: India vs the world, EM, and China

**India vs S&P 500**
- The Nifty 500 ÷ S&P 500 ratio alternates between underperformance and outperformance legs of about 4–12 months, visible on charts back to 1995/2000 [YVGWKmpHStM].
- His experience: underperformance legs last about 6–7 months; outperformance legs about 8–12 months.
- Cycle timing cannot be predicted [YVGWKmpHStM].
- Positive policy news fails during an underperformance leg; the market sells off again within a day [YVGWKmpHStM].

**India vs China**
- China-over-India episodes last about 12–18 months. He dated the current one from August–September 2024 [iWa7MbGawvU].
- A later view: the China catch-up has largely played out [YVGWKmpHStM].
- Hang Seng sectors serve as a China proxy, and China recovery spills over into Indian metals [iWa7MbGawvU].

**India vs EM: cyclical rank and weights**
- India's rank among EM is cyclical. About 85–90% of peers beat India in 2025–26, similar to 2011–13 [SNrzU-z3RkE].
- India's EM weight:
  - about 20–24% in late 2024, falling to about 11–12% [SNrzU-z3RkE];
  - about 8% in another video [8QeR8FmYYg4]. The figures are inconsistent.
- Country weights rotate by theme: Brazil 4%→16%→3%; China 17%→43%→19%; Taiwan is essentially TSMC at about 14% of the index [dpsU7JvHiMA].
- The worst EM of the last 2 years can become the best. His example is the KOSPI [DU5Y5AsCMZM].

**Persistence vs mean reversion**
- Opposing view: the weakest-RS market keeps getting weaker [8QeR8FmYYg4, sIo5XQqZ0b0, rhiXs5Kh_0g].
- India below its 50/200 DMA while EM glides back to its 50 DMA marks persistent underperformance [sIo5XQqZ0b0, s1ZCPQI5894].
- The EM index holding its 50 DMA helps India [jcWYGNInYg8].
- He does not reconcile this persistence view with the cyclical-rank view above.

**Other relative signals**
- Ex-US world beat the US from early 2025, which he reads as diversification, not decoupling [Qu49zHwEGwo, YVGWKmpHStM].
- MSCI World and EM beat the Nifty 500 since the September 2024 top, putting India in a lagging cycle [Oq4FjWEDbd0].
- Under-owned markets are safer when crowded trades unwind [SNrzU-z3RkE].

### 2.13 Dollar index and rupee

**Direction**
- A rising dollar index hurts EM and India, and also commodities and global equities [jcWYGNInYg8, bhTEw6wD_CU, zHzq3uAFJFs, V41BUpPK3E4, M4Z-Gi4gGag].
- A falling dollar plus a stable US 10-year yield is good for EM [bhTEw6wD_CU].
- Dollar and yields rising together is a tendency to struggle, not a rule (citing John Murphy) [bhTEw6wD_CU].

**Levels (these change by episode, so the stable idea is "base breakout / range top," not the number)**

| Level or range | Reading | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 95–105 | Expected range; fine for EM | [hwOw2HaLUcQ] |
| Below about 110 for 2–3 weekly closes | Relief rally odds rise | [zHzq3uAFJFs] |
| 120–121 | New Indian low | [zHzq3uAFJFs] |
| Below 105–103 | New EM bull cycle | [zHzq3uAFJFs] |
| 99–105/106 for 2–3 years | Supportive for the market | [V41BUpPK3E4] |
| Top of 100–107 range | Pressure on EM; a pullback gives a short EM rally | [JcxWhCO0hqI] |
| About 100→110 (+10%) | Linked to EM weakness, after an earlier about 90→114 move | [M4Z-Gi4gGag] |
| +14–15% after the Trump election | EM struggles | [s1ZCPQI5894] |

### 2.14 Crude oil

**Crude as the crisis barometer**
- During a geopolitical crisis, watch the Brent chart instead of the news [Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA, KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- Only a sharp reversal *holding* below about 99 means something positive for equities [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- Escalation = breaking successive pivots at about 88, 92, 93, 96 and 99. De-escalation = a long-wick rejection after a large candle [yYwiSDVwgyA].
- He uses a 50-day MA on crude [B8HZvUkY5sA, sIo5XQqZ0b0].

**Thresholds**
- About 100 is the sentiment line [KUlNy1_Rk9M, YFsvQXZZt1g, NtpyZH5cfaE].
- The Indian market started reacting above about 97–98 [r63sarc_qV8].
- Further falls if crude reaches 116 or more, with 120–130 as a higher risk zone [r63sarc_qV8].
- Crude sustained above 100 hurts importers (India, Korea, Japan, Taiwan) more than the US [-qQntPmfe-s, Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA].
- Acceptable ranges:
  - 80–100 [SuYHqw5zA8U];
  - 75–95 or 80–90 [YFsvQXZZt1g];
  - an expected 80–90/100 range [rhiXs5Kh_0g].

**Mechanism and behaviour**
- High crude brings cost-push inflation. Rate cuts get harder, which weighs on banks and rate-sensitive sectors [Sb6KTt64YKE, NtpyZH5cfaE, yYwiSDVwgyA].
- A short shock is followed by a quick rebound; a prolonged one forces a reprice [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- Crude tends to make about 100% moves after a multi-year base triggers [yYwiSDVwgyA].
- Spikes like 88→100+→88 in a week should not alarm [DU5Y5AsCMZM].

**Sensitivity changes over time**
- In the earlier crude rally (about 64→108–116), Nifty fell about 26,000→22,800. In the later one (about 70→108), it fell about 24,600→23,400. He concludes the market gives crude less weight now [r63sarc_qV8].

**He does not trade crude:** weekend news gaps can be 8–15% [jcWYGNInYg8].

## 3. How a program would test it

**Common data needed**
- Daily OHLCV for NSE sector and theme indices (and sector ETFs).
- Nifty 500 and Nifty 50.
- Constituent lists with industry tags, **point-in-time** to avoid survivorship.
- Bank Nifty.
- DXY, Brent, USDINR, US 10-year and 30-year yields.
- S&P 500, Nasdaq, Nikkei, MSCI EM and MSCI World.
- NSDL FII and AMFI/NSE DII flow series.

**T1. Sector RS ranking.**
- Every 2 weeks, rank sector indices by the slope or new-high status of (sector ÷ Nifty 500) over 6–12 months.
- Hold the top 2–3, filtered to price above a rising 50 DMA with a higher-low structure.
- Compare the next 1, 3 and 6-month excess return vs the Nifty 500 and vs the bottom-ranked sectors.
- Also compare 2–3 week vs 6–7 month ranking windows, per [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- Sources: [BxY2ofigO8I, LWoEC9OjajE, Oq4FjWEDbd0, DCG1GO9TGFU, JJEB-UZgK4E].

**T2. Sector breadth.**
- Monthly, compute the % of constituents above the 200 DMA (and the 30-week MA).
- Test top vs bottom tercile forward 1–3 month returns.
- Test the event "rises above 75% after being below 25% within the prior 3 months," with a 3–6 month forward return.
- Sources: [WI0tdUjH_Vg, 0nbQs92kYuY, DU5Y5AsCMZM].

**T3. Sister-stock confirmation.**
- Define a base breakout (e.g., close above a 40–60 day range high on volume above the 50-day average).
- Compare the forward 20/60-day return and hit rate of breakouts where at least 3 same-industry peers broke out within ±15 trading days, vs isolated breakouts.
- Repeat for short signals (breakdowns).
- Sources: [Bqp9jKQCs30, uFFAAZNSozg, jlpd-YpqwiQ, VET4JHrc2vU].

**T4. Fall-least leads.**
- For each Nifty 500 drawdown of 10% or more, rank sectors by peak-to-trough drawdown.
- Test whether the smallest-drawdown tercile beats the largest over 3–6 months after the trough.
- Also test the first 20–40 days, where [-qQntPmfe-s] predicts the opposite.
- Add the [B1ynZNvqesE] confirmation: the sector also leads days 4–15 off the low.
- Sources: [dERi8MUU5es, V41BUpPK3E4, s1ZCPQI5894, Pm_1kb8Fku0].

**T5. Bank Nifty as lead.**
- Events:
  - a weekly Bank Nifty gain of 3.5% or more;
  - Bank Nifty crossing above or below its 200 DMA.
- Measure the next 4–5 week (and 1–3 month) returns of Nifty 500, midcap and smallcap indices, and % above the 50 DMA, vs the unconditional average.
- Split pre- and post-2020 to test [CFJNeCPMQ1o]'s claim that the effect faded.
- Sources: [6K1LOJqZYEg, c-0bHPew1fg, YFsvQXZZt1g].

**T6. Intermarket regime filters.**
- Classify each week by three conditions:
  - DXY above or below its 50/200 DMA and breaking a 52-week high;
  - Brent above or below 100 (and above or below its 50 DMA);
  - S&P 500 and Nikkei both below their 50 DMA.
- Compare Nifty 500 forward 1–3 month returns and drawdowns across regimes.
- Also test Nifty's reaction within 10 trading days after fast (≤2 months) vs slow S&P drawdowns of 10–20%.
- Sources: [bhTEw6wD_CU, zHzq3uAFJFs, Sb6KTt64YKE, B8HZvUkY5sA, hwOw2HaLUcQ, JJEB-UZgK4E].

**T7. Relative-cycle durations.**
- On weekly Nifty 500 ÷ S&P 500 (in USD and INR) since 1995, detect relative-trend legs, e.g., by swing pivots of at least 8%.
- Check the leg-length distribution against 4–12 months, and whether outperformance legs (claimed 8–12 months) outlast underperformance legs (claimed 6–7 months).
- Repeat vs MSCI EM and vs China.
- Sources: [YVGWKmpHStM, iWa7MbGawvU].

**T8. FII sensitivity.**
- Regress monthly Nifty returns on FII net flow scaled by FII holdings, separately for 2006–2014 and 2016 onward.
- Test whether months with DII buying greater than FII selling have better 1–3 month forward returns.
- Sources: [YVGWKmpHStM, dpsU7JvHiMA, sIo5XQqZ0b0].

## 5. Gaps and doubts

**Thresholds rarely fixed**
- "Higher high on the RS line," "rising 50 DMA," "base," and "pivot" are never parameterised. Neither are the lookback for RS slope, the number of peers or the window for sister stocks (except 3–5 in [Bqp9jKQCs30]), or the definition of a "crowded" sector.
- His RRG settings are not stated [c-0bHPew1fg].
- Details of his sector-strength method are in paid material [sbHzNd2iA8k].

**Episode-specific levels**
- Dollar-index lines range across 95–121 depending on the video. Crude lines move between 97 and 116. Rupee "return zones" also move.
- Treat these as one-off chart reads, not stable parameters.
- India's EM weight is quoted inconsistently: about 11–12% in [SNrzU-z3RkE] vs about 8% in [8QeR8FmYYg4].

**Contradictions to resolve by testing**
- "Strong gets stronger / weak gets weaker" vs "avoid consensus, buy out-of-favour" vs "cyclical rank reversal" (worst EM becomes best).
- "Fell least leads" vs "most beaten-down rebounds hardest in the first leg" [-qQntPmfe-s].
- Bank Nifty as the market's engine [c-0bHPew1fg, 6K1LOJqZYEg] vs it lost that role after 2020 [CFJNeCPMQ1o].
- India cannot decouple from the US [r11n7ztY2YY] vs India takes limited US cues [YFsvQXZZt1g] vs India falls about half of a US fall [butE8gDPXxw].
- Domestic money now absorbs FII selling vs momentum still needs FII money.
- Macro data have no timing value [hKnYYRpd63s] vs heavy use of macro inputs elsewhere.
- Prefer exchange-defined indices (8 years ago) vs build custom ones (recent).
- One large-cap per sector (5 years ago) vs prefer smaller names (recent).

**Unclear captions**
- Portfolio weights summing to more than 100% [YLAIkbRSnsA].
- Garbled sector or stock lists [BhQ_YnaRqa0].
- "CAS closing" [YFsvQXZZt1g].
- Unverified third-party figures: carry-trade size, AI share of US gains, Bloomberg survey percentages, X reports of pre-news shorting.

**Not covered**
- No explicit exit rule for leaving a sector, beyond "leaders crack → raise cash" and the 50 DMA.
- No position sizing across sectors beyond 85–90% in chosen pockets.
- No transaction-cost or liquidity treatment for thin Indian sector ETFs.
- No systematic short-side sector rotation; shorts appear only in top-down checks and sister-stock confirmation.
- No backtest evidence of his own for any of these claims. The track-record anecdotes he gives are self-reported: five of six pockets worked [p5O_uspLu9k]; Hang Seng Tech +30% [iWa7MbGawvU].
