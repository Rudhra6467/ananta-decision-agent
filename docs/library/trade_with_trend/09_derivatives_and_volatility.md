# Derivatives and volatility

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits

Raunak's method is built on positional trading of cash equities, plus some index futures. In that method, derivatives are mostly something to stay away from and volatility is something to read. For the retail trader he repeats one stance across about eight years of videos: avoid futures and options, especially with a small account, as a beginner, or in short-term and expiry trading. Option writing is acceptable only for experienced, well-capitalised traders. He rejects open interest, the option chain and the put-call ratio as signals of direction or of a bottom. Volatility is a different matter. He uses India VIX actively in three ways:
- as a regime gauge, deciding when to worry and how big positions should be;
- as a marker for where a market bottom may form;
- as a filter for intraday setups (opening range breakout, initial balance) and for choosing between defensive and high-beta sectors.

Separately, he wants price volatility to fall as a new uptrend begins. Everything below is his claim or method. None of it is established fact; treat each item as a hypothesis to test.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Retail should avoid F&O; equities compound, F&O does not

**What he says**
- He estimates that about 90% of beginners in F&O blow up their accounts or suffer psychological damage. He says the ecosystem pushes retail toward F&O for others' benefit: brokers, advisers, weekly expiries, scalping [eeKRfAbbvZA, mim7ylyaN74, 9dbnXEEHgsc].
- He frames F&O as an opportunity cost. While retail sold options and straddles, Nifty went from under 19,000 to about 21,400 (Oct–Dec 2023). He says taxes and losses compound in F&O, while money and experience compound in equities [Lw6h6T30VvE].
- Short-horizon option buyers and writers, working over days, fight time and volatility. Longer-horizon investors make time and volatility work for them [-qQntPmfe-s].
- In news-driven markets with gaps on policy statements, short-term F&O players take heavy losses. Systematic dip-buyers benefit [KDSbZ0oW2U0].
- Impatient traders chase option swings such as a premium going from 20 to 100 and back in a day, and lose capital [uw5QQOenJnQ].

**Conditions where he says to avoid F&O entirely**
- Small account. He calls small-account options trading a trap. To grow a small account, he says ignore open interest and option-chain analysis completely [arFNgAPisHU, sbHzNd2iA8k].
- Beginner [arFNgAPisHU, eeKRfAbbvZA].
- Volatile, thin markets. His example: Nifty fell 250 points in 15 minutes and expiry-day puts rose several-fold. Here he says avoid intraday, options and futures; position traders can simply sit through it [N5bb0Cu8RAs].
- He says he has repeated for over a decade: avoid F&O unless you can manage risk and give screen time. His community carries no short-term or F&O content [kwbfXjRtEkk].

**How often and how clearly:** This is his most repeated theme in the set. It appears in at least 11 videos, from 8 years ago to 1 month ago, mostly stated with high conviction.

**Nuances and tensions in his position**
- **Futures.** He is "not against futures." He trades index futures with leverage himself and teaches equities plus index futures [KRoie4cj5TQ]. Elsewhere he tells beginners and small accounts to stay away from futures as well [arFNgAPisHU, 9dbnXEEHgsc].
- **Outlook for F&O traders.** In one recent video (5 months ago) he expects responsible F&O traders working within risk limits to do well over the next 24 months [-qQntPmfe-s]. That is softer than his usual stance.
- **Ranking of options.** In one video he ranks options last because of their complexity [zWYCYySQVNc]. In another he calls options the most rewarding instrument, but also one of the trickiest [9dbnXEEHgsc]. Both are 8 years old.
- **A benefit for cash traders.** He also says F&O-driven gap-downs followed by gap-ups shake out weak hands. For cash-equity traders this creates pullbacks to add to winners [Y4TewQHVhvc].

### 2.2 Instrument mechanics: why he prefers cash stocks

- **Fixed lot sizes.** You cannot scale in or out in small amounts. You may be stuck with a large position while price falls below entry. For his scaling strategy he specifies cash stocks only [2Yv0E57_bs4, zWYCYySQVNc].
- **Options need two things to go your way.** Time decay and volatility must both work for you, or the option can expire worthless. Gap moves in options are hard to manage [zWYCYySQVNc].
- **Buying versus writing.** Buying an option caps the loss at the premium. Futures need care. Naked writing risks large sums for small rewards; his example is risking ₹50,000 to make ₹20,000. He says backtests show single days where writing wipes out months of profit [KRoie4cj5TQ].

### 2.3 Option writing / selling for income

**What he says**
- Selling is a valid income approach, and he notes most options expire with the buyer losing the premium [6ZKLrl0z8jY].
- It is only appropriate after about **5+ years of profitable trading** with good capital protection [6ZKLrl0z8jY].
- It also needs a large account, tested position sizing and entry methods, strong risk management, and good reading of support and resistance for strike selection [6ZKLrl0z8jY, arFNgAPisHU].
- Few people succeed at it. Anyone who thrives at it early is lucky [arFNgAPisHU, 6ZKLrl0z8jY].
- Recent margin rules made selling harder [6ZKLrl0z8jY].

**Counter-argument he also gives:** the tail risk of naked writing, described in 2.2 [KRoie4cj5TQ].

**What is missing:** No strike-selection rule, distance from spot, spread structure, stop rule or sizing is given. Spreads and straddles are not taught at all. Straddle selling appears only as something retail did while missing an equity rally [Lw6h6T30VvE].

### 2.4 Open interest, option chain and PCR: not signals

**The rule:** Do not mix price-action trend analysis with futures or options open-interest data to predict direction. Price decides; open interest does not [kkJ4m9JQ4pM].

**Why he says it fails for trend trading**
- For 4–6 month trend views, option data is useless.
- It tends to show "resistance" overhead on every dip, which causes early exits [kkJ4m9JQ4pM].

**His evidence:** In Oct–Nov 2020, option-chain resistance was widely quoted at 11,300, 12,000, 12,500 and 13,000 on Nifty. The index kept rising to about 14,500 [kkJ4m9JQ4pM].

**Related dismissals**
- Open-interest expansion and aggressive put writing, as cited by breakout chasers, are hot-stock noise to him [mim7ylyaN74].
- PCR and oversold readings (RSI, stochastic) are not bottom signals during a correction, because markets stay oversold for long periods. He cites O'Neil's 2000 example. He says do not bottom-fish on claims like RSI at 18 or stochastic below 8 [hKnYYRpd63s].

**Clarity:** High. The theme appears in 4 videos and is consistent over time.

### 2.5 India VIX as a regime gauge

**Current bands (recent videos)**

| India VIX | His reading |
|---|---|
| about 9 to 13–14 | Acceptable, fine for markets [SbtFCxbIB1w] |
| above about 16–18 | Concern begins [SbtFCxbIB1w] |
| 9.2–9.4 (very low) | Not a reason for complacency; random pullbacks can still happen, keep sizes measured [SbtFCxbIB1w] |

**A low level by itself is not a warning.** With VIX at 10.45, inside a recent 10.4–11.9 range, he said VIX needs a catalyst to rise; his examples were a tariff announcement and Iran–US conflict. Worry starts only when the *range* shifts up, for example from 10–12 to roughly 14–18 [kwbfXjRtEkk].
- He partly attributes low VIX to thin derivative positioning, and to the market being held up by SIP/STP inflows. He also cited a 75–90 range that holds, presumably crude oil; the captions are unclear [kwbfXjRtEkk].

**A renewed spike after a recovery means more trouble.**
- In one video he warned that volatility would likely expand. He listed catalysts for the next quarter: an expected September Fed cut, the US election and the debates.
- He said not to believe the "back to normal" talk [B8HZvUkY5sA].

**He treats VIX fear-mongering itself as noise** [SbtFCxbIB1w].

**How his thresholds changed over time**

| Video | Approx. age | "Low" | "High / worry" |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4kP0_EuS_EY | 13 years | 12–14 (sleepy) | above 17–18 |
| hk4d8AxbQ3A | 6 years | — | above 18, and he cites 20 |
| SbtFCxbIB1w | 8 months | 9–14 (okay) | above 16–18 |
| kwbfXjRtEkk | 1 month | 10–12 | a range shift to 14–18 |

The "worry" level has drifted down by roughly 2–4 points. This may simply reflect lower baseline India VIX in recent years. He does not say so explicitly.

### 2.6 VIX spikes for locating bottoms (long side)

- **Recent peak zone.** India VIX has tended to peak at **28–32** over the last 2–3 years. A peak there followed by a decline is a positive sign [KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- **Episode one: final bottom near 32–33.** With VIX at about 23, he expected a final bottom could come near **32–33**, the Russia–Ukraine peak level. He also cited a prior peak of 28–30. The bottom might not come at once; it might follow a V-shaped bounce to the 50- and 200-day averages first [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- **Episode two: extremes not yet seen.** With VIX at about 21, he said extreme volatility had not yet appeared. For comparison he gave peaks of about 68–72 in 2008 and 80–85 in Covid. Short-term traders should be careful because the eventual peak is unknown. He favours deploying into ETFs when volatility spikes [yYwiSDVwgyA].
- **2013 bottom.** In 2011–13, VIX cycled between spikes and crashes. The market bottomed in August 2013 with VIX at about **35–36** [s1ZCPQI5894].
- **Druckenmiller citation.** He cites Druckenmiller watching VIX spikes, as marks of extreme fear, to find turning points [ywccnKeWXNo].

**What is missing:** He gives no entry rule. There is no threshold-cross trigger, no confirmation step and no sizing. The candidate "bottom zones" also differ by episode: 28–32, 32–33, 35–36, and Covid-scale 80–85. There is a tension between these and his rule against bottom-fishing on oversold readings (2.4). He does not reconcile treating a VIX spike as a bottom marker with rejecting RSI or PCR extremes.

### 2.7 VIX as a filter for intraday setups

**Opening range breakout (ORB)**
- ORB works best when VIX is high: above about 18, and he cites above 20 as the condition for it to perform for months. VIX was about 25 at the time.
- Avoid ORB when volatility is low.
- In other markets, check the local volatility index [hk4d8AxbQ3A].

**Initial balance, a market-profile concept (13 years old)**
- Wide initial balance with sleepy VIX (12–14): stay out.
- Wide initial balance with VIX above about 17–18: pay attention, because the initial balance is more likely to be breached later in the day.
- In that case prefer high-beta names over low-beta ones. His high-beta examples were JP Associates, SBI and ICICI Bank; his low-beta example was ITC [4kP0_EuS_EY].
- His supporting episode: in Sep–Dec 2012, Nifty was stuck around 5,900–6,100 with falling VIX and many non-trend days. Trader-friendly structures only appeared once VIX rose [4kP0_EuS_EY].

These are older teachings. They sit uneasily with his later "no intraday / no short-term" stance (2.1).

### 2.8 VIX and sector choice

- **When VIX is rising or spiking:** defensive pockets do well over time.
- **When VIX stays in a tight range without frequent spikes:** high-beta sectors do well [s1ZCPQI5894].

This is taught once. Which sectors count as "defensive" or "high-beta" is not specified in the notes.

### 2.9 Price volatility inside a new uptrend (long side)

**Guideline 3, which he calls non-negotiable**
- As price rises, volatility must fall.
- If price is up 5–6% and volatility has not dipped, or has stayed flat, be cautious [tTdy5srpW9Q].

**Over the trend's life**
- Falling volatility as price rises suggests a long-lasting trend.
- As the trend matures and volatility rises with deeper retracements, book profits.
- After a prolonged rise in volatility plus some shift in the economy or market, look for a trend change [tTdy5srpW9Q].

**Unclear:** Which volatility measure he means is not named in the captions. It could be ATR, range or something else; it is probably stock-level, not VIX. This comes from one 7-year-old video, and that part of the notes is rated low clarity.

### 2.10 Hedging and the short side

- **He does not believe in hedging with options.** Occasionally he keeps large-cap longs and shorts Nifty futures, so a sharp fall does limited damage [U7YrYjIS9B8]. No sizing or hedge ratio is given.
- **Weak phases.** To satisfy the urge to be active in a weak phase, one can short sell or buy puts while keeping a long-term bullish mindset [M4Z-Gi4gGag]. Clarity is low and no rules are given.

### 2.11 Indian market-structure observations

- **Derivatives-to-cash volume ratio.** He cites India in the hundreds (the captions suggest about 420, uncertain), Germany in the 30s, the US about 9 and Japan about 2. He reads this as speculative excess [Lw6h6T30VvE]. Elsewhere he says F&O volume is several hundred times cash volume; the exact multiple is unclear [Y4TewQHVhvc].
- **NSE closing auction (live since 3 August) — expiry distortion.** He says it distorts expiry days. Far-out-of-the-money puts priced at a few rupees jumped to hundreds in minutes, and Sensex fell about 1,500 points in minutes on expiry. He believes the system is being gamed, is hurting sentiment, and may push option writers out [kwbfXjRtEkk].
- **NSE closing auction — chart distortion.** Because of the new CAS system, Nifty and Bank Nifty candles look distorted on lower timeframes. He uses the futures charts for these two on daily and weekly timeframes and prefers the weekly. He expects 2–3 months for the system to stabilise [SuYHqw5zA8U].

## 3. How a program would test it

1. **VIX regime buckets.** Bucket each day by India VIX: below 14, 14–16, 16–18, above 18. For each bucket, compare Nifty 50 forward 1-, 4- and 13-week returns and maximum drawdown.
   - *Prediction:* the above-16–18 buckets carry materially larger forward drawdowns.
   - *Data:* daily India VIX and Nifty 50, 2008 to present. [SbtFCxbIB1w]

2. **Range-shift rule.** Define a calm regime as VIX holding within 10–12 for at least 4 weeks. Define the signal as the first close where the 10-day VIX average exceeds 14. Test whether Nifty's 1–2 month drawdown after the signal is larger than after random dates inside calm regimes.
   - *Data:* same as test 1. [kwbfXjRtEkk, B8HZvUkY5sA]

3. **Spike-and-fade bottom.** Define the event as VIX peaking in 28–33, or more generally above 28, then closing at least 20% below that peak. Compare forward 1-, 3- and 12-month Nifty returns with the unconditional distribution.
   - Also test a variant that requires Nifty to be below its 200-DMA.
   - *Data:* same as test 1. Expect few events (on the order of 10), so report each one individually. [KUlNy1_Rk9M, Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA, s1ZCPQI5894]

4. **ORB conditioned on VIX.** Run a fixed ORB on Nifty futures, or a liquid stock basket, with a fixed opening window and fixed stops and targets. Compare per-trade expectancy on days where the prior-close VIX is above 18 versus below 14.
   - *Data:* 1- or 5-minute intraday bars plus daily VIX, with costs included. [hk4d8AxbQ3A]

5. **Initial balance breach.** Define the initial balance as the first-hour range. Classify it as wide if it is in the top tercile of trailing-20-day initial-balance width. Measure P(day range exceeds the initial balance) when VIX is above 17 versus 12–14. Split stocks by 1-year beta to Nifty.
   - *Data:* intraday bars and betas. [4kP0_EuS_EY]

6. **Option-chain resistance.** Each week, take the strike with the highest call OI (and highest put OI) for the nearest monthly expiry. Measure how often Nifty closes beyond that strike before expiry. Test whether adding OI-based levels to a price/volume trend model improves 4–6 month direction forecasts. Also test PCR extremes as bottom signals against a "higher low plus 50-DMA reclaim" entry.
   - *Data:* historical NSE option chains, including OI by strike. [kkJ4m9JQ4pM, hKnYYRpd63s]

7. **Volatility contraction in new uptrends.** For breakouts, such as new 52-week highs from a base, compute ATR(14) at the breakout and at the point price is up 6%. Compare forward 3–6 month returns for "ATR fell" versus "ATR flat or rose."
   - *Data:* daily OHLC for the Nifty 500 or similar. [tTdy5srpW9Q]

8. **VIX-conditioned sector rotation.** When VIX is above its 50-day average and rising, compare 1–3 month returns of defensive sector indices (FMCG, pharma) against high-beta indices (metal, realty, PSU bank). Repeat for low, range-bound VIX.
   - The sector choices here are the tester's assumption; he does not name sectors.
   - *Data:* NSE sector indices plus VIX. [s1ZCPQI5894]

## 4. Transfer to TSX and US markets

**Applies largely as is**
- The cautions on lot sizes and fixed contract sizes, though US/Canadian equity options and futures have their own contract sizes.
- The asymmetry argument against naked option writing.
- The experience and capital gate for selling options.
- The rule not to use OI, option-chain levels or PCR for trend direction.
- The idea that falling volatility should accompany a new uptrend (test 7 is market-neutral).
- Watching for a renewed volatility spike after a recovery.
- Hedging a long book by shorting index futures: ES/MES for the US, SXF for TSX 60. Costs and margin differ.

**Needs recalibration**
- **All VIX levels** are India VIX levels. For the US, use VIX; for Canada, use the S&P/TSX 60 VIX (VIXC). Bands such as 9–14 okay, 16–18 worry, 28–32 peak and 35–36 bottom must be rebuilt from each index's own history, for example as percentiles, not copied.
- **Sector rotation** must be mapped to US/Canadian sector definitions. The TSX is heavy in financials, energy and materials, which changes what counts as "defensive."
- **ORB and initial-balance filters** depend on local session structure and the local volatility index.
- **The F&O-boom framing.** The retail F&O-loss argument is framed on the Indian boom. The US has its own retail 0DTE options boom, but his specific evidence is Indian.

**India-only**
- The derivatives-to-cash volume ratio.
- Weekly-expiry dynamics.
- The NSE closing auction / CAS distortions and the advice to use futures charts.
- SIP/STP flows as a VIX dampener.
- Historical Nifty episodes: 2012 range, 2013 bottom, 2020 OI levels.
- Indian margin rules.

## 5. Gaps and doubts

**Thin or absent topics**
- He gives no teaching on spreads, straddles or strangles as structures. He has no strike-selection, delta, implied-volatility or Greeks content beyond saying time decay and volatility matter.
- There are no option-buying entry or exit rules, and no rules for buying puts or for hedge ratios.
- PCR is mentioned only to dismiss it.
- The chapter title's topics are mostly covered by his refusal to teach them.

**No codable rule for VIX bottoms**
- Bottom zones vary by episode: 28–32, 32–33, 35–36, and Covid-scale.
- The trigger and confirmation are never defined.
- This sits in tension with his rejection of oversold and PCR bottom-fishing.

**Threshold drift**
- The VIX worry level moved from about 17–18 (13 years ago) and 18–20 (6 years ago) to 16–18, then to a range shift to 14–18 in recent videos.
- It is unclear whether this is a deliberate recalibration.

**Internal tensions**
- Options are ranked last [zWYCYySQVNc] versus called most rewarding [9dbnXEEHgsc].
- Beginners are told to avoid futures, while he trades leveraged index futures himself [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- Old intraday ORB and initial-balance VIX filters coexist with his later "avoid intraday" stance.
- A recent optimistic outlook for responsible F&O traders [-qQntPmfe-s] versus a decade of avoidance advice.

**Caption uncertainty**
- The unnamed volatility measure in the uptrend guideline [tTdy5srpW9Q].
- Derivatives/cash volume multiples [Lw6h6T30VvE, Y4TewQHVhvc].
- The "75–90" range in kwbfXjRtEkk.

**Unverified empirical claims**
- The 90% beginner-failure figure is his estimate. Indian regulator studies on F&O losses exist but are not cited with numbers in the notes.
- The claim that most options expire worthless.
- The claim that backtests show writing wiping out months of profit in a day.
- The claim that the closing auction is "gamed."

All of these are assertions to verify, not findings.
