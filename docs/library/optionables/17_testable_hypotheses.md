# Testable hypotheses (the register)

This chapter restates Optionables' teaching as a list of testable hypotheses. Every row is his claim or method rewritten so it can be checked. **None of them is validated.** Several rest on a single day's example or a handful of events, and some contradict each other across videos.

Before any row could become a trading rule, the program's research rules apply:

- **Pre-register** the exact rule before looking at results.
- **Count every look** at the data, including parameter variants.
- **Charge real costs.** His own videos stress that Indian statutory charges and slippage are first-order.
- **Keep a sealed holdout period** that is touched once.

The notes carry no recording dates. Where videos disagree I flag the conflict, but I cannot say which version came later.

Two general caveats:

- **Liquidity and expiry structure.** Much of his method depends on Indian weekly index expiries. Several notes warn that this structure has changed over time [lkOC0gpSmQQ, NXE7KFxmH7I]. In the Markets column, "US (adapt)" means testing on SPX/XSP-style short-dated options with rescaled distances. "CA" is marked weak wherever the notes say Canadian index-option liquidity is thin [3uZnthbvz3A, ufH0zRUyuIw].
- **Point distances.** Distances quoted in points (Bank Nifty 300/500/800, Nifty 100/150) must be rescaled as percent of spot or volatility units outside the original period.

## The 10 to test first, and why

These are chosen because each is central to how he actually trades, is stated precisely enough to pre-register, and can be run on Indian 1-minute option data or free NSE end-of-day files.

1. **OPT-H02 (1 pm Bank Nifty straddle, 30% stops, 14:55 exit).** It is the most fully specified system in the notes, with entry time, stop, exit, sizing and claimed statistics (about 70% wins, about 11% drawdown). That gives a clean pass/fail replication [8i-eCbGznbo]. Run it out of sample, past his period.
2. **OPT-H03 (9:30, 50% stop, 15:00 exit).** It is equally specified and provides the morning counterpart that OPT-H05 needs [L9f-oq8XVOo].
3. **OPT-H06 (stop width).** It appears in a dozen videos with contradictory conclusions. Run one grid (25–100%) on identical entries, pre-registered, with the number of looks counted. This settles whether the stop parameter is robust or curve-fit.
4. **OPT-H07 (stop-to-cost).** It recurs in at least nine videos, including Budget and Bitcoin systems, and one video explicitly argues against it [Fe766Um-ayQ]. It is a simple binary toggle on OPT-H02 and OPT-H03.
5. **OPT-H08 (staggered entries).** His portfolio logic [R22ZKq9rs_M] depends on staggered entries being less than perfectly correlated. It is directly measurable from the same dataset.
6. **OPT-H17 (overnight expiry strangle).** It is fully specified, with return and drawdown figures for both the unhedged and hedged versions, so it is a direct replication [oYfLG7jLvPQ].
7. **OPT-H16 (naked vs winged weekly straddle).** It is specified for 2017–2022 and contradicts his own repeated advice to hedge. Resolving it decides the hedging stance used everywhere else.
8. **OPT-H25 (OI band contains the expiry close).** It is the most repeated market-reading claim (over a dozen videos). It is codable from free NSE end-of-day OI. It needs an honest baseline, such as an equal-width band or a straddle-implied band.
9. **OPT-H13 (daily loss cap).** It is a core risk rule in many videos and is cheap to overlay on the P&L series from OPT-H02 and OPT-H03.
10. **OPT-H18 (range-day base rates).** This is the foundation of his "sell premium because two thirds of days are range-bound" argument. It needs only OHLC data, is trivially reproducible, and can be checked on later years and on US and Canadian indices.

Next in line is OPT-H42 (put ratio spreads, his main positional structure). It is deferred only because it needs monthly chain history with margins.

## What is not testable as taught, and what it would need

- **Discretionary support/resistance and "conviction".** Many rows (strangles at chart levels, bull put spreads after "confirmed" bounces, removing hedges on conviction) rely on levels he picks by eye [e.g. 8W58_AoCKrw, 35_p7TIkaTQ, 91-eSFLtkhg]. Testing needs a pre-registered, mechanical level rule (swing points, touch counts, tolerance) applied blind.
- **Judgment-based adjustment.** Examples include "exit on the first adverse gap vs adjust" and "adjust only in low-uncertainty regimes" [0tWQskBo6Pc, marked not codable]. These need an explicit regime and event definition first.
- **Rules that live in another video or were never stated.** Missing pieces include:
  - the counter-trade definition [-uIGVxZWSHU];
  - stop distances [A0sLu7cjjlU, c29sN7KQSIs, GxKIIKl4eZA, m3izeXsRdW8];
  - the imbalance threshold [09Bt6pDtUpw];
  - the full rule set behind expiry results [c4yDnTlhYSM].

  The missing parameter must be fixed before looking, or the test becomes a parameter search.
- **Single-day anecdotes and dated level calls.** Examples:
  - "Nifty stays 17,000–17,600 next week" [4Zd60oo6vOM];
  - "16,800–17,400" [pZPCRg5w9gw];
  - one-day re-entry stories [alxZlbVHVeo, SalkWOxQ2Rk, 7LlVnFIIEB4].

  These are outcomes, not rules. They can only be turned into a general rule (e.g. a range-persistence test) and scored on many instances.
- **Behavioural claims.** Examples: oversized trading produces more rule-breaking, increasing size after a losing month hurts, and staged scale-up after drawdowns [qhHfMHyouqs, iZcd9gImTFU, dAx2mchFMSs]. These need a personal trade journal with sizes and adjustments, not market data.
- **Data that is hard or impossible to get.**
  - Grey-market IPO premiums [fhtdX_cbQ_c, g2EoRGqSNUg, V0yxJqF2Ccs].
  - Retail flow and demat data [IusAI8P3JEI].
  - Account-level survival before and after margin rules [AGATtid-fhU].
  - Pledge haircut histories [GZlwZIH4UOI].
  - Tick-level order-book depth for freak trades [vx8x_O7-01g, JfeLqaREQU0].

  Each needs a sourced dataset first; otherwise it stays a claim.
- **Unfalsifiable or non-hypotheses.**
  - "20% weekly targets can't be sustained" [MsdTw_a4l-U] is a general statement.
  - "Positive edge repeated wins" is not a market claim.
  - The compounding arithmetic [yDec0_hqKWk] is a calculation to verify, not a hypothesis.
  - Crypto-ban recovery [VKz6QVWrR48] and the 2020 China-shutdown comparison [X7Q4YvmDDTE] have no rule and too few events.
- **Contradictions that must be resolved by design, not by picking a favourite.** OPT-H05, H06, H16, H20, H28, H36 and H52 contain opposing claims from different videos. The notes carry no dates, so I cannot tell whether his view changed or he simply held both views. Each needs all variants pre-registered together, with the multiple-comparison count recorded.
