# How they work

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits his method

Optionables (a team channel, mostly led by one trader) presents trading as a business run on process rather than prediction. The core is non-directional selling of index option premium, mainly Bank Nifty and Nifty straddles and strangles. This is run as two books:

- **A rule-based "system" book:** fixed entry times, fixed per-leg stops and fixed exits, increasingly automated.
- **A discretionary intraday/positional book:** managed with adjustments, open interest (OI) and price levels.

Around this core he teaches:

- planning entry, adjustment and exit before the trade;
- sizing so that any single loss is psychologically small;
- judging systems by expectancy rather than total profit or win rate;
- modest return targets (roughly 2–5% a month) instead of large payoffs;
- standing aside in disorderly markets;
- a heavy routine of journaling, broker-report reconciliation and public posting of losses.

Much of the "mindset" material comes from his own documented mistakes: revenge trading, oversizing after losses, stray orders and discretionary trades added on top of the system. All of the following are his claims and methods, to be treated as hypotheses.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Plan the whole trade before entering, exit first

**What it is.** Every short-volatility trade has three parts decided at entry: the entry logic, the adjustment plan and the exit plan. Each expectation should have a matching adjustment and exit [h5XNvyTMSsk, tY0noMv3lp0].

**Supporting habits.**
- Define exit criteria before entry criteria. Know in advance what you do on a 10–15% news move [3MMjcR_ortI].
- Imagine the worst outcome first, so the loss is already accepted when it comes [d6ZOFGDVw2Y, dAx2mchFMSs].
- Fix the price that proves the view wrong. When it breaks, flip the position; in his example, Bank Nifty was expected to hold 34,700 and fell to about 34,400 [nMlFJigaxnM].
- Have a pre-planned "fire-fighting" plan before carrying into expiry [G2Jm-SaT6M8].

**Conditional plans on the open.** Before uncertain events he carries nothing overnight. He prepares separate plans for a gap up, a flat open and a gap down [16t3E1t2tgk, jNqYVp_RXgg, SalkWOxQ2Rk]. A guest's version: decide entry, exit, stop and risk the evening before, then execute mechanically, ignoring opening noise and TV news [dAx2mchFMSs].

**Confirmation before acting.** He enters on a gap only if the level holds after the open, for example above 33,750 [0xrgICpggJI]. Ten minutes of a gap holding proved too short in one costly case [oAJ8k8TzB7E].

**How often.** This is taught often and clearly, across both older and newer videos.

### 2.2 System trading vs discretion (his views changed and conflict)

**The system.** A strategy is defined by its legs, stop, entry time and run days, and is executed with no intraday intervention [F5O2b6_5npQ, ZJyMZJekVBk, Hma2mI-kOjY]. The reasons he gives:
- system trades keep emotion out;
- diversification across indices and entry times lets one bad trade be offset by others [bLFCt52D89o, xj-1uHZotgY, 8No43FVgZfU, dzFMyd0FiXA].

On a good day for sellers, he credits not adding or adjusting, with a 4 lakh daily book-out level, for the result [dzFMyd0FiXA].

**Discretion hurts.** Adding discretion defeats the system, yet he still does it occasionally [Fe766Um-ayQ]. Several videos blame discretionary add-ons for damage:
- Thursday discretion took a peak of about +5–5.5 lakh to about −1–1.5 lakh [Vn40nCE-lLI];
- an extra intraday trade broke a positional winning streak, costing −1.8 lakh against +1.4 lakh [5vAHDwZkoHc];
- he closed and restarted after breaking the system with extra trades [vmjxCxSdIn0].

**Discretion helps.** Other videos say discretion reduced losses or rescued the day:
- [3uZnthbvz3A];
- a pure system would have hit the Nifty call stops [RiTtKlJDrdI];
- the day went from −2.1 lakh to +2.65 lakh after switching to discretion [NHkRRMWhx4U];
- Bqh1AevjUzg.

His reconciliation is to run both books so that each hedges the other. Discretion adds edge when the trend is clear and can repair the book after a stop [lkOC0gpSmQQ, nXjmslNVkyo, L8SZ30on7po].

**For beginners.** He suggests trading discretionary with small size first, then deriving your own rules. Borrowed rules tend to be dropped in drawdown [d6ZOFGDVw2Y]. He also suggests picking a simple strategy, such as a straddle with a fixed stop, and running it for a few weeks [NMxUve9X2hY].

### 2.3 Staggered fixed-time entries (the schedule drifted)

**Structure.** He takes three separate intraday short straddle or strangle entries per day. Each has its own stops and is tracked separately [UEOPUfhENcE, LK5yGOZMcj0, OGPVzN-tt3k].

| Schedule (IST) | Videos | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 10:00, 13:00, 14:00 | fQfzG_emzZw, k6bDh3ONgz0, Vn40nCE-lLI, OGPVzN-tt3k, 9pT7h-M2v64, jEF9S2_P5Po | Closed together around 15:12. The 10:00 slot was skipped most days near financial year-end to be less aggressive. |
| 12:30, 13:00, 14:00 | ybZYfvQzy7E, 6knNR520aK8, wr82JkzZyys | The 10:00 slot was moved because markets turned choppy or the timing changed. The first entry could be shifted later on volatile days. |
| about 9:30, 11:30, 13:30 | AyC9r0_ZiN8 | — |

**Stops.** Stop widths vary: 30% on the algo system [nXjmslNVkyo, uoFM, lkOC0gpSmQQ] and 75% per leg in one layered-straddle video [nXjmslNVkyo].

**Contradictory views on timing.**
- Earlier entries perform better [lkOC0gpSmQQ].
- On Budget day, pre-noon entries far outperformed post-noon ones [QVeSJBPD_i4].
- Afternoon-only entries lacked decay, and he considered adding morning entries back [A0sLu7cjjlU].
- Wednesday is generally profitable; Thursday is traded discretionarily [wr82JkzZyys].

**Regime weakness.** The system did badly in sharp V-shaped reversal months, for example one April [AyC9r0_ZiN8, O0PDivTMZXE].

### 2.4 Judge systems by expectancy, test before trusting

**Rank by expectancy.** Rank candidate strategies by expectancy ratio first. Do not rank by total profit, win rate, longest streak or fewest stop days; he says he made that mistake [L9f-oq8XVOo].

**Thresholds he gives** [urrT8zpnGus, lkOC0gpSmQQ]:

| Measure | His benchmark |
|---|---|
| Single-strategy expectancy | 0.3–0.4 acceptable |
| Basket expectancy | aim for 0.5–0.7 or more |
| Expectancy near 1 | excellent |
| Expectancy 0.48 with return-to-drawdown of 12 | fairly good |
| Profit factor 2.1 | low; he wants 3–4 |
| Slippage assumed in backtests | 0.5% |

**Comparing VIX regimes.** Also look at the share of days with no stop hit and with both stops hit [X8sW2WNaKAs].

**What to read in a backtest** [L9f-oq8XVOo]:
- the monthly breakup;
- daily bars;
- the equity curve;
- the trade log;
- optional VIX filters, such as skipping days above 15 or 20.

**Discretionary adjustments.** These cannot be tested on a rules backtester and need a day-stepping simulator [ZJyMZJekVBk].

**Caveats he repeats.** A backtest only shows past behaviour and drawdown size. Five of six good budgets can still be followed by a loss [8No43FVgZfU, QVeSJBPD_i4, oYfLG7jLvPQ, xj-1uHZotgY]. Backtesting is a filter, not a prediction [NMxUve9X2hY]. Popular retail setups, such as a 9:20 straddle with a 50% stop, lose their edge as they spread [d6ZOFGDVw2Y].

**Trial lengths.**
- Run a new system for 3–4 weeks before revising [LrBXT5C_YYY, Hma2mI-kOjY].
- Allow 60–90 days to judge [3MMjcR_ortI].
- Do not abandon a system during 3–4-day losing streaks; judge it over months to years [NXE7KFxmH7I].
- A 60% edge needs about 100 trades to show; the first ten trades may give four winners [8i-eCbGznbo].

**Portfolio of strategies.** Backtest each strategy and export daily P&L. Build a correlation matrix and combine the pairs with near-zero or negative correlation. Highly correlated additions do not help, and every combination must be tested [R22ZKq9rs_M]. His claimed result: similar profit, lower drawdown and a smoother curve, though not always more profit than the best single strategy. He splits capital as fixed shares, but the exact figures are unclear [R22ZKq9rs_M]. He also suggests diversifying across non-correlated systems rather than quitting one during a drawdown [d6ZOFGDVw2Y], and keeping an "arsenal" of strategies for range versus breakout regimes [F0x5faXXRTI].

### 2.5 Win rate vs payoff

About 50% accuracy is enough if winners are larger than losers. He criticises sellers who advertise 90–95% accuracy [k6bDh3ONgz0, FJ9Bz0shh_I]. He cites reward-to-risk of 1:3–1:4 at roughly 50% wins [K6Fnb-G4Fl0]. For trend following he expects 30–40% wins, so reward-to-risk must be high [dmZmt1T5bU0].

He frames this with a casino analogy: the house has about a 52/48 edge repeated many times [MsdTw_a4l-U]. His own option win rate is about 60–65%. He prefers frequent small losses to long streaks, believing long winning streaks tend to be followed by long losing streaks [pdGXhkLJPj0]. Judge yourself by P&L, not by being right about direction [K6Fnb-G4Fl0].

### 2.6 Position sizing as the main risk control

**Numbers he gives.**
- Risk 1–2% of capital per trade [VmiJLxaYPUQ].
- Keep a losing trade to 2–3% [aDbp7e_Qvxo].
- Losses of about 1% keep him calm; a 10 lakh loss would make him emotional [Pt5pbpK1eoI].

**Sizing as gap protection.** Correct sizing, rather than wings, is the real protection against black-swan gaps. His figure: about 99% of overnight gaps stay within 3% [1fDefykVmnk].

**Psychological limit.** Above some size you cannot control your psychology, whatever you intend [qhHfMHyouqs]. Smaller size lets a position "breathe" [7yL3qGyijeM]. Trade only a size you are comfortable holding, and reduce or exit when uncomfortable [QEj_68mxDHI, wdUlevVfZL4, XLBtvv6Jq18]. Never commit the entire capital to one trade; spare capital allows adjustment [dPuJiR0oxRs].

**Stops: a contradiction.** He says he never trades without a stop [Bqh1AevjUzg, G2Jm-SaT6M8]. Yet his positional trades carry no fixed stop and rely on hedges and adjustments [JdR7-suVxCI].

**Daily loss limit.** Stop trading for the day once the limit is hit [YtLDcJgJzcQ, dzFMyd0FiXA (4 lakh)].

**New instruments.** Start with a small share of capital or one lot, and scale slowly. No business scales tenfold in a few months [VmiJLxaYPUQ, NMxUve9X2hY, Hma2mI-kOjY ($5,000 with a possible $50–60k), MeKx6Qh50vs (capped risk only), LrBXT5C_YYY]. He also enters small and scales in [lev6pAAKvYE].

**Weekly buckets.** He splits weekly targets by trading style:

- about 4 lakh total [RFvomGYSt6k].

**Compounding arithmetic.**
- 2.5% a month compounds to about 35% a year [yDec0_hqKWk].
- 0.75% a week compounds to about 26× over 10 years [9a3TdAs7xUw].
- He claims 2.5–3% a month in his own prop account [yDec0_hqKWk].
- He calls 20% a week on a small account unsustainable [MsdTw_a4l-U].
- He says newcomers expecting 20–30k a month on 3–5 lakh are unrealistic [VDvCfzInayY].

**Inconsistency on recovery.** He sometimes divides a loss by the weeks remaining: 15 lakh over 5 weeks is about 3 lakh a week [ow6NsXYp9G0, XmP7nK_TMdo]. Elsewhere he warns against recovery pressure and says to judge against the annual target and ignore past P&L [qhHfMHyouqs].

### 2.8 Standing aside and choosing the regime

Not trading is a valid decision. Examples he gives:
- about 2 months in cash after the Bitcoin crash [NSeHRFG7XVA];
- event uncertainty [Ga6QSMLRT4o, 6WGhlEAouxM];
- no clear nearby support or resistance [pMVDiDH8v08];
- choppy two-sided spikes: trade small, use zero-DTE or level-based hedged trades, or skip [20cgOw1G6FE];
- stop for a while after consecutive losing days [dAx2mchFMSs];
- no rush to enter [cA07gugsaeY, HcdusL_8W5U].

**High-volatility rules.**
- Prefer intraday over overnight when direction is unclear and VIX is peaking [tn3IDCnC6dc].
- Keep positional size very small until India VIX is below 18 [iljL1viJ6I8].
- Trade intraday only for 2–3 days until VIX falls after an event [6qive5Kb4Jk].
- Wait about two weeks before new community positions [yS4PWW8mfsE].
- Skip intraday when high VIX coincides with large FII/DII flows [tJEhYTG7_k4].

**Conflict.** In whipsaw markets he does the opposite: skip intraday, where both stops get hit, and sell hedged far-OTM positional strangles [km4uw-zxRZM]. He openly switches between intraday and positional books as conditions change [7RI6oed3Eak].

**Direction rules.**
- Do not trade non-directional just because you are confused [xpMrVzXpW34].
- Do not position against the prevailing direction [Mt5qSl4j3fQ, NAxOcOcyQog, c1hz4zsTuvc, m3vTimQ3HiE].
- Add a slight positive delta after an upside breakout [rc4ZbNpzND8].
- Wait for HDFC Bank to confirm before trading Bank Nifty [5PSE_0Yw9sc].
- These "follow the trend" rules come mostly from his newer videos (about 2 years old).

### 2.9 Charts, bias and adapting views (changed over time)

**Older view.** Older videos say he avoids charts because a chart creates a directional bias and hope, and gives false signals. He used technical analysis for 5–7 years before preferring non-directional selling [xXDwxid9Fjc, BFZXz79936Y, IMjqrkYosgY, O0PDivTMZXE, GsrNVbSwH04, though he glances at Ichimoku and Bollinger Bands]. He opens charts only in fast markets [F6sjGAcJXXc].

**Other videos.** He combines chart levels, OI and payoff [cJpUydwXnIw, tbHLBk84Btc, vRGhcoCUbBE, lyQNM8KBec4]. He marks only the swing high and swing low and does nothing inside them [oQXCIfaJ1fs].

**Newer videos** are level-driven:
- ignore distant levels in weekly trades [2twL9IgbmTY];
- range-bound until 43,900 or 43,200 breaks [pnY7S-Oi3aQ];
- 1-hour support and resistance [VyF_xG1f6Aw];
- react to the break rather than predict [9gMDEfgWmFg].

**Common thread.** Never predict [LPGCiis_rlg]. Change the view quickly when the market disagrees [oXZsCNPBUoE, DXTdywSl0D0, FBNJsrqlPiQ, 7LlVnFIIEB4]. Before forming a weekly view, review the prior week back to the previous Friday [m22yYC83nG0, T2p11C5qPxk, D84ygO71gvA].

### 2.10 Adjustment discipline

- Do not hurry to adjust. Ask what happens if price returns 200–300 points to the strike, and have a contingency, even if it is just exiting [-IDDp5bvrVQ].
- Do not adjust merely because breakeven is touched; require several supporting facts, such as call OI at 40,000 and 40,500 [-IDDp5bvrVQ].
- Do not adjust a balanced position because another one stopped out [jztFfYokRqI].
- Do not adjust when no stop is triggered and the market is in range [rEX0xkc5Fes].
- Focus adjustments on reducing loss, not adding profit [8W58_AoCKrw].
- Not every fire needs fighting [0tWQskBo6Pc].
- Exiting from discomfort is not a mistake. Exit everything in violent markets and re-enter later [GJ-1jOUSekk, jztFfYokRqI, wdUlevVfZL4].
- Intraday system adjustments must not be applied to positional trades [09Bt6pDtUpw].

### 2.11 Psychology and the mistakes he names

**Losses.** A loss is not necessarily a mistake; judge the process [VV3vue5PaQo, YtLDcJgJzcQ, O0PDivTMZXE, ow6NsXYp9G0, ocl7mwUeZA8]. Losses teach more than profits; profits feed ego [YMB-ruN6sp8, it9ef0_jnVY].

**Revenge trading and oversizing after a loss.**
- Revenge trading added about 1 lakh to a 2.7 lakh error [s2KhVugNPfI].
- He compares it to a martingale [iZcd9gImTFU]; see also [dAx2mchFMSs, ZDUmKEZXWMw].
- He tripled or quadrupled size after a 10 lakh (about 2.5%) October loss [qhHfMHyouqs].
- He refused to cut size for 60–70 days, with a peak loss of about 18 lakh [7yL3qGyijeM].

**Overconfidence and social pressure.**
- A 13–14-month winning streak led to the belief he could adjust anything [qhHfMHyouqs].
- Fixed costs (about 6–7 lakh a month in interest and salaries) added pressure [qhHfMHyouqs].
- Social comparison and peer-group confidence distort judgment [qhHfMHyouqs, iZcd9gImTFU].
- Confirmation bias [dAx2mchFMSs, iZcd9gImTFU].

**Hunch-based additions and execution timing.**
- Selling extra puts on a hunch of support [tKlpB5RfdFA, oAJ8k8TzB7E, pZPCRg5w9gw: +2–2.5 lakh turned into −40–50k].
- Closing a hedge leg on a bounce [nMlFJigaxnM].
- Hurried exits [z0Ke0RrtVHw].
- A greedy late strangle at about 15:17 gave back about 2.5 lakh [L8SZ30on7po].
- Entering before IV peaked [peakDi79pVs].

**Remedies.**
- Breaks of 10 minutes [qyY60yFUp04], 20 minutes [UIf_6_Bg66I], 40–45 minutes [ZDUmKEZXWMw], 1–2 hours [GJ-1jOUSekk], 4–5 days with counselling [qhHfMHyouqs], or until a set date [LPGCiis_rlg].
- Treat each day fresh.
- Keep physical cash beside you as a reminder that losses are real money (guest) [dAx2mchFMSs].
- Before going full-time: no dependents, at least a year of expenses outside the account, and no withdrawals [d6ZOFGDVw2Y].

**Mindset over strategy.** He repeatedly says psychology and discipline matter more than strategy [oQXCIfaJ1fs, Gv3AMZVWrrI, NSFWr0h2n_c, 303gQALZWuw, ybZYfvQzy7E].

### 2.13 Costs, journaling and review

**Costs.** Keeping profit matters as much as making it. Ask the broker for all charges and recompute contract notes [LsyNOm0vDec]. Costs can swamp gross profit:
- F&O gross of 2.94 lakh against 5.66 lakh charges for a quarter [7yL3qGyijeM];
- about 1 lakh of charges against 35–40k of gains [oO-T8_3l_5g];
- an expiry-day gross of 8,700 turned into −4,500 net after adjustments [-uIGVxZWSHU].

Statutory charges dwarf brokerage itself [5_i637lBoPU, IMjqrkYosgY, o2JJ5p03cyc]. His daily journal assumes about Rs 7,000 of costs on normal days and Rs 12,500 on expiry days [NTavZ18KPh8, wcTPYuhzvAU, qyY60yFUp04].

**Journal.** Keep an Excel record of each trade's P&L, times and reason. A guest adds tracking by weekday [dAx2mchFMSs]. Analyse win rate and average win and loss [3rLNeQZk0mk]. Posting a P&L statement is worthless without analysis [YMB-ruN6sp8]. Big losses (about 1–1.5 lakh or more) get a screenshot review [GQEbyZhG-ME]. Reconcile the journal monthly against the broker back office [3rLNeQZk0mk, uoFM5_FM4].

**Measuring returns.** Return = (realised + unrealised − charges) / starting capital. Use the broker's "indicative" period report. Separate F&O from equity, crediting equity mostly to the market [Au2cYvYojNM, TotwNo2N6Jg, YMB-ruN6sp8, kmfo3YKUF4Q, o2JJ5p03cyc]. Benchmark quarterly against Nifty [U_YWXcxYBXc, TotwNo2N6Jg]. Distinguish MTM (from yesterday's close) from P&L (from entry) [3FZRs6nCvDk].

**Daily routine.** Summarise the market, read OI (Nifty then Bank Nifty), state the next-day range, review trades and open positions, and name the main risk [dTaB6gl7Ja4, YhGttuO7nGQ, rEX0xkc5Fes, 0pvrPKfpU4U, 81ad8bxpbrA].

**Transparency.** He posts losses publicly [vmjxCxSdIn0, 303gQALZWuw, -kE6iIjC1Ys, YtLDcJgJzcQ, ocl7mwUeZA8]. He describes long flat or losing stretches:
- Oct–Jan, about −20 lakh, after +1.5 crore [xwWE0ZPmP3s];
- about 5 months, by an attendee's account [lm_sHquPkYw].

### 2.14 Broker and platform due diligence (brief)

A broker should allow far-OTM and long-dated hedges, have good technology, transparent pricing and support, and a later MIS square-off. Trial a new broker with Rs 5–10k [fiueDBlIWgw]. Ask every safety and custody question before using a new venue [uN9SRyTv_yY].

## 3. How a program would test it

1. **Staggered entry slots.** Sell an ATM Bank Nifty (and Nifty) weekly straddle at each slot: 9:30, 10:00, 11:30, 12:30, 13:00, 13:30, 14:00. Use per-leg stops at 30% and at 75%, exit at 15:12–15:15, and apply 0.5% slippage plus Indian charges. Compare:
   - per-slot expectancy and stop-hit profile;
   - weekday effect, testing Wednesday > others [wr82JkzZyys];
   - a three-slot portfolio vs a single slot on maximum drawdown and return-to-drawdown.

   *Data:* 1-minute option and spot data, 2017 onward.
2. **Correlation-chosen baskets.** From the backtested daily P&L of several variants, choose combinations by lowest correlation. Out of sample, test whether they beat random combinations on return-to-drawdown, and whether adding a strategy with correlation above 0.8 leaves drawdown unchanged [R22ZKq9rs_M].
3. **Expectancy as a selection rule.** Across many parameter variants, rank in-sample by expectancy, by total profit and by win rate. Compare out-of-sample return-to-drawdown, and test whether in-sample expectancy above 0.4–0.5 persists [L9f-oq8XVOo, urrT8zpnGus, lkOC0gpSmQQ].
4. **Behavioural rules on a fixed system.** Simulate the same system under four rules:
   - constant size;
   - 3× size after a losing month or week;
   - stopping for the day at a daily loss limit;
   - a 2-day pause after a 1–2% drawdown.

   Measure drawdown and risk of ruin [qhHfMHyouqs, YtLDcJgJzcQ, iZcd9gImTFU].
5. **VIX regime and overnight risk.**
   - Check whether overnight short-straddle loss tails exceed intraday tails when India VIX is above 18 [iljL1viJ6I8, tn3IDCnC6dc].
   - Check whether entering positional selling only after a VIX spike retreats beats selling during it [6qive5Kb4Jk].
   - Check whether at least 99% of Nifty and Bank Nifty overnight gaps are within 3% [1fDefykVmnk].

## 4. Transfer to TSX and US markets

**Applies as is:**

- exit criteria first;

## 5. Gaps and doubts

- **Expectancy is never defined.** The notes do not say whether it means average profit over average loss, or a per-unit-risk ratio. His thresholds cannot be applied until this is pinned down.
- **System details are thin.** Stop width (30% vs 75%), strike choice, re-entry rules and the exact daily exit time vary by video, and several videos withhold the rules entirely [Vn40nCE-lLI, AyC9r0_ZiN8].
- **Unresolved contradictions:**
  - discretion helps vs hurts the system;
  - always use a stop vs positional trades with no stop;
  - high volatility means intraday only [iljL1viJ6I8, tJEhYTG7_k4] vs skip intraday for positional strangles [km4uw-zxRZM];
  - avoid charts (older videos) vs level-based trading (newer videos);
  - earlier entries better vs moving the 10:00 slot to 12:30;
  - recovery-target division vs "ignore past P&L".
- **Performance figures are self-reported and inconsistent.** Example: 5% of 2.5 crore stated as 1.5 lakh [UEOPUfhENcE]. Many single-day anecdotes are not evidence. Budget-day statistics rest on 7 samples.
- **Caption quality.** Several notes are too garbled to use [cmhJCosywwM, DimfDZRACgQ, FJ9Bz0shh_I, UGkKoGdRX8o, adkYwcQWv1c, xTBYk8YodKA, 3PZu-YM162o].

- **Not covered here:** his long-term investing, IPO and fundamental-screening material (index ETFs, buy-the-dip, promoter holding, PE myths, pair-trading construction). These appear in the notes but belong to other chapters.
- **Missing specifics.** No concrete drawdown-recovery rules are given beyond pauses and size cuts. Workshop psychology sessions are named but not described [16tbGPu8iYg].
