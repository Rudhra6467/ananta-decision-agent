# Fundamentals

## 1. What he teaches and where it fits

In these notes, fundamentals are a filter and a backdrop. They do not trigger trades. His usual order is to screen for a reasonably good business first and then let the chart decide timing: stage, structure, relative strength [NUDjIohACWs, hpbDmEK-FvM, 488FDla8kuc, zWYCYySQVNc].

He frames the goal as three things lining up: a strong sector, a strong stock and a decent business [488FDla8kuc]. He also says simple patterns work in good companies, and that trading names with no revenue or profit is speculation [T4849hhdwTo].

His fundamental toolkit is short:
- growth (sales, profit, margins);
- return ratios (ROE, ROCE);
- debt;
- ownership (promoter and institutional);
- a loose valuation check.

He also gives sector-specific checklists for banks and defence, and frequent commentary on India-level earnings, valuation and macro. He repeatedly says deep fundamental analysis is not needed, only general awareness [tTdy5srpW9Q, BhQ_YnaRqa0]. Lasting trends, in his view, need a fundamental or macro cause behind them [tTdy5srpW9Q].

Everything below is his method or claim, to be tested.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Fundamentals first, chart second

- **Workflow for swing trading:** run the fundamental scan, then a volume filter, then a high-beta filter (beta about 1.2 or more, 1.1 tolerated; 252-day average volume, with a 20-30% shortfall acceptable). He says the fundamental scan matters more than rigid figures [zWYCYySQVNc, 2Yv0E57_bs4].
- **Narrowing the universe:** basic fundamentals (revenue growth, operating margin) combined with technical patterns cut 3,000-4,000 stocks to 50-75 [hpbDmEK-FvM].
  - One screener.in run cut about 600-700 stocks to 19 [59vJPo6oiNA].
  - He claims growth checks alone (PE, PEG, yearly or quarterly growth) eliminate 85-90% of stocks [Jpu536_AmGc].
- **Valuation plus stage:** he claims that once a stock or sector is both cheap and in a favourable stage, 60-70% of the decision is done [uFFAAZNSozg, 8 years ago]. A later video says only that valuation must be combined with price action, with no metrics given [kkJ4m9JQ4pM].
- **Clarity and frequency:** the ordering is taught clearly and repeatedly. Most supporting videos are old (6-8 years) swing-trading material, plus a few recent ones.

### 2.2 Growth: sales, profit, margins

The fullest treatment is one video, ag4pL-OONoo (1 year ago), which he says follows O'Neil.

**Sales growth**
- Look for consistent double-digit year-on-year sales growth. He says he tracks roughly 18% and up; a stated 133% upper figure may be a caption error.
- Three-year sales CAGR above 20-25% signals sustainable expansion. He also checks 5-year and 7-year CAGR.
- Year-on-year growth matters more than quarter-on-quarter, because seasonality can distort a single quarter. Quarterly acceleration is still a plus.
- Organic growth is preferred over acquired growth.

**Margins**
- Gross margin above roughly 22-23% suggests pricing power. He is more positive when it rises together with profit.
- Operating margin should be in "higher double digits" and expanding.
- Margins should be compared yearly, not quarterly.

**Profit**
- Profit should grow faster than revenue. His example is profit up 30% on revenue up 20%.
- Profit rising on flat revenue should be checked: is the company only cutting costs?
- Profit rising while margins shrink is a warning. His example is an unnamed large-cap paint company.
- He suggests tracking revenue, profit and margins together in a spreadsheet.

**Other videos on growth**
- Sales growth, operating-margin trend and low debt form a basic screen [NUDjIohACWs].
- Check 5-10 years of results: sales, operating profit, margin, net profit, EPS and PE should all grow steadily [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- Growth/market-share grid [Jpu536_AmGc]:
  - stars (high growth, high share) are the top candidates;
  - question marks (high growth, low share) are acceptable;
  - cash cows (high share, slower growth) are optional;
  - dogs (low growth, low share) are to be avoided or sold.
- Earnings-season screen: profit growth above 25%, ROE and ROCE above 13-14%. Use your own lens, not media [lRUDp336l7Q, 2 months ago, the most recent explicit numbers].
- Following O'Neil, EPS growth matters far more than dividends or low PE [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- Traits he says multibaggers share [Bqp9jKQCs30]:
  - aggressive, growth-focused management;
  - strong sales growth in a market that is still expanding;
  - low debt and low volatility;
  - multi-year relative strength outperformance.

### 2.3 Return ratios: ROE and ROCE

His thresholds vary widely across videos and years:

| Threshold | Video |
|---|---|
| ROE and ROCE at least about 6-7% | [488FDla8kuc, 8 years ago] |
| ROE and ROCE both above 20%, for swing candidates | [zWYCYySQVNc, 8 years ago] |
| ROE near 25% | [59vJPo6oiNA, 6 years ago] |
| ROE and ROCE above 13-14% | [lRUDp336l7Q, 2 months ago] |
| ROE above 15% is efficient; growth sectors often above 20%; utilities, FMCG and banking about 8-12% | [ag4pL-OONoo] |

He adds context and quality checks [ag4pL-OONoo]:
- Compare ROE with peers (for example 18% against a 10-12% peer average).
- A falling ROE trend is a red flag.
- He claims great compounders (about 25% a year) usually show ROE of 15-18% or more, sustained or rising.
- ROE should be backed by free cash flow and operating cash. High ROE with negative free cash flow may mean manipulated earnings.
- ROE should not be inflated by debt or one-off gains. Ideally it is driven by rising net margin.

One old video says growth stocks cover ROE/ROCE automatically [2Yv0E57_bs4]. He also claims Indian large banks and IT firms are world-class on ROE/ROCE [rxqOUxO_vP4].

### 2.4 Debt

- **Absolute cutoff:** debt-to-equity below 0.5 appears in old screens [2Yv0E57_bs4, 59vJPo6oiNA].
- **Bands** [ag4pL-OONoo]; he notes the captions are loose on the upper bands:
  - below 0.5 is low risk;
  - 0.5-1.5 is moderate, acceptable for capital-intensive firms only with strong operating cash flow;
  - above about 2 is high risk unless cash flow offsets it.
- **Industry context:** banks and NBFCs carry structural leverage, so the debt filter is relaxed for them [2Yv0E57_bs4]. A raw debt screener wrongly removes high-growth firms and financials [ag4pL-OONoo].
- **Good versus bad debt:**
  - good debt funds expansion, R&D or capacity, and shows up later in profit, margin and cash flow;
  - bad debt funds losses, interest, dividends or waste;
  - he says bad debt is common in small and mid caps [ag4pL-OONoo].
- **When he stresses debt most:**
  - in bear markets, tight liquidity, and when he does not expect rate cuts to help [elpQ8wyLZLQ, 20BxPDdAZAE, hpbDmEK-FvM];
  - for long-term SIP stocks [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].
- **Low-debt claim:** low-debt firms do better long term, even though leverage can help short term [hpbDmEK-FvM].

### 2.5 Ownership and governance

- **High promoter holding** and **high institutional ownership** are listed as quality markers [488FDla8kuc].
- **Institutional ownership cutoffs** [zWYCYySQVNc]:
  - at least 15% for large caps;
  - about 10% for mid and small caps;
  - his reasoning is that institutions research well, though he admits exceptions.
- At least 15% institutional ownership also appears in [2Yv0E57_bs4].
- **Promoter or insider selling** is generally negative, citing Lynch. He promised later coverage; it is not in these notes [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- **Idea sources:**
  - holdings of top small/mid-cap mutual funds and scanners tracking marquee investors [hpbDmEK-FvM];
  - institutional accumulation plus good governance, CANSLIM-style [T4849hhdwTo].

### 2.6 Valuation: the main tension in his teaching

He holds two positions that pull against each other.

**Growth-first position (valuation mostly ignored)**
- Low PE usually reflects an inferior record, and high dividend payouts can signal a weaker company [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- He is comfortable with high P/E, high P/S and high P/B when growth is visible and the chart sets up.
- His own top-25 portfolio snapshot [KRoie4cj5TQ]:
  - average PE in the 40s, about double the Nifty's roughly 21;
  - individual PEs from the 30s to about 115;
  - P/S about 3 to over 20;
  - P/B mostly 4-23.
- He says to ignore noise about PE above roughly 70-80 when the company has low debt and strong management. He cites O'Neil, Darvas and Minervini buying stocks at PE above 100, and says to stick with "pedigree" companies [N5bb0Cu8RAs].

**Value-aware position**
- PEG (P/E divided by growth, from Lynch): a high P/E can still be fine if growth is high enough [p5O_uspLu9k, Jpu536_AmGc].
- One old screen states PEG above 2 as a growth measure [2Yv0E57_bs4]. This is likely a misstatement and the direction is unclear.
- Compare P/E and P/B with industry and market, as a rough guide only. His examples [BhQ_YnaRqa0]:

  | Stock | P/E | Industry P/E | Market P/E | P/B | Industry P/B | Market P/B |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | HDFC Bank | 23.6 | 12.4 | 30.4 | 3.1 | 1.6 | 2.4 |
  | SBI | 8.4 | – | – | 1.5 | – | – |

  Automated fair-value estimates are reference only.
- Avoid very high PE (80-120) when sales are negligible, typical of micro caps [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- Cheap plus a favourable stage covers 60-70% of the decision [uFFAAZNSozg].
- **Four-box grid** of valuation against past performance [Pm_1kb8Fku0]:
  - overvalued outperformers;
  - undervalued outperformers;
  - overvalued underperformers;
  - undervalued underperformers.

  His 2024 stance favoured undervalued underperformers with good businesses. His example was oil marketing companies at single-digit P/E.
- **Contrarian sector valuation calls:**
  - PSU banks as among the cheapest pockets [yEKNcJ4EH0U];
  - IT at March-2020 or GFC-level trailing valuations after a 36-40% fall [rbSj4pOg3_I, dpsU7JvHiMA, 0Hnffbj5pgE];
  - private banks near March-2020 levels [rbSj4pOg3_I];
  - he always warns that cheap can de-rate further [dpsU7JvHiMA, 0Hnffbj5pgE].
- **Value as a factor:** a Nifty 500 top-50 value index in a higher-high, higher-low structure with good relative strength, read as money moving toward "valuation comfort" [Oq4FjWEDbd0].

**How the two reconcile (my reading, not his stated rule)**
- The high-PE tolerance comes from O'Neil-style breakout and growth material.
- The value-aware material comes from bear-market, sector-rotation and long-term contexts.
- He does not explicitly reconcile them.

### 2.7 Sector checklists

**Banks**
- He tracks ROE, ROCE, NIM, CASA share and NPA, reviewed every quarter, to confirm his chosen banks remain the sector's best [Q-uCkMMTh5Q].
- SBI example: clean balance sheet, NPAs within norms, deposit growth, solid NIM, ROE about 17% at PE about 8.4.
- HDFC Bank example: 3-year ROE about 16-17% [BhQ_YnaRqa0].
- Sector backdrop [azzUmy2l_0U]:
  - gross NPA about 3.2%, down from an 11.2% peak;
  - credit growth 14-16% year on year;
  - better provision coverage.
- Risk he flags: EMI stress among 24-34-year-olds [rxqOUxO_vP4].

**Defence** [p5O_uspLu9k]
- Six questions, run by feeding annual reports into an AI assistant:
  1. policy alignment and strategic role;
  2. order book and revenue visibility;
  3. financial health (ROCE, ROE, profit growth, cash flow);
  4. exports and private or global partners;
  5. valuation against growth;
  6. execution and innovation.
- This narrows about 14 names to about 5.

**Financials themes** [azzUmy2l_0U]
- Over 15 crore demat accounts.
- Monthly SIP flows about ₹20,000 crore.
- Industry AUM grew from about ₹10 lakh crore to over ₹50 lakh crore.
- Insurance penetration 4.2%, expected to reach 6% by 2030.
- He calls these valuations fine but not cheap, and frames it as a multi-year theme.

**IT and AI** [NUDjIohACWs, 8QeR8FmYYg4]
- AI could expand IT margins and multiples over 2-3 years, but only after price structure confirms.
- Broader claim: AI lets companies work at under 20-25% of the cost, which he sees as a net positive for earnings.

### 2.8 New listings and IPOs

**Favourable view**
- Recent listings with low float, low ownership and exciting models are the fast movers in momentum phases (credited to Minervini and Ryan). Ten-year-plus companies mostly move with the market [-qQntPmfe-s].
- He prefers listings after September 2024 that fell 30-60%, some below issue price. He sees 2020-24 IPOs that already rose 5-20x as offering no long-term reward-to-risk [-qQntPmfe-s].
- Lynch filter: buy what you understand and use. He applied it to new-age names that fell 45-50% and then rebounded 25-30% [NtpyZH5cfaE, yEKNcJ4EH0U].
- Screen: double-digit growth, double-digit operating margin, low debt; expected to lead in 2025-26 [elpQ8wyLZLQ].
- He calls this an opportunity for about 5 years. Watch the IPO pipeline, listing gains, and new listings breaking out with rising relative strength [kwbfXjRtEkk].
- Do not chase every IPO; a 7-12 month hold for a decent return is fine [yEKNcJ4EH0U].

**Contrary view, depending on regime**
- When liquidity is scarce (he estimated it at about 20-25% "on"), avoid SME and IPO names with little revenue and high debt, and favour large and mid caps [20BxPDdAZAE].
- An IPO frenzy is mostly unfavourable to retail buyers [LWoEC9OjajE].

### 2.9 Market-level fundamentals (backdrop, not stock selection)

**Valuation readings**
- India forward P/E of 22-23x is neither cheap nor expensive against a 12x to 26-30x band [yYwiSDVwgyA].
- India's premium to emerging markets fell from about 88% to the high 20s or low 30s. He says stabilisation tends to come near 18-20% [SNrzU-z3RkE].

**FII return trigger** [SNrzU-z3RkE]
- FIIs need the top 300 companies to deliver fast double-digit growth.
- Large caps were growing in single digits, against about 28% median growth in small/mid caps.
- Nominal GDP of 11-13% is what suits large caps.

**Earnings readings**
- Median growth across 1,073 reporters: revenue 17%, PAT 25%, EPS 23%. 61% of companies grew profit through margin expansion [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- Other remarks:
  - best results in 15-24 months [SuYHqw5zA8U];
  - good quarters plus contained inflation [CFJNeCPMQ1o];
  - GDP of 7.8% [YFsvQXZZt1g].

**Inflation worry (judgment, no data)** [YFsvQXZZt1g]
- He guesses real inflation at 7-9% against headline CPI of 4.3-4.8%. He says this is not backed by data.

**Contrarian sentiment rule** [DCG1GO9TGFU]
- Worry when everyone is happy about earnings; worry less when everyone is concerned.

**Valuation debates**
- Small/mid valuations are defended by growth [0nbQs92kYuY] and he rejects the "India too expensive" view [YVGWKmpHStM].
- Yet he also lists inflated small/micro valuations as a 6-12 month risk [LWoEC9OjajE].

**Recovery confirmation** [20BxPDdAZAE]
- A genuine recovery needs improving fundamentals plus confidence, confirmed over 2-3 months.

### 2.10 Applied portfolio use

- **Income-planning example:** about ₹5 lakh in about 5 quality stocks showing strength, held for months. Gains come in lumps, so judge over 6 months or more [arFNgAPisHU].
- **Bear-market watchlist:** 5-10 high-quality companies on revenue growth, low debt and profitability [hpbDmEK-FvM]. The "SPREAD" check covers sales, profit, ROE and debt [ag4pL-OONoo]:
  - high sales and profit but low ROE: look for inefficient capital use;
  - high sales but low profit: suspect cost leakage;
  - high debt with good profit: risky if conditions change.

**Long versus short.** All stock-level material is long-side selection. There is no fundamental short screen. The only negative signals are:
- avoid or sell "dogs" [Jpu536_AmGc];
- promoter selling [WI0tdUjH_Vg];
- profit rising on shrinking margins [ag4pL-OONoo];
- no-revenue speculative names [T4849hhdwTo].

## 3. How a program would test it

1. **Quality overlay on chart entries.** Take his chart entries (stage-2 breakouts or stochastic pullbacks).
   - Compare trades with the screen against the unscreened set on return, maximum drawdown and win rate.
   - Screen variants: ROE and ROCE above 20%, D/E below 0.5, market cap above ₹5,000-10,000 crore; also the recent variant of profit growth above 25% with ROE/ROCE above 13%.
   - Data: point-in-time fundamentals (to avoid look-ahead), daily prices and a Nifty 500 universe.
2. **High PE versus low PE among breakouts.**
   - Among stocks making 52-week-high breakouts, compare forward 3-, 6- and 12-month returns by PE quintile, including PE above 70.
   - The claim is that high-PE breakouts do no worse. Condition on EPS growth.
   - Data: point-in-time trailing PE and EPS growth.
3. **Valuation × performance four box.**
   - Each quarter, split stocks by trailing 12-month return (above or below median) and by PE percentile within sector.
   - Compare forward 6-12 month returns and drawdowns.
   - The claim is that undervalued underperformers have the best reward-to-risk in some regimes.
4. **Growth-quality composite.**
   - Composite: 3-year sales CAGR above 20%, 3-year profit CAGR above sales CAGR, operating margin expanding year on year, ROE above 15% and above industry median, positive free cash flow, D/E below 0.5.
   - Compare against stocks failing at least one criterion over 12-24 months.
   - Also test the sub-claim that year-on-year growth predicts forward returns better than quarter-on-quarter growth.
   - Data: annual and quarterly statements plus cash-flow statements.
5. **Absolute versus industry-relative debt filter.**
   - Compare an absolute D/E below 0.5 rule with D/E below the industry median, holding the other filters fixed.
   - Measure bear-market drawdown and forward returns.
6. **Recent-IPO rebound.**
   - Stocks listed 1-3 years, at least 40% below their listing-period high, that then form a base and break out with relative strength rising against the Nifty 500.
   - Compare with seasoned stocks over 3-12 months.
   - Condition on a liquidity-regime proxy (for example small-cap breadth, IPO issuance or rate trend) to test his tight-liquidity warning.
7. **Institutional ownership as crash protection.**
   - Compare the frequency of drawdowns above 40% in 6 months between stocks above and below the 15% (large-cap) or 10% (mid/small-cap) ownership cutoffs.
   - Separately, test net promoter selling over a quarter against 6-12 month forward returns.
8. **India premium to EM.**
   - When India's forward P/E premium to MSCI EM falls below about 20%, check 12-month relative returns against EM.
   - Data: monthly forward P/E series.
   - The sample of episodes is small, so treat any result as weak.

## 4. Transfer

**Usable as is in the US and on the TSX**
- The fundamentals-then-chart order.
- Sales and profit growth logic: year-on-year over quarter-on-quarter, profit growing faster than sales, margin expansion, organic over acquired growth.
- ROE quality checks against free cash flow, one-offs and leverage.
- Industry-relative debt and the good/bad debt framing.
- PEG and PE relative to industry.
- The four-box valuation × performance grid.
- The O'Neil-style high-PE tolerance.
- Contrarian earnings-sentiment framing.
- These are largely O'Neil, Lynch and Minervini ideas he has repackaged.

**Needs adapting**
- **Absolute thresholds** were tuned to Indian conditions. Recalibrate the 6-7% ROE floor and the 18-25% growth cutoffs against US and TSX distributions.
- **Market-cap cutoffs:** rescale ₹5,000 and ₹10,000 crore (roughly $0.6-1.2B USD at current rates; check this).
- **Sector norms:** the TSX is heavy in banks, energy and materials, so cyclicals and commodity producers will fail growth and margin screens through the cycle.
- **Ownership:** promoter holding maps imperfectly to insider and controlling ownership. Use Form 4 / SEDI insider filings and 13F for institutional ownership. US institutional ownership is far higher, so the 10-15% floors are non-binding.
- **Bank checklist:** replace CASA with deposit mix or cost of deposits, and NPA with net charge-offs and non-accruals.
- **Benchmark:** replace the Nifty 500 with the S&P 500, Russell 1000/2000 or the S&P/TSX Composite.
- **Value factor:** the Nifty 500 Value 50 index maps to Russell or S&P value indices.
- **IPO ideas:** the US has a deeper IPO and SPAC history for testing. The TSX has few growth IPOs, so this is likely weak there.

**India-only**
- GST, RBI and CPI commentary.
- The India-versus-EM premium.
- The FII return thesis.
- Demat, SIP and AMC penetration theses.
- PSU banks, defence-policy alignment (Make in India) and the SME segment.
- The specific liquidity read.
- Screener.in workflows; any US or Canadian screener with point-in-time data is the substitute.

## 5. Gaps and doubts

- **Thresholds drift:** ROE/ROCE floors range from 6-7% to 20-25% across videos, and growth floors from 6-7% to over 25%. There is no single stated rule. The recent video gives 25% profit growth and 13-14% ROE/ROCE [lRUDp336l7Q]. The 1-year-old deep dive [ag4pL-OONoo] gives 15%+ ROE and 20-25% sales CAGR.
- **Valuation stance is unresolved:** "ignore high PE" [KRoie4cj5TQ, N5bb0Cu8RAs] sits against "cheap + stage is 60-70% of the decision" [uFFAAZNSozg] and "undervalued underperformers" [Pm_1kb8Fku0]. There are no stated valuation cutoffs except PEG as a concept.
- **Caption problems:**
  - PEG above 2 is likely reversed [2Yv0E57_bs4];
  - the 133% sales figure [ag4pL-OONoo];
  - the upper D/E bands [ag4pL-OONoo];
  - "operating margin higher double digits" is not quantified.
- **Contradictions on IPOs and small caps:** new listings are favoured [-qQntPmfe-s, kwbfXjRtEkk, elpQ8wyLZLQ] yet IPO/SME names are warned off in tight liquidity [20BxPDdAZAE]. Small/mid valuations are both defended [0nbQs92kYuY] and flagged as a risk [LWoEC9OjajE]. The regime switch (his liquidity estimate) is not defined in a codable way.
- **Missing detail:**
  - several videos defer to other videos without details [Jpu536_AmGc, kkJ4m9JQ4pM, hwOw2HaLUcQ, WI0tdUjH_Vg];
  - no cash-flow thresholds;
  - no fundamental exit rule, such as selling on an earnings miss or margin decline;
  - no rule for handling results-day gaps;
  - no short-side fundamental screen;
  - no guidance on how earnings dates interact with entries.
- **Hindsight risk:** the multibagger traits [Bqp9jKQCs30] and the unnamed example company [ag4pL-OONoo] are hindsight descriptions with survivorship risk.
- **Market calls are unverified opinions:** India valuation, GST, inflation, AI margins and sector theses (IT recovery timing [mim7ylyaN74], financials [azzUmy2l_0U]) should be logged as dated calls and checked, not used as rules. He himself labels the inflation view as judgment without data [YFsvQXZZt1g].
- **Claimed percentages are untested:** the 85-90% screen-out rate, the 60-70% decision share, and the 18-20% premium stabilisation zone come from his experience only.
