# Madhav's method, as written on 17 Oct 2026

## His words

> **Options trading strategy.** First 30 mins: I want to see market direction and proof in that 20–30 mins to clear the noise and for direction proof. Sometimes I guess here and bet on one direction. I use scalper for entries and exit according to our scenario and market, for intraday and deliveries; they give good for scalper. Theta and delta values are important. A bit risky, but if we could guess the direction and bet less, like 1–2 lots in Nifty options, there are good days and bad days.
>
> Looking for days like after a long consecutive market in loss, looking for saturation points to see if there are chances in either direction. Even after long buyings, looking for reversal. Also riding with pullbacks. I guess our YouTube classes should help with this.
>
> **Every day:** conversation with AI about best stocks to trade, mostly with Claude, to analyse the previous market and news and know about buyers' and sellers' domination, and analysis on next-day movements.
>
> **Expiry-day option trading strategy**, especially on Nifty Bank: looking for market direction and, if there is strong movement and prediction in either direction, bet a small lot accordingly.
>
> **Stock selection** is based on fundamentals like brand value and future scope, leadership quality. I am looking for standard here in buying and seeing for long-term returns rather than short term. Whenever we sense reversal, that is when we change positioning and wait, back to enter at good places, back to maximise profits: like riding along rather than just sitting.
>
> We keep track of stocks which are in saturation after long ups or long downs. We will predict what happens next and see if there is a place for us to enter with proper stop-loss setups in case the market moves in the opposite direction: a stop-loss with enough space to breathe. This stop-loss space is one important thing; we have to explore it from our historical market research.
>
> We divide stocks based on their effectors: some news affects bank stocks a lot, but still some stocks do good; some stocks are affected more by fuel prices; some more by quarterly results; some stocks wait for some news to break out in either direction (we have to always keep looking at them and for them).
>
> For IPOs as well, we check for demand, quality, and evaluate our chances, and check whether booking is good or entry on the first day is, or whether it is overpriced. We always keep an eye out for mispriced stocks too.
>
> It's like every trading decision is based on so many gates, but the key is to find which gate to check depending on the current state and status of the stock, market, volume, demand, future scope and prediction analysis. But if we just build logic to pass through gates, Ananta will just be a filter rather than a brain deciding the best possibilities in the given scene.
>
> **For my portfolio:** we start with analysing the current portfolio and ranking it, providing me reasons and suggestions, changing based on approvals; and we start with paper on options and futures using ₹50,000 capital, and analyse, modify and update its performance.

## The design rule in it

"Which gate to check depends on the scene" is the rule the rest is built on. Ananta first names the scene a stock or the index is in, then weighs the evidence that matters in that scene and builds a case, and only then decides; the record keeps the case (what was weighed, with what weight, and why), not a pass/fail list. The scenes he names:

| Scene | What he looks for | Evidence that matters in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening noise (first 20–30 minutes) | Proof of direction before any bet; sometimes a guess | Index 5-minute bars, breadth of the first bars, VIX, overnight news, FII/DII of the prior day |
| Saturation after a long decline | A turning point in either direction, entered with a stop that can breathe | Length and depth of the decline, volume and delivery drying up or climaxing, zones below, prior reversals' shape |
| Saturation after a long rise | Reversal risk; reposition rather than sit | The same, mirrored; distribution signs; breadth divergence |
| Pullback inside a trend | Ride it | Trend state, pullback depth versus the stop space, volume on the pullback |
| Expiry day (index, Nifty Bank especially) | Strong directional movement with a prediction, then a small lot | Chain: OI build-up and unwinding by strike, theta and delta, VIX, time of day |
| Effector events | Which group moves on which news | Group map: banks on policy news, fuel-sensitive names on crude, results-driven names on quarters, news-waiting names on their catalyst |
| IPO | Demand, quality, pricing; book, first-day entry or wait | Subscription data, grey-market and listing-day behaviour, valuation versus peers |
| Mispricing | A standard name trading away from its worth | Fundamentals versus price, relative strength, flows |
| Portfolio review | Rank holdings; reasons; suggestions; act on approval | The ledger, regime, sector reads, the holding's own scene |

## What this changes in the India plan

1. A third teacher in the library and in hypothesis_map: the M-series above, beside TWT and Optionables.
2. The decision record stores the scene and the case, not only the gates walked (the "filter, not a brain" warning): a scene column and a weighed-evidence list per decision; the knowledge map tags each hypothesis with the scenes it applies to.
3. Two new research families with their own registrations: the ₹50,000 paper options-and-futures book (M-17; Madhav decides, Ananta proposes and records, nothing executes), and the daily India brief as a scored call (M-11), both inside L0.
4. The first registered India study is the stop-space study (M-06), because every other scene depends on a stop that can breathe.
5. Two datasets added to the lake list: IPO subscription and listing data (M-14), and a fundamentals source behind gate G-U (M-15).
6. The expiry-day family must carry the current expiry calendar. As of the January 2026 series: Nifty 50 lot 65 (from 75), Bank Nifty lot 30 (from 35); Bank Nifty weekly expiries ended in November 2024 (SEBI's October 2024 circular), so Bank Nifty expires monthly on the last Tuesday, and Nifty 50 is the NSE index with a weekly expiry, on Tuesdays (sources in the 17 Oct chat; confirmed against NSE contract specifications before the family is registered). A ₹50,000 book therefore holds one or two Nifty lots or one Bank Nifty lot at most, with premium at risk per lot measured before any paper bet.

## Adopted from an outside review (ChatGPT, shared by Madhav on 17 Oct 2026)

The review restated the method cleanly and agreed with the architecture already built; these points were taken into the registrations because they sharpen a test or a rule:

1. The 20–30-minute mark is a decision checkpoint, never an entry time; NO TRADE is a valid outcome, and so is "wait longer".
2. Evidence is counted by family (price structure, participation, acceptance, context, options chain), one vote each, never several versions of the same price signal.
3. The daily brief (M-11) writes bullish, bearish and neutral scenarios before the open, with the proof condition each would need; the checkpoint records which came true. That makes the brief falsifiable.
4. Stop placement and position size are tested together (M-06): when the stop that can breathe makes the loss too large, the answer is to skip, never to tighten the stop.
5. An option-premium stop and an index stop are different things (theta, gamma, IV); the paper book (M-17) carries both and records which one fired.
6. The ₹50,000 book's limits are set and approved before the first paper trade: loss per trade, loss per day, open risk, drawdown response; rejected trades are recorded beside taken ones; performance is read by setup and regime, never as one total.
7. Riding-and-re-entry (M-12) is measured against simply holding, after costs, taxes and missed upside.
8. Every stock carries two separate assessments, company quality and entry timing, never one score.
9. A probability is stated only from a calibrated set of comparable past setups; otherwise Ananta says "uncalibrated".

Not adopted: historical option bid–ask spreads, strike-level liquidity and intraday implied volatility are not in the free F&O files, so fills and Greeks in the paper book are computed from settlement prices and a labelled spread estimate, as Canada's C0 does; GIFT Nifty and grey-market figures are context at best; the review's phase order puts the portfolio review last, ours puts it first because it is read-only analysis on the ledger and needs no research to be useful.
