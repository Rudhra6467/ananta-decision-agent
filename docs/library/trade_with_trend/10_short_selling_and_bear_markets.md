# Short selling and bear markets

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits his method

Raunak A's position on shorting changed over time. In his older videos (about 6–8 years ago) he teaches short setups as mirror images of his long setups. These cover Heikin Ashi initiation candles, Ichimoku cloud breakdowns, relative-strength (RS) weakness, ADX/20-EMA pullbacks, kickers, coil springs and gap-downs. Each comes with trend filters: price below the 100-, 150- or 200-day average, Stage 4, and a falling index. Even then he warned that shorting is harder than buying, that risk-reward is usually poor, and that beginners should stay long or use puts.

In the recent videos (about 2 weeks to 2 years ago) the message is firmly long-only. He says shorting rarely works, by his estimate 1–2 times in 10. He says it breeds overtrading and blinds you to the next RS leaders. His advice for corrections is defensive (cut longs) and preparatory (build a watchlist), not short selling.

So "bear markets" in his method are mainly a signal to reduce long exposure and research the next leaders. The short rules survive mostly as older material and as regime filters.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Overall stance: long-only, shorting discouraged (the dominant recent teaching)

**What he says**
- Do not short the market.
- During sharp falls, the right move is to cut losing positions and some profitable long positions, not to go short [d3-OMTGOGx8].
- Treat corrections as preparation for the next upswing. Chasing the next 2–3% on the downside damages your psychology [elpQ8wyLZLQ].
- He calls shorts and option writing during corrections traps that pay "pennies". He prefers to research the best stocks and hold them in reserve until the market stabilises [20BxPDdAZAE].
- He stays long-only because he expects the eventual reversal to be sudden and to catch people off guard [KDSbZ0oW2U0].
- Citing William O'Neill, he argues that:
  - markets look for reasons to rise;
  - a short-selling mindset stops you spotting RS leaders;
  - few people can run longs and shorts at the same time [rhiXs5Kh_0g].
- Below the 50- and 200-day averages, shorting is the only trading play. He still avoids discussing it because it requires futures and options (F&O), which he never encourages [Sb6KTt64YKE].

**His success-rate claims (they differ)**

| Claim | Videos |
|---|---|
| Shorts work about 2 in 10 | [RVbze1duD-U, 8y; d3-OMTGOGx8, 1y] |
| Shorts work about 1 in 10 | [T4849hhdwTo, 2y] |

No data is shown for either figure.

**Older caveats pointing the same way** [uFFAAZNSozg, 8y]
- Risk-reward on shorts is usually unfavourable.
- His typical short trade runs 11–16%.
- He would not short a stock that has already fallen about 78%.
- Beginners should apply his tools to long trades only. Anyone who wants downside exposure should use put options.

**Clarity:** high and repeated across six or more videos. This is his firmest position in the chapter.

### 2.2 Regime and stage filters: when a short is even allowed

**Stock-level filters** [G2gjQUJRfJM]
- Short only in Stage 4, with price clearly below the 150-day average.
- Never short in Stage 2 or above the 150-day average.

**Filters stated in other videos (they do not agree)**

| Filter | Video |
|---|---|
| Price below the 100-day average | [H0XAVFBdbzw] (low clarity) |
| Market below the 200-day average (DMA) before running the reversed RS process | [488FDla8kuc] |
| Below both the 50- and 200-day averages | [Sb6KTt64YKE] |

**Market-level filters**
- The broader index trend must be down, and broad-market RS should confirm [RVbze1duD-U].
- Bearish patterns should be traded only in a confirmed downtrend [VqoLpEvre04].

**Do-not-short list**
- Do not short on high P/E alone; valuations can get more expensive [G2gjQUJRfJM].
- Do not short merely because a stock ran up a lot short-term [G2gjQUJRfJM].
- Do not short after an extended decline (about 78% in his example) [uFFAAZNSozg].
- Do not short or write calls near an intermediate bottom [-qQntPmfe-s, 5 months ago] when all of these hold:
  - the bearish view is consensus;
  - price is near known support;
  - volatility (VIX) is elevated.

  In that situation he says reward-to-risk favours the long side over about 6 months, even though momentum can still push prices lower. He cites call writers losing heavily on a sharp rebound.

**Clarity:** the 150-day/Stage 4 rule is stated clearly. Stage 4 itself is not defined anywhere in these notes. The moving-average length varies by video (100, 150, 200, or 50+200).

### 2.3 Heikin Ashi short rules: initiation, strength, management

**Entry condition**
- Short only after wide-range bearish initiation candles appear at the start of the down move [L99LgSdCDD8].
- A decline made of narrow, weak bearish candles with no initiation candle is a weak downtrend. Do not short it, however many continuation candles follow.

**Why he treats shorts more strictly than longs** [L99LgSdCDD8]
- A short needs strength visible immediately, and confirmation must be checked at each stage.
- A long can tolerate confirmation arriving 2–3 candles late, because demand can come in late.

**His picture of a strong downtrend** [L99LgSdCDD8]
1. The first three candles are wide-range initiation candles.
2. Continuation candles follow steadily, with no indecision candles.
3. The first indecision candles appear only after a 5–7% decline.
4. Price then consolidates sideways.

**Reading supply** [L99LgSdCDD8]
- Judge two things together: wide bearish candles, and narrow or indecision bullish candles on the rebounds.
- In his example, rebounds came on narrow candles and the decline resumed on fresh wide bearish candles.

**Counter-trend candles** [IHqkSPVCxko]
- React to them only if they are wide-range and confirm a decisive shift.
- Ignore small indecision candles inside a downtrend.
- Down moves tend to come on wider candles, so wait for confirmation before shorting.

**Index example** [e34G88-BKI4, 6y]
1. Consider shorting Bank Nifty only below 31,600.
2. Require strong downside initiation candles on the weekly chart.
3. Enter on a weak retracement back up, not on the initial drop.

**Trade management** [uqEFppHC4Jw]
- A red Heikin Ashi candle with no upper shadow marks a strong downtrend.
- Stay in, do not book profit, consider adding to the short, and exit longs.
- Weak, small-range red candles did not follow through.

**Clarity:** high and consistent across four videos. The terms "wide" and "narrow" are never quantified.

### 2.4 Ichimoku short (Kumo breakdown)

**Main rule** [2crnCCz4Y3s]
- Price is below the cloud.
- Both Senkou A and Senkou B are falling.
- No short while the cloud lines are flat or a twist is just forming.
- Beginners should wait until the twist is complete and both lines are clearly heading down. Experienced traders may short earlier.

**Confirmation** [2crnCCz4Y3s]
- Chikou is below price.
- Tenkan and Kijun are both trending lower.
- Otherwise, avoid the trade.

**Full bearish alignment** [153pJZk-ixo]
- Price below the cloud.
- Senkou A below Senkou B.
- Price below Tenkan and Kijun.
- Chikou below price.
- With this alignment, avoid buying and prefer short-side trades.

**Intraday stock shorts** [xzR6bwNgb18]
- Use 10- or 15-minute charts.
- Avoid the 5-minute chart.

**Clarity:** high and codable.

### 2.5 Relative-strength shorting

- **Sector process reversed** [488FDla8kuc]: with the market below its 200 DMA, take the weakest sectors from the RS ranking, then short the weakest-RS stocks within them.
- **Stock setup** [RVbze1duD-U]: lower highs in price plus lower highs in the RS line. Requires a falling market trend and confirming broad-market RS.

**Clarity:** medium. No ranking method, lookback period or exit is given in these notes.

### 2.6 Pullback entries into a downtrend

**ADX/20-EMA mirror setup** [LahSe1vu1kY], daily chart:
1. ADX(14) is above 30 and −DMI is above +DMI.
2. Price is below the 20-day EMA and rallies back up to it.
3. Mark the low of the candle that touches the 20 EMA.
4. Short when that low breaks, on the next day or in a following session.
5. Place the stop above a swing high:
   - conservative trader: the nearest swing high;
   - aggressive trader: the larger major swing high.

**Clarity:** high; fully codable.

**Fibonacci resistance** [d7cwBnW78XI]
- In a down-swing, a rally to the 50% retracement tends to act as resistance; sellers eventually win.
- Once the 50% area gives way, price moves on steadily.
- 38.2% or 61.8% may also serve.
- He gives no entry trigger in these notes.

**Stochastic** [H0XAVFBdbzw]: short with Stochastic above 70 and price below the 100-day average. Low clarity, and no worked short example.

**Weak retracement after a weekly breakdown:** see the Bank Nifty example [e34G88-BKI4] in 2.3.

### 2.7 Pattern-based shorts

**Bearish kicker** [QWcb7yxYAxk]
1. Context: an uptrend.
2. Candle one is a strong bullish candle.
3. Candle two opens near candle one's low and falls with a strong bearish body.
4. Short by the close of candle two. Stop at candle one's high.

**Inverted coil spring (distribution)** [VqoLpEvre04]
- He calls it rare. Trade it only in a confirmed downtrend.
- Entry: a break of the A-C-E support line, or the wide-range Heikin Ashi candles after the breakdown. Add on those wide candles.
- Target: entry minus the A-B distance. His example: entry 8300, A-B about 300, target about 8000.
- In strong momentum he expects 2–3× the A-B distance.
- Book out if short-term momentum becomes excessive.

**Gap-down with RSI in its bearish range** [HHkcCy5LrCM]
- Look for gap-downs coming out of an RSI that has been moving between about 20 and 60, capped near 60.
- Gap-downs that come after RSI in the 40–80 range tended to fail or fill.

**Clarity:** the kicker is clear. The coil spring is medium. The gap-down rule is medium; the trigger and stop are not specified in the notes.

## 3. How a program would test it

**1. Trend filter (150-day).**
- Statement: among mechanical short signals in stocks, those with close below a falling 150-day SMA beat those with close above it.
- Measure: 10-, 20- and 60-day forward returns and R-multiples.
- Data: daily OHLC for a broad universe with delisted names; borrow-cost estimates.
- Stage 4 needs an operational definition, for example price below a 150-day SMA that has been declining for at least 20 sessions.

**2. Heikin Ashi initiation filter.**
- Statement: shorts entered after at least one wide-range bearish HA candle at the start of the down leg beat shorts taken in down legs made only of narrow candles.
- "Wide" could mean HA body ≥ 1.5× the 20-bar median body. Test how sensitive results are to this threshold.
- Secondary test: after two or more consecutive red HA candles with no upper shadow, 5-day forward returns are below the unconditional average.
- Data: daily and weekly OHLC.

**3. Ichimoku alignment.**
- Statement: shorts with all four bearish conditions plus falling Senkou A and B beat shorts on "price below cloud" alone.
- Standard settings: 9/26/52.
- Repeat on 5-, 10- and 15-minute bars to test his preference for 10/15 minutes over 5 minutes.
- Data: daily and intraday bars, with costs and slippage.

**4. ADX/20-EMA pullback short.**
- Rules: ADX(14) > 30, −DMI > +DMI, a close below the 20 EMA, then a bar whose high touches the 20 EMA. Short on a break of that bar's low within N sessions. Stop above the nearest swing high (and, separately, the major swing high).
- Measure: expectancy in R, for both stop variants.

**5. RS sector short.**
- Rules: with the index below its 200 DMA, short the bottom-decile RS stocks in the bottom-quintile sectors.
- Measure: monthly-rebalanced returns versus the benchmark.
- Data: sector classifications as they stood at each date.

**6. Elevated-volatility trap.**
- Statement: shorts or short calls opened with VIX in its top 20% and price within X% of a prior pivot low perform worse over 1–6 months than the same signals in normal VIX away from support.
- Data: the relevant VIX series, pivot detection, and options data for the call-writing variant.

**7. His meta-claim.**
- Statement: mechanical shorts win about 10–20% of the time and underperform long-only versions of the same signal over full cycles.
- Report win rate and expectancy separately. A low win rate does not by itself imply negative expectancy.

**8. Bearish kicker.**
- Rules: shorts at candle-two close with a stop at candle one's high.
- Measure: mean R; also condition on the trend filter from statement 1.

## 4. Transfer to TSX and US markets

**Applies as it is**
- The price logic: Heikin Ashi initiation, Ichimoku, ADX/EMA, Fibonacci, kicker, RSI ranges and moving-average regime filters.
- The "don't short on valuation or run-up alone" rules.
- The long-only defensive stance (cut longs, build a watchlist).

**Instruments**
- US and Canadian equities can be shorted overnight in cash accounts with borrow, so his India-driven F&O objection weakens.
- Borrow fees, hard-to-borrow lists and recall risk must be modelled, especially for small TSX names.
- Puts, which he suggests for beginners, are liquid on large US names but thin on many TSX stocks.

**Needs adapting**
- **Index levels:** Bank Nifty 31,600 is India-only. Use structural equivalents such as a weekly close below a prior breakout level on the S&P 500, Nasdaq or S&P/TSX Composite.
- **Sector RS:**
  - The TSX is concentrated in financials, energy and materials, so "weakest sector" shorts become heavy commodity bets.
  - The TSX universe is small, so deciles are thin. A combined US+Canada universe may be needed.
- **Volatility gauge:** India VIX maps to VIX (US) or VIXC (S&P/TSX 60).
- **Index futures:** ES/NQ for the US; SXF on the Montréal Exchange for Canada.
- **Intraday Ichimoku:** US intraday short rules (uptick rule under SSR) matter more than Indian intraday-only cash shorting.

**India-only**
- Cash-market shorting being intraday only, which drives his F&O framing.
- The specific index levels.
- His "never encourage F&O" stance as a response to the Indian retail context.

## 5. Gaps and doubts

**Contradictions over time**
- Older videos teach detailed short setups; recent videos say do not short at all.
- He never reconciles this. It is unclear whether he thinks the older setups lack edge or only that they are unsuitable for his audience.

**Inconsistent numbers**
- Success rate: 2 in 10 versus 1 in 10. No sample, period or definition of "works" is given.
- Regime moving average: 100, 150, 200, or 50+200 days, depending on the video.

**Undefined terms**
- Stage 4 is required [G2gjQUJRfJM] but not defined in these notes.
- "Wide" and "narrow" Heikin Ashi candles are never quantified.
- "Confirmed downtrend" and "intermediate bottom" are judgment calls.

**Missing rules**
- Exits are missing for most setups. Only these have stops or targets: ADX/EMA, kicker, coil spring, and HA management. Ichimoku, RS and Fibonacci shorts have no stop or target in the notes.
- Position sizing for shorts, short-squeeze risk, borrow cost and gap risk are not covered.

**Thin "defence" material**
- It amounts to: cut losers and some winners, avoid shorting and option writing, and build a watchlist.
- No rules are given for:
  - how much exposure to cut, or when;
  - bear-market detection beyond moving averages;
  - re-entry after a bear market;
  - hedging with puts beyond a one-line suggestion.

**Unsupported claims**
- The low-clarity Stochastic/100-day rule [H0XAVFBdbzw] has no worked example.
- The 6-month long-side reward-to-risk claim near bottoms [-qQntPmfe-s] is an assertion without shown data.
