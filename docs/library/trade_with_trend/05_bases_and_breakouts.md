# Bases and breakouts

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits

Raunak A's core long method is to buy strong stocks and sectors as they leave a proper base. Price is expected to move in a stair-step: trend, then pause or consolidation, then a fresh breakout. The breakout should be confirmed by a strong candle, expanded volume, rising relative strength (RS) and a supportive market. He cites William O'Neil, Mark Minervini and Stan Weinstein as the sources of most of this and says it is their teaching, not his [sIo5XQqZ0b0, Oq4FjWEDbd0, 1jsLESmVLVU].

His teaching changed over time:
- **Older videos (roughly 5 to 15 years ago).** He approached ranges and breakouts through other toolkits: Wyckoff phases, Heikin Ashi "initiation" candles, Ichimoku cloud breakouts, Market Profile value areas, Renko ranges, DeMark (TD) trendlines, gaps and opening-range breakouts.
- **Recent videos (the last 1 to 3 years).** These converge on a small set of structures: VCP and contractions, flags, cup and handle, and bases near the 50-day or 30-week average. They add a growing emphasis on market regime. Breakouts fail in broken markets, so he prefers pullbacks or retests and scaled entries.

Everything below is his claim or method, to be treated as a hypothesis.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 The stair-step structure: trend, pause, breakout; bases on bases

**What he teaches**
- Trends rarely run 0 to 100 at once. They advance, pause, retest and advance again; he compares it to trekking uphill with rests [1jsLESmVLVU].
- He calls this "tennis ball action": a strong move, a shallow pullback, then higher again [mim7ylyaN74].
- A second breakout from a consolidation inside an established uptrend is a strong-trend signal to enter and hold. This applies when no bearish candle showing supply appears after it [eeKRfAbbvZA].
- Bases forming on top of earlier bases are a positive sign (O'Neil). His example is the micro-cap index, which went sideways for about 3 months while building new bases [jcWYGNInYg8].
- After a breakout, volume should settle near average. The next base should again show contracting volume before the next breakout [N5bb0Cu8RAs].
- Several stages can occur on one stock over time, for example a contraction, then a flag, then a breakout [VET4JHrc2vU].

**Contradiction: first base versus later bases**
- In one video he wants to buy as the stock leaves its first base. Buying after a 120–130% advance from a later base is poor risk/reward to him, even if the stock keeps rising [mR0HMssJek8].
- In another, he favourably presents KPIT forming a second base when it was already about 100–120% above its 52-week low. He argues stocks that look too high keep rising while the broad trend is strong [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- He does not resolve the two.

**How often:** a recurring theme, with medium-to-high clarity.

### 2.2 Volatility contraction (VCP) and tightness

**What it is**
- A strong trending stock consolidates in progressively tighter contractions, then breaks out on a volume spike [1jsLESmVLVU].
- He says he has preferred this to cup and handle since about 2013–14. He applies it to stocks and index futures.

**Measurable versions he gives**
- **Successively shallower pullbacks:**
  - Nifty Bank: about −12%, −9%, −8.7%.
  - Metals: −36%, −24%, −11%, then a breakout.
  - Realty: a contraction, then a breakout and a retest [Y4TewQHVhvc].
  - SBI: three contractions, a breakout in Q4 2023, then consolidation at the breakout level [BhQ_YnaRqa0].
- **Narrowing daily candle range:** an explosive move follows, more often in the direction of the prevailing trend, though it sometimes backfires [g3mcqf6rqoc].
- **Higher lows inside a range while range contracts:** he reports names flagged this way moved about 8–12% in 7–8 days. This is his own selected report [g3mcqf6rqoc].
- **Inside bars:** two or more consecutive narrow inside days precede a wide-range break. He treats this as context, not an entry [LahSe1vu1kY].
- **Mother candle:** a wide "mother" candle followed by smaller candles inside its range. He also calls this a VCP [CXJZMbE_d5o, mRAndF9wyaA, Y4TewQHVhvc]; see 2.6.

**Duration varies widely**
- Weeks for stock VCPs.
- About 15 months (India manufacturing index) and about 1.5 years (pharma) for sector contractions [SuYHqw5zA8U, 0Hnffbj5pgE].
- 3–4 years for some stocks [V41BUpPK3E4].
- He says longer contractions should not be judged over days [SuYHqw5zA8U].

**Textbook strictness:** he accepts patterns that only look similar but share the same psychology, such as rounding bases and long contractions [mR0HMssJek8].

**Loose versus tight:** gold was called a "loose base" needing tighter action [jcWYGNInYg8].

**Missing:** no rule is given for the number of contractions, maximum depth or minimum tightness. The notes repeatedly reference other videos for definitions [KRoie4cj5TQ].

**How often:** taught or cited in roughly 20 videos [also mim7ylyaN74, T4849hhdwTo, kwbfXjRtEkk, rbSj4pOg3_I, uw5QQOenJnQ, S--7ta4FmbY, r63sarc_qV8, sIo5XQqZ0b0]. It is his most-cited recent structure.

### 2.3 Where the base sits: 50-DMA, 30-week MA, 200-DMA

**Anchors by trader type**
- Positional traders should want a base or contraction near the 50-DMA. Investors should want one near the 200-DMA. He credits Livermore, O'Neil and Weinstein [Oq4FjWEDbd0].

**The template [Oq4FjWEDbd0]**
- A contraction at the 50-DMA.
- RS strong or flat over the prior 4–5 months.
- Entry as price moves above the contraction high, accepting that it can fail.

**Distance limit [S--7ta4FmbY]**
- Buy within about 3–5% of the 50-DMA.
- Do not buy once price is 10% above it.

**Weekly-chart version**
- A base whipsawing in and around the 30-week MA, then a clean breakout [NUDjIohACWs].
  - Mastek was his good example.
  - A base below the 30-week MA (Tech Mahindra) drew a caveat.
  - A stock with no base (Mphasis) is avoided.
- For sector indices: a tight range at a rising 30-week MA with RS holding [lRUDp336l7Q, CFJNeCPMQ1o].
  - He also cites two consecutive weekly closes above the 30-week MA plus a DeMark trendline break [lRUDp336l7Q].
- These setups "take time" and suit patient holders [CFJNeCPMQ1o].
- He equates the 30-week MA with about the 200-day [kwbfXjRtEkk]. Thirty weeks is closer to 150 trading days, so that equivalence is loose.

**Index tightness**
- Weeks of tight range above the 30-week MA in an index is rare and often precedes a large move [kwbfXjRtEkk].
- The **Bank Nifty contraction at the 50-DMA** is his live test, and it failed:
  - He read it as a classic contraction expected to resolve up.
  - It broke below its pivot on a crude-driven sell-off.
  - He says the structure read was right and the timing wrong [r63sarc_qV8, rhiXs5Kh_0g].
  - A later video again describes Bank Nifty tight near its average, with a breakout area of about 58,000–58,800 [YFsvQXZZt1g]. The caption shows a 30-period average where he meant 50.

**Older weekly variant:** price above the 50-week average, with both price and the RS line clearing multi-year resistance (Reliance in April 2017, about a 3-year base) [Ux_uuOj-gKs].

**How often:** very frequent in videos from the last 1–2 years, with medium-high clarity. It is still discretionary about what "near" and "tight" mean, except for the 3–5% / 10% rule.

### 2.4 Cup and handle, flags, high tight flag, W bottom

**Cup and handle [1jsLESmVLVU, mim7ylyaN74]**
- A rounded cup, then a small handle, then a breakout on a clear volume spike.
- Watch volume as the cup's legs form.
- He enters at the breakout [1jsLESmVLVU].
- It works on daily charts, not only weekly [mim7ylyaN74].
- On 30-minute charts he reads a deeper retracement followed by a shallower one as strengthening demand. He says not to rely on textbook definitions [Tkx2xIboYsw].
- On broad-index weekly charts (Midcap 100, Nifty 500), a validated cup and handle is a reason to look for buying in strong pockets. Validation does not mean immediate momentum; the index can consolidate for months [yEKNcJ4EH0U].
- He gives **no depth, duration or handle rules**. One video uses cup and handle only as a process-focus illustration [NYWbbvaJnPc].

**Flag / flat base [1jsLESmVLVU]**
- An uptrend, then a sideways pause where weak holders exit, then a breakout.
- He says professionals buy near the breakout, not inside the pause.
- Example: IndusInd consolidated about 12–13 days, then moved about 4%.
- An index breaking out of a flag means: scan its constituents for the same pattern [q26J58h5Qt8].

**High tight flag:** a breakout within 3–5 weekly candles (O'Neil) [dERi8MUU5es].

**W / double bottom (O'Neil style)**
- Confirmed only on a break of the middle pivot [d3-OMTGOGx8, L-PGsMh2vHw].
- Nifty 500 pivot near 21,900, next zone about 22,900–23,300. He noted the index was still making lower highs and lower lows.
- Volume should rise on advances and shrink on declines [L-PGsMh2vHw].

**Other cited forms**
- Ascending triangle, horizontal flag, ascending VCP, and a double bottom at the first 50-DMA pullback after a cup and handle. These were members' examples, not rules [T4849hhdwTo].
- A cup and handle inside a larger flag on Nifty, triggered 14 Feb 2024 [Pm_1kb8Fku0].

**Hindsight warning:** a watchlist of about 30 names built from cup/VCP, volume and RS reportedly returned about 50% to over 1,000%. This was hand-picked and the captions are garbled [Lw6h6T30VvE].

### 2.5 Wyckoff accumulation (older, most detailed base teaching)

This is taught clearly in one long video, with a follow-up [K06GqbJwEv4, tTdy5srpW9Q]. The context is a range after a momentum-driven downtrend. His only tools are candle spread and volume.

**Phase A**
- Preliminary support (PS): a wide bullish candle on high volume, often tailed.
- Selling climax (SC): a bearish candle with a volume spike.
- Automatic rally (AR): back to about the PS area.
- Range top: a line from the **close of the PS candle**. Range bottom: the SC low area.
- A more intense climax makes the lows more likely to hold for months.

**Phase B**
- The secondary test of the SC area should come on average volume and average range.
- Volume and range shrink through the phase.

**Phase C**
- Spring: a brief undercut of the SC low that reverses quickly, on low volume.
- An undercut on expanding volume and wide candles means further decline.
- The spring is optional.

**Phase D**
- Jump above range resistance, ideally on wider spread and higher volume. He accepts that volume sometimes does not expand.
- Then sign-of-strength (SOS) candles.

**Phase E**
- Check candle range, closes near the high, and tail length.
- Repeated SOS candles plus last-point-of-support (LPS) pullbacks mean the move is under way.

**Entry**
- Buy the first LPS.
- If price goes vertical with no LPS: 50% on the breakout with expanding volume, 50% at the LPS test.

**Case: Bank Nifty, Sept 2018–2019**
- Range about 24,200–25,500.
- The breakout had no volume expansion, so he advised waiting for the LPS.
- Price reached about 27,000, roughly 1:1, which he attributes to the missing volume.

**Post-breakout trend strength [tTdy5srpW9Q]**
- Bullish candles dominate. His example: 31 strong bullish candles against 5 relevant bearish ones in the first 15% of the move.
- No strong bearish candles early.
- Volatility should fall and the first retracement should be negligible. Bharti Airtel failed these tests and retraced to the breakout point.
- Momentum is judged by the angle of the rise (above about 45–50°). That depends on chart scale and would need converting to a rate-of-change rule.

**Related preference:** long horizontal accumulation ranges, with examples of about 4–6 years, are preferred over V-shaped recoveries from markdown [Bqp9jKQCs30].

### 2.6 Master/mother candle consolidation (monthly and weekly)

- **Monthly setup [mRAndF9wyaA]:** a large master candle, then a few monthly candles inside its range, then a breakout above it. The target window is a 2–4 month trend.
- **Breakout candle requirements [mRAndF9wyaA]:** long range, close near the high, little upper wick, substantial volume.
  - He rejected Adani Ports 2016–17 because its breakout candle was weak.
- **Weekly example [CXJZMbE_d5o]:** Amara Raja, valid on a weekly close above about 850. Long-tailed candles inside the range were read as buying.
- **Monthly variant on Nifty Bank [Y4TewQHVhvc]:** sustain above the high of an indecision candle (about 47,255–47,374) inside the mother range.
- **Related monthly read [Tkx2xIboYsw]:** narrow consolidation near the close of a prior long-wick wide-range candle (TCS about 2,480–2,900). The wick high is treated as resistance.

### 2.7 Coil spring (older, one video, high clarity) [VqoLpEvre04]

**Structure**
- Three down legs, A–B, C–D and E–F, each smaller than the last.
- Lows B, D and F roughly equal.
- A resistance line through highs A, C and E.

**Duration:** at least 1 month, ideally 1–3 months. He says that range gave him the best risk/reward.

**Preferred variant**
- F above B and D (a rising triangle), which he calls the highest-odds version.
- If A is much higher than C and E, draw the trigger line through C and E only.

**Entry:** at the break of the A–C–E line, or after the wide-range Heikin Ashi candles that follow the break.

### 2.8 Other range-breakout frameworks (mostly older)

These are summarised because they define "range" and "breakout" differently.

**Renko ranges [Mf7ZD9MY7vI]**
- A consolidation of at least 1 month, ideally 1.5–2.5 months, on bricks of 0.5–1% of price, is "strong" support or resistance. A breakout either way is the signal.
- About 20 days is "weak".
- Bricks of 0.3% or 5–15-minute charts produce many false double and triple tops.
- He claims this beats three-touch trendlines.
- Examples: Bank Nifty, a 60-minute S&P 500 chart, Tata Steel. All were chosen after the fact.

**DeMark (TD) lines [1FA93wpDNq4, Vz5lqc_RO7M]**
- A TD point's level is the minimum of the consecutive lower-high counts on each side (higher lows for demand points).
- Join only two points of the **same** level.
- Construct right to left, starting from the most recent point.
- Entry is a decisive close above a falling level-3 supply line. "Decisive" is not defined.
- He claims fewer whipsaws than discretionary lines.
- The supply line is the short-side mirror.

**Ichimoku Kumo breakout**
- *Positional* [2crnCCz4Y3s]:
  - Price clearly above the cloud, with Senkou A and B both rising.
  - Chikou above price; Tenkan and Kijun rising.
  - Prefer breakouts out of a consolidation. Reduce size on V-shapes.
- *Short-term* [xzR6bwNgb18]:
  - All four conditions are required: volume, RS, Tenkan/Kijun rising, Chikou clear of price.
  - Flat or falling Senkou lines are acceptable. This **contradicts** the positional rule, apparently by timeframe.
- *15-minute* [59vJPo6oiNA]:
  - Stop at Senkou B. Filter by the higher-timeframe trend because whipsaws are frequent.
  - Skip if the cloud is still falling, volume fades, the candle closes below its open, or resistance is just overhead.
- *Skip conditions* [153pJZk-ixo]: flat Kijun or flat Chikou.

**Market Profile (13–8 years ago)**
- Breakout buy signals are more trustworthy in four cases:
  - The open is outside the prior value area and range [dUeQOEU6FlQ].
  - The initial balance is narrow [z-ae7tLVdoo, 4kP0_EuS_EY].
  - The prior range showed net upside extensions, buying tails and rising value [cDcvc9esnwo].
  - Price closes beyond the combined value area rather than the swing high [uFFAAZNSozg].
- For high-volume-node (HVN) breakouts [p03ho_Rhc_E]:
  - Require a 30-day or longer profile with a prominent HVN and wide bullish breakout candles.
  - Skip if another HVN sits 2–3% above.
- A pullback to the prior high-volume node is an entry [su1cu2Y3AXA].
- In moderate volatility, watch the first two to three 30-minute periods. If price leaves the prior range, be aggressive; if not, reduce size or write options [dUeQOEU6FlQ].

**Opening-range breakout [hk4d8AxbQ3A]**
- Use the first 15- or 30-minute candle's high and low.
- He claims the common feature of his failed ORBs was low breakout-bar volume. He wants a wide, strong bar.

**Gaps [HHkcCy5LrCM]**
- Trade only runaway (breakout) and continuation gaps that exit a defined range, on 30- or 60-minute charts.
- The gap candle's range should exceed about 0.8–1% and close near its extreme.
- The gap window should be up to about 1–1.5%; wider gaps fill more often.
- Stop looking for gaps after a run of about 10–15% (index) or 20–25% (stock).
- With weak gap candles, take only starter size.

**Heikin Ashi context**
- Before a long-range breakout, count wide-range initiation candles up versus down inside the range. In gold's roughly 6-month triangle in 2019, bullish ones dominated [FtxgOtPYqVA].
- Combine Heikin Ashi with prior breakout levels [e34G88-BKI4].
- A rise with no initiation candles is suspect [L99LgSdCDD8].
- Double-bottom momentum pattern: the second low forms on contracting range [IHqkSPVCxko].

**Outside bar and kicker [_AiSzhs67J4, QWcb7yxYAxk]:** reversal patterns, not base breakouts. They share his candle-strength criteria (wide range, close at the extreme).

**Elliott Wave [JMZFlzfNOCQ, KvhIh2rU0R0]:** the notes contain wave rules and correction shapes (flats, triangles) but no breakout trigger. They are outside this chapter's practical scope.

### 2.9 What makes a base worth buying, and what to avoid

**Prefer stocks near highs**
- Stay near 52-week or all-time highs, where there is no overhead supply [Jpu536_AmGc, KRoie4cj5TQ].
- Avoid stocks down 40–60%, which meet trapped sellers at every level [Jpu536_AmGc, V41BUpPK3E4].

**Minimum base length:** a sound base of at least **7–8 weeks** into new highs [KRoie4cj5TQ].

**Other base lengths elsewhere conflict with this**
- Flags of about 12–13 days [1jsLESmVLVU].
- Ranges "as short as about 10 days" [p6pwKWQgxUw].
- Renko ranges of at least 1 month [Mf7ZD9MY7vI].
- High tight flags of 3–5 weeks [dERi8MUU5es].

**Avoid V-shapes:** they "usually" do not give good forward returns. Require a VCP, cup and handle or flag [20BxPDdAZAE, Bqp9jKQCs30]. In the Ichimoku framework, reduce size on V-shapes [2crnCCz4Y3s].

**Healthy consolidation [jBYC0t30fz8]**
- Mostly indecision candles and few bearish ones.
- Volume drying up on the drift lower, meaning strong holders have not exited.
- Also: bearish candles in the pause produce no downside follow-through [4CzLWJxP4UM].

**Laggard breakouts:** stocks that did little earlier and now attempt a breakout, bought gradually because rallies were being sold [iWa7MbGawvU].

**After a crash:** wait for a new base and settled volatility. Silver was down 40–45% with 7–8% average daily volatility [6qExRlD11sE].

**Scarcity:** in one weak week only about 67 stocks qualified as contractions [V41BUpPK3E4]. In another period about 200 of the Nifty 500 had a clean structure [VCNsjVuGoWQ].

### 2.10 The breakout trigger and candle quality (long)

**Trigger:** price moves above the contraction or base high, or the pivot [Oq4FjWEDbd0]. Clearing a whole zone rather than a single line protects against false breakouts [d7cwBnW78XI]. Resistance can be a zone 5–7% wide where price spent the most time [d7cwBnW78XI].

**Candle checklist [p6pwKWQgxUw]**
1. A wide range that closes near the high, with little upper wick. A small wick is acceptable on a very wide candle.
2. Volume expanded versus recent bars.
3. RS versus the benchmark rising.

He applied this on daily and 60-minute charts.

**Specific thresholds he gives**
- The breakout candle has the widest range of the last **20 sessions**, plus clear volume expansion [jBYC0t30fz8].
- A wide-range candle with volume above the last **20–25 sessions** [4CzLWJxP4UM].

**Warning sign:** a long upper wick on huge volume, closing well below the high. He doubted it at the time and the stock failed [mR0HMssJek8].

**Long-term resistance:** a breakout above multi-year resistance on wide weekly candles is "underrated" and especially positive [Ux_uuOj-gKs].

**Cheat pivot (Minervini):** enter before the formal level clears. This is the aggressive entry, with better odds in strong markets [1jsLESmVLVU]. The caption numbers (about 44,090 versus 43,989) are unclear.

**Short side**
- Mirror rule: avoid shorting a breakdown candle with a long lower tail [p6pwKWQgxUw].
- A Stage 3 top as a range or triangle, shorted on a break below it together with the 150-day average [G2gjQUJRfJM].
- The short side is thin otherwise.

### 2.11 Volume confirmation

**His strongest statement [1jsLESmVLVU]**
- The breakout candle must carry substantially higher volume than normal, whether the market is mature or tired.
- Example measure: above the prior 5 sessions.
- A spike on both the pre-breakout move and the breakout reads as institutional buying.

**Other measures**
- A spike clearly above the **50-day average volume** [N5bb0Cu8RAs].
- Above the 20–25 session average [4CzLWJxP4UM].
- "Strong volume" is the key confirmation; without it, price may fall back [Tkx2xIboYsw].
- Low breakout volume is his marker of failed ORBs [hk4d8AxbQ3A].

**Exception:** in Wyckoff he accepts breakouts without volume expansion but then waits for the LPS and expects a smaller move [K06GqbJwEv4]. This is a softer stance than 1jsLESmVLVU.

### 2.12 Relative strength and sector confirmation

- Price and the RS line breaking out together is "very reliable." Example: Berger Paints, price clearing a 16-month base and RS clearing a 24-month base [Ux_uuOj-gKs].
- RS rising is part of the three-filter checklist [p6pwKWQgxUw].
- RS strong over the prior 4–5 months [Oq4FjWEDbd0].
- Check whether sector peers are also moving [mim7ylyaN74, 4CzLWJxP4UM].
- Prefer VCPs in the pockets that are working [kwbfXjRtEkk].
- The stock-selection template: clean base, above the 50-DMA, rising RS, sector heading higher [sIo5XQqZ0b0].
- High-RS sectors are not bought blindly: wait for a cooling-off, then a fresh breakout [BxY2ofigO8I].
- For recently listed stocks: buy a base at the 50-DMA or an RS breakout versus Nifty 500 or Nifty 50 [SbtFCxbIB1w].

### 2.13 Follow-through

- Breakout quality depends on several consecutive up days after leaving the base; the more the better. Rising volume reads as institutional rather than retail buying [NUDjIohACWs, VET4JHrc2vU].
- O'Neil, as he quotes him: the best trades are profitable right away and rally for days on higher volume [VET4JHrc2vU].
- A follow-through day is loosely defined as a very wide candle, sometimes gapped, between narrow pauses [VET4JHrc2vU].
- No fixed day count is given.
- Constructive alternative: sideways consolidation right at the breakout level for 3–4 weeks (SBI) is read as supply absorption, not failure [BhQ_YnaRqa0].

### 2.14 False breakouts and market regime

**Definition:** price clears resistance, then fully reverses and traps buyers. He calls this the main reason traders abandon breakouts [p6pwKWQgxUw].

**How common:**
- He claims **over 65%** of breakouts initially fail. Breakout buyers get poor location and then exit on the pullback [-SC5vcQ1NuE]. This figure is unsourced.
- Failure odds are highest right at the breakout point [C8IZozIkPJY].

**Regime filter (recent and emphatic)**
- Breakouts fail in broken markets. Example: a clean contraction breakout in defence fully retraced [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- In a weak market even small index breakouts tend to fail (Nifty IT) [M4Z-Gi4gGag].
- In a non-momentum market, chasing breakouts does not work and holding good-structure stocks pays [CFJNeCPMQ1o].
- In the current market he names only two workable approaches: buy at support, or proper base breakouts [rhiXs5Kh_0g].
- The broad market can itself sit in a long contraction whose end is hard to anticipate, which is why breadth matters [6GqBBVt6fUs].

**Undercut principle (O'Neil):** a pivot can be briefly undercut and then rally, or exceeded and then reversed. A single break or reclaim is not conclusive [8QeR8FmYYg4].

**High-volatility caution:** a breakout can become an open-rejection reverse, especially in bear markets. He suggests options hedges [dUeQOEU6FlQ].

### 2.15 Entry timing: breakout versus retest, scaling, extension

**The tension**
- One video says he enters at the breakout, and that professionals buy near the breakout [1jsLESmVLVU].
- Another says buying breakouts "more often than not" gets you into trouble, while buying pullbacks rarely does if done right [mim7ylyaN74].
- A third says he does not chase. After a multi-month range breakout on a wide candle with volume, he waits for a pullback to the old resistance and buys only if strength reappears there [4CzLWJxP4UM].

**Retest logic**
- Broken resistance becomes support [jBYC0t30fz8, -SC5vcQ1NuE, Tkx2xIboYsw, dV4MiNaAenI].
- Two or more long lower-wick candles holding just above broken resistance are a positive sign [4CzLWJxP4UM].
- He accepts three states [V41BUpPK3E4]:
  - already broken out;
  - threatening to break out;
  - broken out and retesting.
- Either pullback or breakout entry is acceptable depending on momentum [mRAndF9wyaA].

**Scaling**
- Starter position at the breakout, add on a pullback in the established trend [sbHzNd2iA8k, C8IZozIkPJY].
- Wyckoff version: 50/50 [K06GqbJwEv4].
- Members' examples: a probe on 19 July, an add on 23 July [T4849hhdwTo].
- Slow accumulation in a market that sells rallies [iWa7MbGawvU].

**Extension**
- Prefer a fresh breakout about 7% up over one already about 30% up [6fowSBDs3eY].
- Within 3–5% of the 50-DMA, not 10% above [S--7ta4FmbY].

**Stops (thin)**
- Nearest pivot low; about 10% was acceptable for a positional trade (MCX) [T4849hhdwTo].
- Demand-zone low on a 30-minute entry, about a 50–60 rupee risk [1klATqC7oks].
- Senkou B for Ichimoku [59vJPo6oiNA].

## 3. How a program would test it

1. **Breakout-candle filter.**
   - *Event:* a daily close above the highest high of a base lasting at least 7 weeks.
   - *Compare:* events where (a) the bar range is the largest of the prior 20 bars, (b) the close is in the top 25% of the bar and the upper wick is under 25% of range, and (c) volume is at least 1.5× the 50-day average, against all events.
   - *Outcomes:* failure rate (close back below the pivot within 5 and 10 days) and 20/60-day forward return.
   - *Data:* daily OHLCV, split- and dividend-adjusted.
   - *Sources:* [p6pwKWQgxUw, jBYC0t30fz8, N5bb0Cu8RAs, mR0HMssJek8, KRoie4cj5TQ]

2. **The 65% initial-failure claim and retest entry.**
   - *Measure:* the share of breakouts that trade back to the pivot within 10 sessions.
   - *Compare:* the expectancy (R-multiple, stop below the base low) of breakout-day entry versus entry on the first retest that holds within 2% of the pivot, versus 50/50 scaling.
   - *Data:* daily OHLCV.
   - *Sources:* [-SC5vcQ1NuE, C8IZozIkPJY, 4CzLWJxP4UM, sbHzNd2iA8k, K06GqbJwEv4]

3. **Base location and extension.**
   - *Compare:* breakouts where the close at entry is within 5% of the 50-DMA against those 10% or more above it, plus the same split relative to the 30-week MA.
   - *Outcomes:* hit rate and forward returns.
   - *Data:* daily OHLCV.
   - *Sources:* [S--7ta4FmbY, Oq4FjWEDbd0, NUDjIohACWs, 6fowSBDs3eY]

4. **Contraction quality.**
   - *Define:* at least 3 swing pullbacks inside the base, each shallower than the last, plus a 10-day average range at a 50-day low.
   - *Compare:* breakouts from such bases against bases without contraction.
   - *Also test:* whether range compression in uptrends resolves upward more than 50% of the time.
   - *Data:* daily OHLCV, with a zigzag swing algorithm whose threshold must be chosen and stress-tested.
   - *Sources:* [Y4TewQHVhvc, 1jsLESmVLVU, g3mcqf6rqoc]

5. **Regime dependence.**
   - *Split:* breakout failure rate and 20-day return by (a) benchmark above or below its 50- and 200-DMA, and (b) percent of index members above the 200-DMA (under 25% versus healthy).
   - *Data:* constituent histories (survivorship-free) and index levels.
   - *Sources:* [M4Z-Gi4gGag, Sb6KTt64YKE, CFJNeCPMQ1o]

6. **Proximity to highs.**
   - *Compare:* breakouts in stocks within 5% of their 52-week high against stocks 40% or more below it.
   - *Outcomes:* 1- to 6-month returns.
   - *Sources:* [Jpu536_AmGc, KRoie4cj5TQ, V41BUpPK3E4]

7. **Price and RS dual breakout.**
   - *Event:* the stock-to-benchmark ratio also makes a new N-week high on the price breakout day.
   - *Compare:* against price-only breakouts.
   - *Data:* stock and benchmark closes.
   - *Sources:* [Ux_uuOj-gKs, p6pwKWQgxUw]

8. **Follow-through.**
   - *Compare:* breakouts with above-average volume and a close above the pivot on at least 3 of the first 5 days, against the rest, conditioning only on information available at day 5.
   - *Outcomes:* 20- to 60-day returns.
   - *Sources:* [NUDjIohACWs, VET4JHrc2vU]

## 4. Transfer to TSX and US markets

**Applies as is**
- All of the price/volume definitions are pure OHLCV logic:
  - base length, contraction, breakout candle quality, volume versus the 50-day average;
  - 50-DMA, 30-week and 200-DMA anchors;
  - proximity to 52-week highs, follow-through, retests;
  - Wyckoff phases, coil spring, Renko percent-brick ranges, TD lines, Ichimoku.
- The O'Neil and Minervini sources are themselves US-based.

**Needs adapting**
- **Benchmarks for RS:** S&P/TSX Composite or S&P 500 / Nasdaq instead of Nifty 50 or Nifty 500.
- **Sector indices:** TSX sector indices or iShares sector ETFs, and US SPDR sector ETFs, instead of Nifty sectoral indices. The TSX is concentrated in financials, energy and materials, so a "pockets that work" filter will cluster.
- **Breadth thresholds:** the "under about 25%" level must be recomputed on the TSX's smaller universe.
- **Liquidity:** TSX small caps are thinner. Volume-spike thresholds and slippage need recalibration, and minimum dollar-volume filters are advisable.
- **Gap rules:** the 0.8–1% candle and 1–1.5% window thresholds are tuned to Indian hourly bars with overnight gaps; US extended hours change bar construction [HHkcCy5LrCM].
- **Market Profile and ORB timing** depend on session structure.
- **IPO-base ideas** depend on listing flow [r63sarc_qV8, T4849hhdwTo].
- **Costs:** Indian securities transaction tax differs from North American commissions and spreads.
- **Hedging:** his options-hedge suggestion reflects India's limited hedging instruments [dUeQOEU6FlQ]. North America has broader choices (index puts, inverse ETFs).

**India-only**
