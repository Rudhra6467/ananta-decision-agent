# Regime and breadth

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits

Raunak treats the market regime as the first filter, ahead of sector and stock selection. He works top-down: index, then sector, then stock [9dbnXEEHgsc, JJEB-UZgK4E]. He cites O'Neil's claim that about three of four stocks follow the general market down [hKnYYRpd63s].

His early videos (about 6–8 years ago) use simple trend filters on the benchmark: the index versus its 200-day average, Heikin-Ashi initiation candles, and Ichimoku alignment [488FDla8kuc, 2Yv0E57_bs4, e34G88-BKI4, 2crnCCz4Y3s].

From about 2–3 years ago the index 50 DMA becomes his working regime line [KRoie4cj5TQ, U7YrYjIS9B8, N5bb0Cu8RAs].

In the last roughly 18 months he adds a multi-gauge breadth dashboard, updated weekly. It covers:
- the percentage of stocks above the 50, 100 and 200 DMA;
- the share of those that are extended;
- the share in a higher-high/higher-low (HH/HL) structure or in Stage 2;
- counts of sectors that are leading or lagging;
- size-segment relative strength.

He uses these readings for three jobs:
- **Exposure and position size:** aggressive, cautious, or sidelines.
- **Opportunity count:** how many buyable setups exist.
- **Bottoms versus bear-market rallies:** telling a durable low from a bounce, using follow-through days, the moving-average progression and the bottoming sequence.

A recurring theme is that the market is "divided" or "selective": headline indices and breadth often disagree. When they do, he says to trade only the strong pockets.

Everything below is his claim or method, to be tested.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Percent of stocks above the 50/200 DMA: the core breadth gauge

**What it is.** He computes the share of a universe trading above its 50-day and 200-day moving averages. He sometimes adds the 100-day average [SbtFCxbIB1w, d3-OMTGOGx8, N2dUTcb9CzQ] and the 30-week average [c-0bHPew1fg].

The universe varies across videos:
- Nifty 500 in most of them;
- Nifty Total Market, about 750 stocks [0nbQs92kYuY, S--7ta4FmbY];
- the whole NSE, about 2,580 stocks [c-0bHPew1fg];
- "liquid stocks" only, with circuit-hit and illiquid names excluded [V41BUpPK3E4, dERi8MUU5es].

He reviews breadth weekly, not daily [N5bb0Cu8RAs, hwOw2HaLUcQ, LWoEC9OjajE].

**His thresholds for % above the 200 DMA.** These differ by video.

|---|---|---|
| 70–80% | Strong, broad bull with many opportunities | [LWoEC9OjajE, Qu49zHwEGwo] |
| ~90% | Prior peaks, 2020–21 | [elpQ8wyLZLQ, V41BUpPK3E4] |
| ≥50% | Minimum "healthy"; he wants it held | [Qu49zHwEGwo, 6qExRlD11sE, fFUPLUEhzVo] |
| 50–60% | Needed before intermittent volatility fades | [Jpu536_AmGc] |
| <50% | Narrow market | [SbtFCxbIB1w] |
| ~51% | Already "weak, narrow" | [VCNsjVuGoWQ] |
| 35–38% | Danger line where declines accelerate | [fFUPLUEhzVo] |
| <40% | Heavy selling returns even in large caps; gains get given back | [Qu49zHwEGwo, N2dUTcb9CzQ] |
| ~30% | More serious | [Qu49zHwEGwo] |
| 33–38% | "Very poor"; not buying many stocks | [6GqBBVt6fUs, 6qExRlD11sE] |

One video says the 200 DMA reading "typically" swings between 39% and 61%, so 50% is mid-range [0nbQs92kYuY]. That conflicts with the 70–90% readings he cites elsewhere.

**His thresholds for % above the 50 DMA.**
- In the 2020–24 bull, the reading stayed around 58–60% even during corrections.
- Above roughly 50–60% means medium-term stability.
- Below 50% went with the March–April 2025 volatility [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- About 80–83% is good medium-term stability. Combined with only about 46–53% above the 200 DMA, the long-term trend is "not yet reliable" [Jpu536_AmGc, g3mcqf6rqoc, jkga2zdA9dY].
- A reading of 60% is "improved", but he wants it to hold above 60% and reach 75% [L-PGsMh2vHw].
- About 65% is healthy for a sideways market [S--7ta4FmbY].
- Prior peaks were about 95% [d3-OMTGOGx8].

**Short versus long breadth.** When 50 DMA breadth falls much faster than 200 DMA breadth (−21 points versus −10 points in 10 sessions), he calls the damage "fast but not deep" and says structure is still intact [0nbQs92kYuY].

One example: 50 DMA breadth around 40% while 200 DMA breadth is about 70%. He says this can be read either as a silent correction or as a healthy cool-off before a reversal [d3-OMTGOGx8].

**One-day breadth hits.** A drop of 10 points or more in a single day in % above the 50 DMA marks a "breadth hit". In his example (8 July), the reading went from 62% to about 50%, then recovered to 65% by Friday [S--7ta4FmbY].

**Slope breadth.** He argues the share of stocks with a *rising* 200 or 50 DMA matters more than the share above them, and credits O'Neil and Minervini. His readings were 30% in January and 16% on 1 June 2025 [WI0tdUjH_Vg]. This appears in only one video.

**Coverage.** This is his most-taught regime tool, appearing in more than 30 videos. Clarity is mostly high. The thresholds are not consistent from video to video.

### 2.2 HH/HL structure share, Stage 2 share, and structure ranks

**What it is.** The share of stocks whose daily chart shows higher highs and higher lows. It is his second main gauge and has become more prominent recently.

**Thresholds.**
- Healthy Indian bulls have usually been well above 60%.
- About 39–40% means poor conditions: trade with extra caution and cut quickly [rhiXs5Kh_0g].
- A bull market needs at least about 50% [20BxPDdAZAE, Jpu536_AmGc (50–55%)].
- 59–62% was "good"; 35% was "poor" [sIo5XQqZ0b0].
- 45–55% is an unhealthy zone he says he cannot "hang around" in [8QeR8FmYYg4].
- About 10% means limited odds of portfolio gains [Jpu536_AmGc].

**Historical readings he reports.**
- About 61% in the US versus about 20% in India, given as the reason India was more correction-prone [e34G88-BKI4, about 6 years ago].
- Low of about 16% in early April, peak about 56%, then about 52% [YFsvQXZZt1g].
- He measures the share across several universes: about 3,368 liquid stocks, about 750, the Nifty 500, and the Midcap/Smallcap 400 [YFsvQXZZt1g].

**Shakeout versus breakdown (his main use of speed).** A dip below the 50 DMA with breadth eroding slowly (64% drifting to 51%) is a shakeout. A price break while breadth falls sharply over 7–8 sessions to about 45% is a real breakdown [8QeR8FmYYg4].

Breadth holding near 52% through about 4 flat months shows "resilience". He adds that 52% could still fall to 26% and that stops would handle that case [YFsvQXZZt1g].

**Stage 2 counts.** For example, 680 of about 2,580 NSE stocks were in Stage 2, which he read as constructive but not euphoric [c-0bHPew1fg]. Stage 2 versus Stage 4 share is one of his four gauges [Jpu536_AmGc].

**A/B/C/D weekly ranks.** He sorts stocks into four classes using HH/HL versus LH/LL structure, position against the 50 DMA, and position against the "50 DMA pivot" (the nearest swing low near the 50 DMA) [N2dUTcb9CzQ]:
- **A:** HH/HL, above the 50 DMA and above the pivot.
- **B:** HH/HL, above the pivot but near the 50 DMA.
- **C:** LH/LL, below the 50 DMA but still above the pivot.
- **D:** LH/LL, below both.

From November 2025 to January 2026, A fell from 624 to 395 and D rose from 442 to 644. He read this as narrowing. The counts exceed 500, so the universe is unclear.

**Visual screen.** He also classifies stocks by price structure plus the slope of their relative-strength line: about 25% leaders, about 15% flat, and about 45–60% declining [x2RbjJp1eCI].

**Coverage.** About 12 videos, concentrated in the last year. No swing-definition rule is ever given.

### 2.3 Extension and the opportunity count (narrow bull)

**What it is.** He counts the stocks above their averages and then the share of those that are "extended": about 8–15% above the 50 DMA means poor risk-reward [LWoEC9OjajE].

**How he reads it.**
- Narrow breadth plus many extended names means few buyable setups. The stocks near their averages tend to be weak in relative strength or in weak sectors [LWoEC9OjajE].
- Time and price corrections pull extended names back toward their averages, which raises the opportunity count [SbtFCxbIB1w].
- Narrow or deteriorating breadth means smaller positions [Oq4FjWEDbd0, SbtFCxbIB1w].

**Example readings.**
- 41% above the 50 DMA, with about half of those extended, moving to 48% with 21% extended [SbtFCxbIB1w].
- 38% of the Nifty 500 above the 200 EMA, with 11% overextended [Oq4FjWEDbd0]. The captions are ambiguous here.

**Narrow-bull behaviour he claims.** Trends continue, but with sharp, frequent reversals, including after clean breakouts, because weak sectors don't receive money when leaders pull back [LWoEC9OjajE].

**Coverage.** 3 videos, all about 8–10 months ago. The definition of "extended" is loose.

### 2.4 Index trend filters: 200 DMA, 50 DMA, 50 DMA pivot

**Early version, about 8 years ago.**
- Index above the simple 200 DMA means look only for longs; below means look only for shorts. Keep the rule simple enough to explain to a child [2Yv0E57_bs4].
- Don't use relative-strength stock picking for longs when the index is decisively below its 200 DMA. High-RS stocks will simply fall less [488FDla8kuc].
- The length is arbitrary: anything from 180 to 220 works, and other methods can substitute [488FDla8kuc, jlpd-YpqwiQ].

**50 DMA as the working line, about 2–3 years ago onward.**
- Index below the 50 DMA: no big bets, small systematic positions only.
- Once the index stabilises, clears its pivot and holds above the 50 DMA: build aggressively.
- A break of the 50 DMA does not mean exit everything or go short [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- Be long while the index is above a *rising* 50 DMA. Exit if breadth doesn't improve and the index breaks the 50 DMA decisively [N5bb0Cu8RAs].
- Two consecutive sessions below the 50 DMA is a concern; bad things happen there in the short term [U7YrYjIS9B8].
- His summary: good things happen above the 50 DMA, bad things below the index's 50 DMA pivot [8QeR8FmYYg4].

**Trend violation needs two conditions.** A close below the 50 DMA alone is not enough. He wants a close below both the 50 DMA and the nearest swing-low pivot [N2dUTcb9CzQ].

**200 DMA as the backstop.** While price is above the 200 DMA, a dip to the 50 DMA is a pullback, not a trend change [SuYHqw5zA8U].

**Below both averages.**
- Index decisively below both the 50 and 200 DMA: be defensive and cut breakout size, in his example from 100 shares to 10 [M4Z-Gi4gGag].
- No active trading or investing; only slow ETF accumulation [yYwiSDVwgyA].
- Positional traders with a 6–18 month horizon should "do nothing" below both and wait until both are retaken and stable [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- When the market breaches the 50 and 200 DMA, review loss-making or pivot-violating positions and cut them. He rejects the idea that being below the 200 DMA doesn't matter [Y4TewQHVhvc].

**Weak index but still buying.** In one video he still buys strong breakouts while the index makes lower highs and lower lows, at reduced size [uw5QQOenJnQ]. Trading against the broad trend is acceptable only with smaller size [G2gjQUJRfJM].

**Coverage.** More than 15 videos. Clarity is high. The shift from the 200 DMA to the 50 DMA, and then to "50 DMA plus pivot plus breadth", is a clear change over time.

### 2.5 Confirming a bottom versus a bear-market rally

**Bottoming sequence (from O'Neil).** He describes four stages [V41BUpPK3E4, iWa7MbGawvU]:
1. A long fall, after which people feel it can't fall further.
2. Breadth damage: many stocks at new lows, leaders falling, closes near the day's lows.
3. Panic selling, possibly after a sharp 7–8% relief rally.
4. A trigger.

In both videos he placed India in stage 2, with panic still ahead.

He says a durable bottom needs a trigger. His examples: the 2016 US election, the 2019 corporate tax cut, 2020 liquidity, and Fed action in 2021–22 [V41BUpPK3E4].

**Follow-through rules (O'Neil, "slightly tweaked" by him) [B1ynZNvqesE].**
- Mark the low day as day 0.
- **Days 0 to about 4/5:** a strong up candle on volume higher than the prior session is the first follow-through.
- **Days 4–10:** price must not undercut the first rally day's low. More wide-range up days on rising volume should follow.
- **Deadline:** the wide-range, strong-volume rally should come by day 15. If it first appears on day 16 or later, the rally is weak.
- These rules anticipate a bottom; they don't confirm it. Price can still make a new low after a follow-through day.

**Moving-average progression [20BxPDdAZAE].**
1. Price holds above the 50 DMA.
2. Price clears the 200 DMA.
3. The 50 DMA crosses above the 200 DMA (golden cross).

Bear rallies often stall near the 200 DMA. He also requires the index to clear its prior major pivot high and hold above it.

Elsewhere he says he is "not a big fan" of golden and death crosses and that they would need backtesting [M4Z-Gi4gGag]. This partly contradicts the progression above.

**Bottom confirmation (alternative version).** Price back above the 50 and 200 DMA, sustained, with a range formed [M4Z-Gi4gGag]. By then the index is usually 10–15% off the low, and the market gives plenty of time to build positions [hpbDmEK-FvM].

**75-minute chart.** An HH/HL structure on the Nifty 75-minute chart, with price above recent swing pivots, is the "bare minimum" sign of an intermediate bottom. It is not sufficient on its own [-qQntPmfe-s].

**W-bottom.** Wait for the index to close above the middle peak of the W (about 25,300–25,350 in his example), then scale in quickly. Stay conservative until then [d3-OMTGOGx8].

**50 DMA as bear-market resistance.**
- In downtrends the index habitually bounces to test the 50 DMA [-qQntPmfe-s].
- Rallies that get 1–4% above it often reverse; he cites 2008 and 2024–25 [KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- Judge intermediate versus final bottoms by behaviour at the 50 DMA and about 2–3% above it [KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- A bounce toward the 50 DMA is not by itself proof of a bottom [V41BUpPK3E4].

**Size of bear-market rallies.**
- The Nasdaq 100 in 2000–02 had rallies of about 30–50% (41%, 43.8%, about 50%).
- 2008 had retracements of about 15–30% before the final low [20BxPDdAZAE].
- Indian bear phases had rallies of about 7–27% [KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- In 2008 the Nifty kept stalling at the 50% retracement of the fall. In 2018 it held above 50% and 61.8%, which he read as strength [d7cwBnW78XI].

**Quality of the bounce.**
- Check which sectors lead the bounce.
- A bounce not led by the prior winners, followed by a sharper drop, signals distribution [fFUPLUEhzVo, dERi8MUU5es].
- New leaders come from the sectors that fell least or lagged in the prior cycle [iWa7MbGawvU, B1ynZNvqesE].
- Breadth improvement should come along with the price structure [-qQntPmfe-s, ywccnKeWXNo].

**Liquidity lag.** The market typically bottoms about 4–8 months after the policy easing pivot, and sentiment can stay weak longer. He cites 2001–02, 2008 and 2020 [ywccnKeWXNo].

**Coverage.** About 15 videos, mostly from the last 1–1.5 years, in the 2024–25 correction context.

### 2.6 Depth gauges and "how far is too far"

**Index distance below the 200 DMA.** He gives several inconsistent figures, all attributed to O'Neil:
- Intermediate bottoms tend to form 8–14% below the 200 DMA. The Nifty was 9.3% below at the time, so a further 3–5% decline was plausible [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- The prior dip of about 9.7% below ended in a V-shaped recovery. He treats this zone as an opportunity for investors, not traders [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- Sell-offs usually stop about 7–8% below the 200 DMA. The same video also mentions 4–7%. A global disturbance can take it further [yYwiSDVwgyA].

**Intermediate decline sizes.**
- Normal bull-market declines are 8–12%, occasionally 12–15% (O'Neil) [hKnYYRpd63s].
- A bear market is a fall of 20% or more on an index [M4Z-Gi4gGag, hpbDmEK-FvM].
- Two types of bear market: global meltdowns about every 10–15 years, and roughly 20% segment falls about every 2–4 years [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- Average bear duration is 9–18 months [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- He also describes deep crashes about every 5–6 years [sbHzNd2iA8k], and a decadal cycle of 8–10 years [dERi8MUU5es]. These cycle claims vary.

**Drawdown buckets.** He counts liquid stocks by how far they sit below their highs, in 5-point buckets (−20%, −25% and so on down to −75%), and compares the distribution across the 2016, 2018, 2020, 2022 and 2024–25 cycles [V41BUpPK3E4].

In 2024–25, about 35–40% of liquid stocks were down 20–45%. He called this deeper than 2022 and broader than 2018.

Related readings:
- Median stock correction of about 32–35% [KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- Stocks down 25–30% while the index was down about 13–14% [M4Z-Gi4gGag].
- Small and mid caps down 35–40% from September–October 2024 [iWa7MbGawvU].

**ATR-based "time-and-price volatility" indicator.** It tracks the share of Nifty 500 stocks making sharp corrective moves. Spikes in 2013, 2016, 2018–19 and 2020 marked hard phases; long calm periods were when money was made. The formula is deliberately not disclosed [elpQ8wyLZLQ].

### 2.7 Extreme breadth as contrarian, investor-side signal (and its tension with 2.1)

At extreme lows he switches from "stay out" (trader) to "accumulate" (investor):

- A proprietary sentiment indicator bottomed in 2002–04, 2012, 2016, 2020, 2022 and 2025. Near those levels, a rally can come within 1–2 months [6qExRlD11sE].
- Multi-year bottoms formed when the share "below" the 200 DMA reached about 8–12% (possibly 12–14%) [6GqBBVt6fUs]. The wording appears inverted; he likely means 8–12% *above*.
- About 94% of stocks below the 200 DMA was extreme damage [V41BUpPK3E4].
- About 10–11% above the 50 DMA was a 15-month low [dERi8MUU5es].
- He shows a breadth chart whose extreme lows (2017, 2022–23) preceded the next swing, and says he accumulates at those points [VCNsjVuGoWQ].
- After a trough of about 10% in March 2025, a reading of 48% in June meant the trend was "not mature", with room to run [WI0tdUjH_Vg].

At the opposite extreme:
- Only about 5–8% of stocks below their averages (July 2024) means most people are already in. The time to deploy a large corpus has passed. He calls this a mood gauge, not a predictor [r11n7ztY2YY].
- When the market feels too easy, tighten stops and avoid leverage [r11n7ztY2YY].
- Don't commit heavily when sentiment is euphoric (O'Neil) [g3mcqf6rqoc].

**Divergence at highs.** Each new index high came with a lower participation peak: Nifty 500 about 90% in 2020–21, then about 80%, then about 60% in the pullback. He says this can't predict a crash [elpQ8wyLZLQ]. Breadth weakens first at tops and improves first at bottoms. Sectors whose internal breadth improved while the index fell in January–April 2025 became the later leaders [WI0tdUjH_Vg].

**Reconciling 2.1 and 2.7.** He doesn't state a rule for when "poor breadth, stay out" turns into "extreme breadth, accumulate". His implicit split is:
- traders sit out until breadth and the index structure recover [Sb6KTt64YKE, 6GqBBVt6fUs];
- investors stagger into ETFs and quality names [yYwiSDVwgyA, rxqOUxO_vP4, sbHzNd2iA8k].

### 2.8 Narrow versus broad: segments, weights, sectors

**Small/mid versus large.**
- A rising small/mid/micro-to-large ratio while large caps range means the market is broadening. Retail money sits in that space [Qu49zHwEGwo].
- He wants midcap and smallcap strength before calling a recovery [e34G88-BKI4, jkga2zdA9dY].
- A Nifty 100 holding strong relative strength versus the Nifty 500 means the rally is narrow [Yxo-rYOCuAo].
- Cap-segment 200 DMA breadth in June 2025: micro 36–37%, small 43%, mid about 60%, large about 70% [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- Deterioration spread inward: micro/SME from mid-November, the total market in December, the Nifty 500 in January, with the Nifty 50 still holding [rxqOUxO_vP4].
- Pivot-low breaks in large caps first and then in small and micro caps counted as a valid breakdown [sIo5XQqZ0b0].
- In the opposite direction: SMID indices reclaiming the 50 DMA before the Nifty is an improvement [KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- Small caps led 22% versus the Nifty 50's 7% since 1 April [kwbfXjRtEkk].
- A micro-cap index at new lows with all high-beta segments weak means slow repair [JcxWhCO0hqI].
- Small caps run first after recessions (2003, 2009, 2020) [JcxWhCO0hqI].

**Cap-weighted versus equal-weighted.**
- The equal-weight index hit an all-time high while about 10 heavyweights sat 25–30% below their highs [r63sarc_qV8].
- Heavyweights not trending caps the Nifty [zHzq3uAFJFs, kwbfXjRtEkk].
- Cap-weighted indices can hide weak internals [WI0tdUjH_Vg].

**US check.**
- Each week, check whether the Russell 2000 and 3000 move with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq. Divergence means a narrow rally [LWoEC9OjajE].
- In July 2024 only 2 of 11 US sectors carried the index; he notes a similar August 2023 pattern was followed by weakness [r11n7ztY2YY].

**Sector breadth.**
- A sector is healthy with at least 60% of constituents above both the 50 and 200 DMA [Jpu536_AmGc].
- A sector breakout led by only 2 of 10–15 names is unreliable [6GqBBVt6fUs, NUDjIohACWs].
- He tracks 56 sectors by quadrant (leading, weakening, improving, lagging). 36 leading and stable, with lagging rising from 3 to 7, was positive. More leading sectors over about 40 days with a flat index is a "silent bull" [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- A rising count of strong sectors means broadening [c-0bHPew1fg, lRUDp336l7Q].
- A "fractured" market is roughly half the sectors up and half down, with the index going nowhere [rocXNq3_1Zw, butE8gDPXxw].

**Risk appetite.**
- Gains in metals, capital goods, autos, defence and cement, with losses in healthcare, staples, tech and durables, mean risk-on [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- An undisclosed "momentum impulse" score weights high-beta sectors [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- The Nifty High Beta 50 index is a read on risk appetite [lRUDp336l7Q].

**Dispersion counts.** Since 30 March 2026, 37 stocks rose more than 100% and 262 rose 50–100%, while the indices lagged [SNrzU-z3RkE]. A scan for stocks up more than 50% in a year shows hidden strength [butE8gDPXxw].

**Practical conclusion.** In a narrow market, stay only in the strong pockets and be nimble [SbtFCxbIB1w, S--7ta4FmbY]. He frequently calls India a Stage 3 index with Stage 2 pockets [r63sarc_qV8, kwbfXjRtEkk, rbSj4pOg3_I].

### 2.9 Time versus price corrections, ranges, holding patterns

**Definitions.**
- A time correction: net change near zero over weeks, with gaps. Example: Nifty about 21,800 to 21,850 over 45 days.
- A price correction: a measurable fall. Example: Bank Nifty −8.7% [Pm_1kb8Fku0].
- A price correction can later turn into a time correction [V41BUpPK3E4].

**Durations and frequencies.**
- After each leg up, roughly 3–4 months of sideways action [jkga2zdA9dY].
- 12–18 months of consolidation after a big run [s1ZCPQI5894, elpQ8wyLZLQ (15–20% range)].
- 4–5 weeks during earnings season [lRUDp336l7Q].
- Markets trend 30–35% of the time [2crnCCz4Y3s]; elsewhere, up only 20–30% of the time [6qExRlD11sE].

**How to treat a holding pattern.**
- A holding pattern is normal, not a trend change [jkga2zdA9dY].
- In an uptrend, a range is a time to add to winners [g3mcqf6rqoc].
- Mark a fixed index range as a no-trade zone, for example Nifty 19,300–19,800 [U7YrYjIS9B8].
- A "season of gaps" with failed breakouts means sit out or build slowly [U7YrYjIS9B8].
- Stocks that fall usually just fall. They don't base near highs first, so a tight range near highs is not bearish [bhTEw6wD_CU].

**Stock selection by phase.** In index ranges, defensive stocks give the setups; in expansions, high-beta stocks do [CXJZMbE_d5o]. Weak-breadth ranges defeat fast trades, so shift to 12–18 month accumulation [6GqBBVt6fUs].

**Historical analogies.** He keeps comparing the current phase to 2011–13, 2015 and 2018–20 [0nbQs92kYuY, -qQntPmfe-s, Oq4FjWEDbd0, sIo5XQqZ0b0]. His difficulty ranking of these periods differs between videos: in one, 2011–13 is the toughest; in another, 2018–19 is.

### 2.10 Sentiment, structure versus momentum

- He separates structure (HH/HL plus breadth) from momentum and sentiment. A market can be "slow, boring" but not weak [CFJNeCPMQ1o, SuYHqw5zA8U].
- Markets "climb a wall of worry" [hwOw2HaLUcQ, p5O_uspLu9k, bhTEw6wD_CU, L-PGsMh2vHw (US VIX about 56–57 as a short-term bottom)].
- One-sided bearish consensus tends to produce V-shaped rallies [Sb6KTt64YKE, KUlNy1_Rk9M, dpsU7JvHiMA].
- Long lower tails on bad-news days mean the market refuses to fall [q26J58h5Qt8].
- Eight consecutive up weeks means extended; expect consolidation [q26J58h5Qt8].
- Trailing 12-month gains of 20% or more across many countries mean poor short-term risk-reward [r11n7ztY2YY].
- Frequent hits on logical stops are themselves a regime signal [Y4TewQHVhvc].
- Watch which stocks hold near the 50 DMA while the index falls; these are future leaders [T4849hhdwTo, mR0HMssJek8].
- Progressive exposure applies only at the start of a new upleg [YLAIkbRSnsA].

### 2.11 Older regime reads (about 5–13 years ago)

**Heikin-Ashi initiation candles.**
- No downside initiation candle on the weekly or daily chart means buy dips [e34G88-BKI4].

**Market Profile regimes [dUeQOEU6FlQ, Nkp_wwGZFNQ, su1cu2Y3AXA].**
- Opening types by volatility:
  - open drive: high volatility;
  - test drive: low-volatility bull;
  - rejection reverse: high-volatility bear;
  - open auction: low-volatility consolidation or distribution.
- Wide profiles mean balance; thin profiles mean trend.
- Rising initial balance and more range extensions in one direction indicate trend direction.
- Rotation factor of +5 to +6 sustained means buyers are in control.

**VIX above 20** made the opening-range breakout strategy work for him [hk4d8AxbQ3A].

**Intraday advance/decline.** About 3:1 advance/decline supported a large-cap breakout [hk4d8AxbQ3A]. This is the only advance/decline ratio use in the notes.

### 2.12 Short side

- Shorts work better when the broad trend is down; he says he backtested this and found merit. Counter-trend shorts only at small size [G2gjQUJRfJM].
- Don't short a short-term downswing inside a long-term uptrend. Wait until short- and long-term trends align [JJEB-UZgK4E].
- Below the 200 DMA, look only for shorts [2Yv0E57_bs4].
- In bear markets the 50 DMA is resistance, and rallies 1–4% above it fail [KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- Shorting near a base in a congested market is poor [U7YrYjIS9B8].
- Rejection-reverse openings occur in high-volatility bears [dUeQOEU6FlQ].
- He does not give a breadth threshold for turning short. His breadth work is used to cut long exposure, not to initiate shorts.

## 3. How a program would test it

**1. 200 DMA breadth regimes and long-entry quality.**
- Statement: when the daily % of Nifty 500 constituents above the 200 DMA is below 30%, or 30–40%, breakout entries and the index show lower forward 1–3 month returns, larger drawdowns and a lower breakout follow-through rate than when the reading is 50–70% or above 70%.
- Test the claimed danger line at 35–38% specifically.
- Data: point-in-time Nifty 500 constituents (to avoid survivorship), daily OHLCV, and a fixed breakout definition.
- Sources: [Qu49zHwEGwo, fFUPLUEhzVo, 6qExRlD11sE, yYwiSDVwgyA, N2dUTcb9CzQ].

**2. Index below both the 50 and 200 DMA as a size/stand-aside filter.**
- Statement: long breakouts taken when the benchmark closes below both averages have lower expectancy per unit of risk than those taken when it is above both.
- Compare against a version that adds the "50 DMA pivot" condition: a close below the 50 DMA and the nearest swing low.
- Data: index daily data, a stock breakout signal set, and a swing-low rule.
- Sources: [M4Z-Gi4gGag, Sb6KTt64YKE, KRoie4cj5TQ, N2dUTcb9CzQ, 8QeR8FmYYg4].

**3. Speed of HH/HL breadth decline separates shakeout from breakdown.**
- Statement: when an index closes below its 50 DMA and the % of Nifty 500 stocks in HH/HL structure has fallen more than 10 points in 8 sessions, 20–60 day forward returns are worse than when it fell less than 5 points.
- Data: a fixed swing algorithm (for example, N-bar pivots) applied to all constituents.
- Sources: [8QeR8FmYYg4, rhiXs5Kh_0g, YFsvQXZZt1g].

**4. Follow-through day timing.**
- Statement: after a marked low in an index that fell at least 10%, rallies whose first wide-range, higher-volume up day comes by day 15, and that don't undercut day 1's low through day 10, have better 3–6 month returns and fewer new lows than late or failed patterns.
- "Wide-range" must be defined, for example range at least 1.5 times ATR(20).
- Data: index OHLC plus aggregate volume (index or ETF volume).
- Source: [B1ynZNvqesE].

**5. 50 DMA as bear-market resistance.**
- Statement: with the index below a falling 200 DMA, a close above the 50 DMA reverses back below it within 10–20 sessions more often than not, typically within 1–4% above the line.
- Also test the claim that bounces from swing lows reach the 50 DMA within 15 sessions.
- Data: index daily, 2008 to date (Nifty, S&P 500, TSX).
- Sources: [KUlNy1_Rk9M, -qQntPmfe-s, 20BxPDdAZAE].

**6. Extreme-low breadth as investor entry.**
- Statement: when the % above the 200 DMA first drops below about 15% (also test below 10%) after a decline, forward 6–24 month index returns exceed unconditional returns. The reverse should hold when the % below the 200 DMA is at or under 5%.
- The episode count is small, so report per-episode results.
- Sources: [6GqBBVt6fUs, V41BUpPK3E4, r11n7ztY2YY, WI0tdUjH_Vg].

**7. Breadth divergence at highs.**
- Statement: an index new high with % above the 50 or 200 DMA lower than at the prior high is followed by lower 6–12 month returns or larger drawdowns than a high with confirming breadth.
- Sources: [elpQ8wyLZLQ, WI0tdUjH_Vg].

**8. Index distance below the 200 DMA.**
- Statement: index closes 8–14% below the 200 DMA have positive 1–3 month forward returns more often than closes within 5% of it. Also report the distribution of trough distances below the 200 DMA for non-crisis declines, to adjudicate 4–7%, 7–8% and 8–14%.
- Sources: [Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA].

**Secondary tests:**
- extension share versus breakout success [SbtFCxbIB1w];
- count of leading sectors during a flat index [6K1LOJqZYEg];
- small-to-large ratio trend [Qu49zHwEGwo];
- rate-cut-to-low lag of 4–8 months [ywccnKeWXNo].

## 5. Gaps and doubts

**Undefined core inputs:**
- No swing or pivot definition is given for "HH/HL structure", the "50 DMA pivot", or major versus minor pivots [JcxWhCO0hqI].
- "Extended" is only loosely 8–15% above the 50 DMA.
- "Wide-range candle" and "strong volume" in the follow-through rules are not quantified.

**Inconsistent thresholds and universes:**
- Healthy 200 DMA breadth is variously ≥50%, 50–60% or 70–80%, and 51% is called "weak".
- The typical range is stated as 39–61% in one video but readings of 70–90% appear in others.
- Danger is <40%, 35–38% or <30%.
- Universes switch between Nifty 500, about 750, about 2,580, about 3,368 and "liquid" stocks. Some captioned counts are ambiguous or exceed the stated universe [Oq4FjWEDbd0, N2dUTcb9CzQ, hwOw2HaLUcQ, WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- The 8–12% bottom-zone statement appears inverted [6GqBBVt6fUs].

**Contradictions:**
- Index distance below the 200 DMA at bottoms: 8–14% versus 7–8% versus 4–7%.
- Trending share of time: 30–35% versus 20–30%.
- Golden cross: used in the recovery progression versus "not a fan".
- Gap theory: dismissed as a trap [Lw6h6T30VvE] versus used as a downside target [Sb6KTt64YKE, NtpyZH5cfaE].
- Small caps "run first" after lows versus "stay weak a long time" [JcxWhCO0hqI, iWa7MbGawvU]. These may be reconcilable by cycle phase, but he doesn't say how.
- The switch from "poor breadth, stay out" to "extreme breadth, accumulate" has no stated rule.

**Undisclosed formulas:**
- the sentiment indicator [6qExRlD11sE];
- the ATR volatility gauge [elpQ8wyLZLQ];
- the composite breadth score [c-0bHPew1fg];
- the momentum impulse score [6K1LOJqZYEg];
- the breadth chart [VCNsjVuGoWQ].

**Barely or not covered:**
- Advance/decline lines: one intraday 3:1 mention [hk4d8AxbQ3A] and one weekly advancers-versus-decliners note [N5bb0Cu8RAs]. No cumulative advance/decline line.
- New highs versus new lows: only qualitatively, as "many stocks at new lows" in the breadth-damage stage [iWa7MbGawvU].
- Distribution-day counting: not taught as a count. There is only one Bank Nifty anecdote of heavy distribution candles [hKnYYRpd63s] and a 2-sessions-below-the-50-DMA concern.
- Explicit exposure percentages per regime: not given beyond "small", "conservative", "100 shares to 10" and "aggressive".
- A breadth-based short-entry rule.

**Evidence quality:**
- Many items are point-in-time market calls tied to his live narrative of 2024–26.
- His backtests are asserted (shorts with the trend [G2gjQUJRfJM]; a tweaked follow-through rule [B1ynZNvqesE]) but no results are shown.
- The historical episodes behind the extreme-breadth claims are few, perhaps 6–8 in India since 2002, so statistical power is low.
