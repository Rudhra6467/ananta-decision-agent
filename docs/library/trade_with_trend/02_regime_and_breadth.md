# Regime and breadth

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits his method

Raunak A treats the market regime as the first filter in a top-down process: index first, then sectors, then stocks [9dbnXEEHgsc, JJEB-UZgK4E, LahSe1vu1kY]. He cites O'Neil's claim that about three of four stocks follow the market's direction, so market direction comes before stock selection [hKnYYRpd63s].

He reads the regime from three sources:

- **Index structure.** Price versus the 50-day and 200-day averages, plus the higher-high/higher-low pattern.
- **Breadth.** The share of a broad universe above the 50-day and 200-day averages, in higher-high/higher-low structure, or in Stage 2.
- **Participation.** Which cap segments and sectors are carrying the move.

The output is not a buy or sell signal for the index. It is a dial for aggressiveness: how much to deploy, what position size to use, and whether to trade actively, accumulate slowly for 12–18 months, or sit out [Oq4FjWEDbd0, M4Z-Gi4gGag, yYwiSDVwgyA, rxqOUxO_vP4].

His teaching has changed over time:

- **Videos 5–8 years old.** A single 200-day trend filter, Heikin-Ashi "initiation candles" and Ichimoku alignment [2Yv0E57_bs4, 488FDla8kuc, e34G88-BKI4, 153pJZk-ixo].
- **13-year-old videos.** Market Profile regime ideas [dUeQOEU6FlQ, Nkp_wwGZFNQ, su1cu2Y3AXA].
- **Last 1–2 years.** A multi-gauge breadth dashboard, O'Neil/Weinstein framing, and weekly breadth updates.

Everything below is his claim or method, to be tested. None of it is established fact.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Index versus its moving averages: the core regime switch

**What it is.** The position of the benchmark index relative to its 50-day and 200-day averages sets how aggressive to be.

**Conditions and actions, from most to least favourable:**

| Index state | His stance | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Above a rising 50-day | Be long; take it one day at a time | [N5bb0Cu8RAs] |
| Above the 200-day, pulled back toward the 50-day | Short-term pullback, not a trend change | [SuYHqw5zA8U] |
| Below the 50-day | Caution line; no big bets, only small systematic positions | [U7YrYjIS9B8, KRoie4cj5TQ] |
| Below both averages, with lower highs and lower lows | Do nothing on the trading side; only slow ETF accumulation | [Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA] |

Further detail on each state:

- **The 50-day caution line.** He says bad things tend to happen below it. He dislikes even two consecutive Nifty closes below it [U7YrYjIS9B8]. A break of the 50-day does not mean exit everything or go short [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- **Building back up.** He builds aggressively once the index stabilises, clears its pivot and sustains above the 50-day [KRoie4cj5TQ].
- **Positional horizon below both averages.** For a 6–18 month holding period he says nothing good comes there [Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA].
- **Position size below both averages.** He cuts breakout size sharply; his example is 10 shares instead of 100 [M4Z-Gi4gGag].
- **Review on a breach.** When the market breaks the 50-day and 200-day, review losing positions and positions violating their pivots, and cut them. He rejects the idea that being below the 200-day "doesn't matter" [Y4TewQHVhvc].

**The "50 DMA pivot".** This is the nearest swing low around or just above the 50-day. A trend counts as violated only when price closes below both the average and this pivot [N2dUTcb9CzQ]. He sums it up as good things above the index's 50-day pivot and bad things below it [8QeR8FmYYg4].

**Earliest form of the rule (older videos).** Price above the simple 200-day means look only for longs; below means only shorts [2Yv0E57_bs4]. Do not buy high-RS stocks when the index is decisively below its 200-day, because they will merely fall less than the market [488FDla8kuc]. He says 180–220 days would work as well; consistency matters more than the exact length [488FDla8kuc].

**Shorts.** Shorts work better when the broad trend is down. He says he backtested this and found merit. Counter-trend shorts are acceptable only at smaller size [G2gjQUJRfJM]. Do not short a short-term dip inside a long-term uptrend [JJEB-UZgK4E].

**How often taught.** This is very frequent and taught with high clarity across years.

**Tension.** One video teaches a moving-average progression for recovery: hold the 50-day, then clear the 200-day, then a 50/200 golden cross [20BxPDdAZAE]. Another says he is not a fan of golden or death crosses and that they need backtesting [M4Z-Gi4gGag].

### 2.2 How deep corrections go and how often

He uses distance from the 200-day and drawdown size to judge how far a sell-off can run.

**Distance below the 200-day:**

- He cites O'Neil: an intermediate bottom tends to form 8–14% below the 200-day. Nifty was 9.3% below at the time, so a further 3–5% drop to fill open gaps was plausible [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- The last comparable dip, about 9.7% below, ended in a V-shaped recovery. He treats that distance as a zone for investors, not traders [Sb6KTt64YKE].
- Another video attributes to O'Neil that, absent a global disturbance, sell-offs usually stop 7–8% below the 200-day, with a 4–7% range also mentioned [yYwiSDVwgyA].
- **Inconsistency:** 8–14% and 7–8%/4–7% cannot all be the same O'Neil figure.

**Normal declines and when to call the bull over:**

- A normal intermediate decline is 8–12%, occasionally 12–15% (citing O'Neil). Nifty 500 was down about 10.5–10.8% at the time [hKnYYRpd63s].
- A bull market could be called over only on a break below a marked pivot low: the June 2024 election-candle low in Nifty 500 [hKnYYRpd63s].
- Bull and bear trends usually need 2–3 tricky swings before they end, so do not label a decline a bear market too early [hKnYYRpd63s].

**Bear-market definitions and frequencies:**

| Claim | Figure | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Bear market threshold, measured on an index | 20%+ fall from the recent high | [M4Z-Gi4gGag, hpbDmEK-FvM] |
| Typical bear market duration | 9–18 months to bottom | [hpbDmEK-FvM] |
| 20% index falls inside a larger bull | Every 2–4 years | [hpbDmEK-FvM] |
| Global meltdowns | Every 10–15 years | [hpbDmEK-FvM] |
| Deep crashes | Roughly every 5–6 years, with 15–20% corrections in between | [sbHzNd2iA8k] |
| Normal pre-Covid corrections (2010–20) | About 11–30% | [KDSbZ0oW2U0] |

- **Inconsistency:** the crash spacing differs between videos (10–15 years versus 5–6 years).
- **Nested cycles:** a decadal cycle of 8–10 years, a cycle of about 3 years, and a 1-year cycle with 5–7% dips [dERi8MUU5es].

**Damage below the surface.** Many stocks are down 25–30% while the index is down only 13–14%, so the median stock hurts more than the headline suggests [M4Z-Gi4gGag]. He cites a median stock correction of about 32–35% [KUlNy1_Rk9M].

**Time correction versus price correction.**

- A time correction ends roughly flat after weeks of sideways movement. His example: Nifty at about 21,800, then about 21,850 roughly 45 days later [Pm_1kb8Fku0].
- A price correction is a measurable fall; his example is Nifty Bank down 8.7% [Pm_1kb8Fku0].
- A price correction can turn into a time correction [V41BUpPK3E4].

**Warning signs about the pace of declines.**

- **Orderly versus vertical.** Slow pullbacks are healthier than vertical ones. His example of healthy: Nifty 500 down 4.3% in 5 weeks [fFUPLUEhzVo].
- **"Wolf is coming".** Runs of shallow 5–16% dips make traders casual just before a 10–20% fall. He gives Nifty 2005–08 and 2017–20 as examples [Y4TewQHVhvc].

### 2.3 Bottoming and follow-through (long side)

**Bottom sequence (from O'Neil).** He describes four stages:

1. A long fall, after which people feel it cannot fall further.
2. Breadth damage: many new lows, leaders falling, closes near the daily lows.
3. Panic selling, possibly after a 7–8% relief rally.
4. A trigger.

He placed India in stage 2 at the time of these videos [V41BUpPK3E4, iWa7MbGawvU].

**Triggers.** He argues a durable bottom does not form without one. His examples: the 2016 election, the 2019 corporate tax cut, 2020 liquidity and Fed actions [V41BUpPK3E4]. He links liquidity to direction more broadly [ywccnKeWXNo]:

- Easing does not produce an immediate rally; the low typically comes 4–8 months later.
- He uses Druckenmiller's formula: liquidity plus economy plus sentiment.
- Even after easing he wants technical confirmation: higher highs on stronger volume on the weekly chart, with breadth expanding.

**Follow-through rule (his tweak of O'Neil).** The low day is day 0 [B1ynZNvqesE]:

| Phase | Window | What he wants |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 to day 4–5 | A strong up candle on volume above the prior session |
| 2 | Day 4 to day 10 | No undercut of the first rally day's low; more wide-range up candles on rising volume |
| 3 | By day 15 | The wide-range, strong-volume rally has arrived; if it first comes on day 16–17 or later, the rally is weak |

He notes, citing O'Neil, that a follow-through can still fail and make a new low. It is a tool to anticipate a bottom, not confirm it [B1ynZNvqesE]. Clarity is medium, and it appears in a single video.

**Minimum structure.** He wants a higher high and higher low on the Nifty 75-minute chart, with price above the recent swing pivots. He calls this the bare minimum, not sufficient alone [-qQntPmfe-s].

**Confirming a bottom:**

- Price back above both the 50-day and 200-day, sustained, with a range formed [M4Z-Gi4gGag].
- By the time that happens, price is usually already 10–15% off the bottom. Traders can resume once Nifty 500 sustains above its 50-day [hpbDmEK-FvM].
- For a W-shaped base, wait for the middle-peak pivot to be taken out (about 25,300–25,350 then), then scale in quickly. Stay conservative until then [d3-OMTGOGx8].

**The 50-day as a test zone:**

- In downtrends the index habitually rebounds to test the 50-day [-qQntPmfe-s].
- In bear markets the 50-day acts as resistance. Rallies 1–4% above it often reverse; he cites 2024–25 and 2008 [KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- Judge intermediate versus final bottom by behaviour at the 50-day and about 2–3% above it [KUlNy1_Rk9M].
- The 50-day is also where corrections tend to base. Holding there sets up a fresh move; repeated closes below mean more selling [d3-OMTGOGx8].

**Bear-market rallies.** A big bounce alone proves nothing:

| Episode | Rally sizes he cites | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Nasdaq 100, 2000–02 | About 30–50% (41%, 43.8%, about 50%) | [20BxPDdAZAE] |
| 2008 | Retracements of about 15–30% | [20BxPDdAZAE] |
| Indian bear phases (2011–13, 2015, 2018–20) | 8–27% (10%, 7–8%, 15%, 9%, 12%, 17%, 27%) | [KUlNy1_Rk9M] |
| Nifty 2008 | Rallies failed at the 50% retracement of the fall | [d7cwBnW78XI] |
| Nifty 2018 | Price held above the 50–61.8% retracement, which he read as strength | [d7cwBnW78XI] |

- Across all past corrections he reviewed, V-shaped bounces that looked like bottoms came before the real lows [V41BUpPK3E4].
- He also describes bear-market rallies as led by the most beaten-down sectors, creating misplaced optimism; trapped late buyers then fuel the next leg down [20BxPDdAZAE].

**Leadership after a bottom:**

- New leaders come from sectors that fell least, not from the prior cycle's leaders [iWa7MbGawvU, B1ynZNvqesE].
- Sectors whose internal breadth improved while the index fell became the later leaders [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- Stocks that hold near their 50-day while the index falls belong on the watchlist [T4849hhdwTo].
- Recent breakouts holding during a ~10% index fall suggest a normal correction; broad failures suggest a sustained one [mR0HMssJek8].

### 2.4 Distribution and top warnings (short/defensive side)

The notes here are thin. There is no distribution-day counting rule. What he does give:

- **Bank Nifty example.** Heavy distribution candles at resistance, then a bearish engulfing candle at the 50-day, plus falling relative strength. A flag breakdown and a ~2,200-point fall from about 51,000 followed [hKnYYRpd63s].
- **Late bull markets.** After about two years, heavy-volume stalls can start a bear market (citing O'Neil) [hKnYYRpd63s].
- **Quality of the bounce.** A rebound not led by the prior leaders, followed by a sharper drop, signals distribution [fFUPLUEhzVo, dERi8MUU5es].
- **Divergence at highs.** Each new high in the Russell 3000, S&P 500 and Nifty 500 came with fewer stocks participating. Nifty 500 breadth peaked around 90% in 2020–21, about 80% at a recent high, and about 60% in a pullback. He says breadth thinning cannot predict a crash [elpQ8wyLZLQ].
- **Breadth leads.** It tends to weaken first at tops [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- **Euphoria warnings:**
  - When everything feels easy: tighten stops, avoid leverage, preserve gains [r11n7ztY2YY].
  - Trailing 12-month returns above roughly 20% across many markets: poor short-term risk/reward [r11n7ztY2YY].
  - Eight straight up weeks in Nifty: needs consolidation [q26J58h5Qt8].
  - Very positive media and IPO hype: do not commit much money (citing O'Neil) [g3mcqf6rqoc].

### 2.5 Breadth: % of stocks above the 200-day

This is his most-cited gauge.

**Thresholds as he states them:**

| Reading | His interpretation | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| 70–80% | Strong, broad bull market | [LWoEC9OjajE, Qu49zHwEGwo] |
| 50–60% | Wanted before long-term reliability returns | [Jpu536_AmGc] |
| At least 50% | "Okay"; the healthy floor | [Qu49zHwEGwo, fFUPLUEhzVo] |
| Below 50% | Narrow market | [SbtFCxbIB1w] |
| 35–38% | Declines accelerate below this | [fFUPLUEhzVo] |
| Below 40%, more serious near 30% | Heavy selling returns even in large caps | [Qu49zHwEGwo] |
| Below about 40% | Gains tend to be given back | [N2dUTcb9CzQ] |
| 32–33% | Not buying many individual stocks | [6qExRlD11sE] |
| About 37% | Sharper pullbacks and swift reversals | [x2RbjJp1eCI] |
| 29–30% | Even leaders sell off at random (citing O'Neil) | [yYwiSDVwgyA] |
| 27% | "Decimation" | [rxqOUxO_vP4] |
| 21–22% | Failed V-recoveries, churn, frustration | [Sb6KTt64YKE] |
| 17% / 16% | Very poor | [-qQntPmfe-s, B1ynZNvqesE] |
| About 10% (March 2025) | Extreme low | [WI0tdUjH_Vg] |
| About 94% below | Extreme breadth damage | [V41BUpPK3E4] |

**Two readings of the same low numbers.**

- **For traders:** stay out or trade small.
- **For investors:** the same depressed readings are accumulation zones.
  - Multi-year bottoms formed when the share of stocks "below" the 200-day reached 8–12%, possibly 12–14%. The wording is likely inverted; he probably means "above" [6GqBBVt6fUs].
  - With about 80% below the 50-day and 65–68% below the 200-day (March 2024), people should be buying. With only about 5% below the 200-day (July 2024), the time to deploy fresh money has passed [r11n7ztY2YY].
  - Extreme lows in a breadth-style indicator he shows (2017, 2022–23) preceded the next swing [VCNsjVuGoWQ].
  - A low reading that is rising after a trough (48% on 1 June 2025, up from about 10%) means the trend is immature, so dips are opportunities [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- **How he reconciles them:** markets swing like a pendulum between trading phases and investing phases [rxqOUxO_vP4]. Low-breadth distance from the averages is "an opportunity zone for investors rather than traders" [Sb6KTt64YKE]. In weak breadth, lean toward 12–18 month accumulation rather than short trades [6GqBBVt6fUs].

**Tension in the mid-range.** About 50–51% is called "okay" [fFUPLUEhzVo] and also "a weak, narrow market" [VCNsjVuGoWQ]. About 52% (higher-high/higher-low measure) is called resilient [YFsvQXZZt1g].

**Slope variant.** The share of stocks whose 200-day and 50-day averages are rising matters more than the share above them, he says, attributing this to O'Neil and Minervini [WI0tdUjH_Vg].

**Thresholds:**

| Reading | His interpretation | Sources |

| About 60% below | Thin; index moves prone to failure | [N5bb0Cu8RAs] |
| 10–11% | A 15-month low | [dERi8MUU5es] |

**Speed of the split.** If 50-day breadth falls much faster than 200-day breadth, the damage is fast but not deep. His example: 65% to 44% (–21 points) versus 60% to 50% (–10 points) over 10 sessions [0nbQs92kYuY]. He notes 200-day breadth had swung between roughly 39% and 61%, so 50% was mid-range, not extreme [0nbQs92kYuY].

**Further readings of the split:**

- About 40% above the 50-day with about 70% above the 200-day: a silent cool-off, readable either way [d3-OMTGOGx8].
- About 80% above the 50-day but only about 50% above the 200-day: expect intermittent 4–5% pullbacks [g3mcqf6rqoc].

**One-day "breadth hit".** A double-digit single-day drop in the share above the averages [S--7ta4FmbY]. His example:

- Before the sell-off: 62% above the 50-day and 58% above the 200-day.
- On the day: roughly 12 points off 50-day breadth and 9 points off 200-day breadth.
- By Friday: 65% and 58%.

**Extended share.** Track how many above-average stocks are already extended, roughly 8–15% above the 50-day [LWoEC9OjajE, SbtFCxbIB1w, Oq4FjWEDbd0]:

- Many extended names means few buyable setups.
- Time and price corrections that pull extended names back create opportunities.
- Example: 50-day breadth went from 41% (about half extended) to 48% (21% extended) [SbtFCxbIB1w].

**Shakeout versus breakdown filter.** This is the clearest use of breadth as a decision rule [8QeR8FmYYg4]:

- **Shakeout or consolidation:** an index dip below the 50-day while breadth erodes slowly (about 64% to 51%) and structures stay intact.
- **Real breakdown:** the same price break coinciding with a fast breadth drop to about 45% over 7–8 sessions.

**How breakdowns spread.**

- Key pivot lows broke first in large caps, then in small and micro caps (15 September) [sIo5XQqZ0b0].
- Deterioration moved from the edges inward: micro caps and SMEs (mid-November), then the total market index (December), then Nifty 500 (January) [rxqOUxO_vP4].

**Four-gauge dashboard.** He combines:

- % above the 50-day
- % above the 200-day
- % in higher-high/higher-low structure

### 2.8 Drawdown-bucket breadth

**Method** [V41BUpPK3E4]:

- Take a liquid universe of about 1,300–1,900 stocks.
- Exclude circuit-hit and illiquid names.
- Bucket each stock's fall from its recent high in 5-point steps (–20%, –25% … –75%).
- Compare the distribution with past cycles: 2016, 2018, 2020, 2022 and 2024–25.

**Readings he gave:**

- 35–40% of liquid stocks were down 20–45%.
- He judged this deeper than 2022, when most stocks held their 200-day, and more damaging than 2018 [V41BUpPK3E4].
- Small/mid/micro names were typically down 35–40% from September–October 2024 [iWa7MbGawvU].

### 2.9 Advance/decline, new highs/lows

The notes are thin here.

- **Advance/decline:** he tracks Nifty 500 advancing versus declining stocks weekly, not daily [N5bb0Cu8RAs]. He used an intraday ratio of about 3:1 as supportive context for a large-cap ORB breakout [hk4d8AxbQ3A]. No thresholds or cumulative A/D line are taught.
- **New lows:** they appear only as "many stocks making new lows daily" in the breadth-damage stage [iWa7MbGawvU].
- **New highs:** no new-high/new-low rule is given.

### 2.10 Participation: cap segments, equal weight, sectors, global

**Retail barometer.** Small, mid and micro caps carry retail sentiment, so their recovery matters:

- A rising small/mid/micro versus large-cap ratio while large caps range means the market is broadening [Qu49zHwEGwo].
- Wait for midcap and smallcap strength before calling a recovery [e34G88-BKI4].
- He wants more small/mid/micro stocks above their 200-day [jkga2zdA9dY].
- Breadth improvement comes first; the rally broadens afterward [rocXNq3_1Zw].

**Narrow large-cap rallies:**

- Nifty 100 holding strong RS versus Nifty 500 while small and micro caps lag [Yxo-rYOCuAo].
- Cap-segment breadth on 1 June 2025: micro about 36–37%, small 43%, mid about 60%, large about 70% above the 200-day [WI0tdUjH_Vg].
- Structure improves progressively from micro to large caps, which he links to absent FIIs [0hnffbj5pgE→ see 0Hnffbj5pgE].
- The opposite case: small and micro caps up 18–22% since 1 April versus Nifty 50 at about 7%. He reads the headline index as understating the market [kwbfXjRtEkk].

**Heavyweights versus equal weight:**

- Nifty Equal Weight at an all-time high while about ten heavyweights sit 25–30% below their highs [r63sarc_qV8].
- Rising Nifty 50 names are mostly laggards while the heavyweights stay weak, which caps the index [zHzq3uAFJFs].
- A cap-weighted index can hide weak internals; an index up 1% with few stocks above their 50-day is weak [WI0tdUjH_Vg].

**"Divided" or "fractured" market.** The broad index can be in Stage 3 or a roughly 15% range while pockets are in Stage 2 [r63sarc_qV8, kwbfXjRtEkk, S--7ta4FmbY, rbSj4pOg3_I]. In this state, about half the sectors are up and half down [rocXNq3_1Zw, butE8gDPXxw]. What to do:

- Scan for stocks up more than 50% in a year [butE8gDPXxw].
- Dispersion counts since 30 March 2026: 37 stocks up 100%+, 262 up 50–100%, 469 up 30–50%, 954 up 10–30% [SNrzU-z3RkE].
- Stay only in strong pockets and be nimble. Example: Large-Midcap 250 had 72% above its 200-day while the broad market had 44% [SbtFCxbIB1w].

**Sector breadth:**

- A healthy sector has at least 60% of its stocks above both the 50-day and 200-day [Jpu536_AmGc].
- A sector breakout led by 2 of 10–15 names is unreliable [6GqBBVt6fUs].
- A sector's Stage 2 needs follow-through and more constituents breaking out [NUDjIohACWs].
- Sector-count gauge: 36 of 56 sectors stayed leading over a week; lagging rose from 3 to 7 [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- A rising leading count while the index is flat means a "silent bull market" [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- Broadening means more sectors join with Stage 2 moves [c-0bHPew1fg, g3mcqf6rqoc, lRUDp336l7Q].

**Risk appetite:**

- Cyclicals (metals, capital goods, autos, defence, cement) up while defensives fall means risk-on [6K1LOJqZYEg].
- Nifty High Beta 50 as a risk-appetite read [lRUDp336l7Q].
- During index consolidations, defensive stocks give more setups than high-beta ones [CXJZMbE_d5o].

**Global check:**

- Weekly, check whether the Russell 2000 and 3000 move with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq [LWoEC9OjajE].
- Rally quality needs three things: rising breadth, the right sector leaders, and a global rather than India-only move [-qQntPmfe-s].

### 2.11 Consolidation, ranges and how often markets trend

## 3. How a program would test it

**Data needed:**

- Daily OHLCV for survivorship-bias-free constituent histories: Nifty 500 / Total Market, and ideally the full NSE liquid universe.
- Cap-segment and sector indices.
- India VIX.
- US and Canadian equivalents for the same tests.

**1. Index MA regime and breakout expectancy** [Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA, M4Z-Gi4gGag, 8QeR8FmYYg4].

- **Rule:** classify each day as index above both averages / between them / below both, the last also with lower highs and lower lows. Use Nifty 500, with Nifty 50 as a check.
- **Measure:** for a fixed breakout definition on constituents, compare 20/60-day forward return, win rate and maximum adverse excursion by regime.
- **Variant:** replace "below the 50-day" with "below the 50-day and below the nearest swing low" (the 50 DMA pivot).
- **Also test:** forward index returns after 50/200 crosses versus unconditional returns.

**2. % above the 200-day thresholds** [Qu49zHwEGwo, fFUPLUEhzVo, LWoEC9OjajE, 6qExRlD11sE].

- **Buckets:** <30%, 30–40%, 40–50%, 50–70%, >70%.
- **Measures:** index 1–3 month forward return and maximum drawdown; breakout follow-through (reach +5% before –5%); frequency of ≥4–5% index pullbacks.
- **Separate investor test:** when the reading first drops below 15% (and separately the literal 8–12% band), measure 6/12/24-month forward index returns [6GqBBVt6fUs, V41BUpPK3E4, WI0tdUjH_Vg].

**3. Shakeout-versus-breakdown filter** [8QeR8FmYYg4].

- **Condition:** the index closes below its 50-day.
- **Split:** cases where the higher-high/higher-low share fell more than 10 points in the prior 8 sessions versus cases where it fell slowly.
- **Measure:** 20–60 day forward returns.
- **Prerequisite:** a fixed swing-pivot definition (for example, a 5-bar fractal), because he never specifies one.

**4. Follow-through day as specified** [B1ynZNvqesE].

- **Lows:** mark swing lows after 8%+ index declines.
- **Phases:** day 1–5 up day on volume above the prior day; no undercut of the first rally day's low through day 10; first wide-range high-volume up day by day 15 versus after.
- **Measure:** compare the rate of new lows within 60 days and the 3–6 month return of each group.

**5. Distance below the 200-day as a bottom zone** [Sb6KTt64YKE, yYwiSDVwgyA].

- **Buckets:** first closes 4–8%, 8–14%, and more than 14% below the 200-day.
- **Measure:** 1–3 month forward returns and further drawdown.
- **Purpose:** this also settles which of his two O'Neil figures fits Indian data.

**6. 50-day resistance in downtrends** [KUlNy1_Rk9M, -qQntPmfe-s].

- **Condition:** index below a falling 200-day.
- **Measure:** how often it touches the 50-day within 15 days of a swing low; how often a close above the 50-day (up to 4% above) reverses below it within N days; forward return after each outcome.

**7. Breadth divergence at highs and narrow leadership** [elpQ8wyLZLQ, WI0tdUjH_Vg, r63sarc_qV8].

- **Divergence:** at each index new high, compare % above the 50-day with the value at the prior high. Lower breadth means divergent. Compare 3–12 month forward returns and drawdowns.
- **Narrow leadership:** rolling equal-weight minus cap-weight return, and small/large ratio slope, as predictors of index drawdown.

**8. Extended-share and opportunity count** [SbtFCxbIB1w, LWoEC9OjajE].

- **Measure:** the share of above-50-day stocks that are more than 8% above it.
- **Test:** whether a high share predicts lower forward returns on new breakout entries.

## 4. Transfer to US and Canadian markets

**Usable as-is in the US (computation and logic):**

- % of constituents above the 50/200-day.
- Higher-high/higher-low share and Stage 2 share.
- Index-versus-average regime and follow-through day. These were O'Neil's US tools in the first place.
- Drawdown buckets.
- Divergence at highs.
- Russell 2000/3000 versus S&P 500/Nasdaq. He already uses this [LWoEC9OjajE] and quoted S&P 500 breadth of about 76–81% [d3-OMTGOGx8].

**Needs adapting:**

- **Thresholds.** His levels (50/40/30% above the 200-day; 60/50/45% in higher-high/higher-low) come from broad Indian universes of 500–3,000+ names heavy in small and micro caps. They must be re-fitted for the S&P 500 or the much smaller, sector-concentrated S&P/TSX Composite (about 220 names, dominated by financials, energy and materials). On the TSX, a sector move can swing breadth by itself.
- **Index equivalents:**
  - Total Market / Nifty 500 maps to the Russell 3000 or TSX Composite plus the TSX SmallCap.
  - Smallcap/microcap maps to the Russell 2000 / Microcap or the TSX SmallCap / TSXV.
  - Equal weight maps to the RSP-style S&P 500 equal weight.
  - Nifty High Beta 50 maps to an S&P high-beta index.
- **Sector-count gauges.** His 56 Indian sectors need a GICS industry-level equivalent.
- **Costs.** ETF accumulation in weak regimes is easy in both markets. Thin TSX small caps raise costs for the "trade the strong pocket" approach.

**India-only:**

- Nifty Bank as the sentiment driver [r63sarc_qV8].
