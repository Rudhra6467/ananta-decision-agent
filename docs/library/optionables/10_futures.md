# Futures

## 1. What he teaches here and how it fits

Optionables mainly runs a short-option (income) book. Futures play four supporting roles in his method:

- **Capital-efficient long equity.** He holds stock futures instead of cash shares and parks the spare capital in a liquid or debt fund [PBQXg7M_NVg, BFZXz79936Y, w5oNCUY0Yyg].
- **Hedge.** He shorts index futures against the long portfolio and treats the rolled futures premium as income [BFZXz79936Y, w5oNCUY0Yyg, o2JJ5p03cyc].
- **Pair trades.** He pairs same-sector futures, picked either on fundamentals or on a price-ratio band [E5rCsOzaXyY, gFcyYYtgnOE].
- **Directional overlay.** He takes discretionary index and stock futures trades around ranges, breakouts, valuation levels and events, usually with stated levels [DLJ0AmH6hS0, xV7EMUdSL4w, urrT8zpnGus, km4uw-zxRZM and others].

He also covers mechanics:
- basis and its convergence at expiry [w5oNCUY0Yyg];
- monthly rolling [PBQXg7M_NVg];
- old Indian intraday margin practice [AGATtid-fhU];
- physical delivery, using the 2020 negative oil price [p1Rt5u412Ec];
- rollover data as a sentiment read [cVxwgVbHYKY];
- one payoff comparison of futures against options [l4jQUXK6UOc].

Everything below is his claim or practice, to be treated as a hypothesis. Most of it comes from single videos and live-position commentary, not from tested rules.

**Drift over time (inference from dates).**
- About 5 years ago, futures appear mostly as an investment substitute, a hedge and a statistical-pair vehicle.
- About 4 years ago, they appear as fundamental pairs and staged short index trades.
- About 3 years ago, they appear as tactical index longs at range edges with daily-close stops, and around the Budget.

## 2. The ideas

### 2.1 Stock futures instead of cash shares (synthetic long + liquid fund)

**What it is**
- Buy the stock future rather than the shares. Price P&L is roughly the same, but only margin is posted.
- Put the rest of the capital in a debt or liquid fund.
- Expected return is the futures price gain plus interest on the unused capital [PBQXg7M_NVg].

**Numbers**
- Example capital is about Rs 10 lakh, and margin is roughly 15–25% of contract value. Both figures are implied, and the captions are unclear [PBQXg7M_NVg].
- He rolls about one day before expiry and expects about 10% a year from futures [BFZXz79936Y].
- His portfolio once showed about Rs 90 lakh of long stock futures (e.g., Nestle 100 qty at ~18,000; TCS 300 at ~3,000) beside about Rs 3.15 crore of cash equity [w5oNCUY0Yyg].

**Conditions**
- Use only liquid large caps that are on the F&O list and are held for a long horizon.
- Named examples: TCS, Reliance, Infosys, HUL, Dabur, Kotak [PBQXg7M_NVg]. This is low-clarity, judgment-based guidance.

**Disadvantages he names**
1. **No dividends.** He sets the lost dividend against the interest earned on freed capital [PBQXg7M_NVg].
2. **Monthly expiry.** This forces repeated rolls, each with costs and an inter-month price difference [PBQXg7M_NVg].

**Tension: how much carry matters**
- Elsewhere he says carrying costs matter little, because only a fraction of capital is margin and the net return on margin is high [BFZXz79936Y].
- That is the opposite side of the same basis he says a short hedger *earns* (2.2). A long roller pays roughly that premium each month.
- He does not reconcile the two.

**Living with mark-to-market losses**
- He says he is untroubled by futures MTM losses as long as the options book does well [kmfo3YKUF4Q].

**How often taught:** once as a lesson [PBQXg7M_NVg], with practice references in [BFZXz79936Y, w5oNCUY0Yyg, kmfo3YKUF4Q].

### 2.2 Basis, convergence and delivery

**Index basis**
- Nifty futures normally trade at a premium to spot of about 30–80 points at the start of the month, averaging about 50.
- At the time of the video he observed 30 points on the next-month contract.
- The futures converge to spot at expiry, so a short futures hedge "collects" the premium each roll if spot is unchanged [w5oNCUY0Yyg].
- Clarity is high, but it is a single video.

**Physical delivery (oil, April 2020)**
- A buyer holding a delivery-settled future through expiry must take delivery. Expiry was 21 April 2020.
- Holders who could not take barrels had to sell at any price, even below zero.
- Sellers with no storage would pay to offload [p1Rt5u412Ec].
- This is presented as an explanation of an event, not a trading rule.

### 2.3 Margin and leverage

**Old intraday system (India)**
- Brokers reported margin only at end of day. A flat-by-close trader showed zero margin.
- Brokers therefore extended 10x–40x intraday leverage.
- One Nifty futures lot (about Rs 1.5 lakh margin) could be traded with about Rs 3,000–10,000 [AGATtid-fhU].
- This is descriptive, India-specific and historical.

**Other margin assumptions**
- Pair trading: margin of about one-third of notional is implied. He states 3–5 lakh of capital for 10 lakh per leg [E5rCsOzaXyY, gFcyYYtgnOE].
- Synthetic long: 15–25% (unclear) [PBQXg7M_NVg].

### 2.4 Rollover data as sentiment

- On monthly expiry he reads whether participants are building long or short positions in next-month futures, as a sentiment gauge [cVxwgVbHYKY].
- No thresholds, horizon or decision rule are given.

### 2.5 Hedging with index futures

**Portfolio hedge**
- He shorts Nifty futures against long stock futures or cash equity [BFZXz79936Y, w5oNCUY0Yyg].
- In a rally the hedge lost heavily: about Rs 4.37 lakh on the September future, about 3.95 lakh unrealised. Equity gains offset it, and he calls that acceptable [o2JJ5p03cyc].
- No hedge ratio, beta adjustment or rule for when to put the hedge on or lift it is given.

**Futures beside the short-option book**
- He presents futures as a cushion on some days:
  - futures +1.97 lakh, of which Nifty futures +1.52 lakh;
  - net day +1.25 lakh despite option losses [5_i637lBoPU].
- On other days they were the main source of loss:
  - futures −60k vs options −40k [tKlpB5RfdFA];
  - futures −80k vs options +2 lakh [d-_3zxoHyEg];
  - futures −7 lakh, about −9 lakh total on a crash day [XLBtvv6Jq18].
- On the crash day he cut options early so that total MTM would not exceed about Rs 10 lakh [XLBtvv6Jq18]. This is the closest thing to a stated combined-book loss cap.
- Rising markets help both futures/equity and the bullish option legs [ufH0zRUyuIw].
- Taken together, the evidence is mixed: futures are directional risk added to a short-volatility book, not a reliable hedge for it.

**Options hedging a future**
- Long 5 ETH perpetual at 1,625, stop 1,595, plus an options hedge against a violent move.
- The stop hit and the trade lost about $157. The hedge also expired worthless [VmiJLxaYPUQ].

**Futures + short call system (low clarity)**
- Daily at about 3 pm: buy 1 lot Nifty futures and sell an ATM call (possibly about 100 points away; the captions are unclear).
- Hold overnight to capture positive opens, then close the next day [sk4_m8Yfzu0].
- No backtest numbers are given in the notes.

### 2.6 Futures vs options for a directional view

**His example**
- Nifty at 16,200; the 15,900 call is at about 350.
- If Nifty falls 400 points, the call drops to about 100: the short gains about 250.
- If Nifty rises 400 points, the call goes to about 715: the short loses about 365.
- He concludes that a short ITM call is a worse bearish vehicle than short futures [l4jQUXK6UOc].

**A caution on the arithmetic**
- Short futures would gain or lose 400 either way.
- So the ITM call actually loses *less* than futures on the way up. What it gives up is the downside gain (250 vs 400).
- His point is the call's own unfavourable up/down asymmetry. A tester should state the comparison carefully.

### 2.7 Pair trading with futures

The setup and legs below are the ones he gives. The exit and stop rules are largely missing.

**General rules [E5rCsOzaXyY]**
- Long one stock and short another at the same time. The edge is relative performance, not market direction.
- Both legs must be in the same sector, so that sector news hits both and only company news separates them. A large bank against a small FMCG company is his example of a bad pair.
- The companies must be of similar market cap. TCS at about 20x Mindtree is his example of a bad pair.
- Good pairs he names: TCS–Infosys, HDFC–ICICI, Kotak–HDFC, Hero–Bajaj, REC–PFC.

**(a) Fundamental pair trading** [E5rCsOzaXyY]. He coined the term himself and says he is unsure whether it already exists.

*Selection*
- Compare market share, product range, sales growth and net-profit growth.
- Go long the improving firm and short the stagnant one.

*Example (education only, not a recommendation)*
- Long Bajaj Auto futures: 10 lots, about 2,500 shares.
- Short Hero MotoCorp futures: 10 lots, about 3,000 shares.

*His worked return*
- Assume Bajaj +10% and Hero +5% a year, i.e., 5% outperformance.
- Long gains about 8.75 lakh; short loses about 3.75 lakh; net about 5 lakh on "about 87 lakh notional."
- The same logic is claimed for a falling market.

*My inference on the numbers*
- The legs look unequal in rupee terms: roughly 87.5 lakh long against 75 lakh short.
- So the pair carries some net long exposure. The figure of about 15% return on margin assumes margin is about one-third of notional.

*Hindsight illustration*
- Infosys went from about 500 to 1,800 while TCS went from about 1,700 to 3,700 [E5rCsOzaXyY].

*Live outcome*
- The Hero–Bajaj pair later lost. Hero rallied on its EV-company investment while Bajaj lagged [kmfo3YKUF4Q].

**(b) Statistical ratio pairs** [gFcyYYtgnOE]

*Rules*
- Track the ratio A/B with mean ± 2 SD bands.
- **Short side:** if the ratio is above mean + 2 SD, short A and buy B.
  - Example: HDFC Bank/Kotak at about 0.92 vs an upper band of about 0.908.
- **Long side:** if the ratio is below mean − 2 SD, buy A and short B.
  - Example: March 2020, ratio below about 0.713.
- Exit when the ratio returns to its mean.

*Worked examples (both low clarity, cherry-picked, no sample statistics)*
- **Dec 2017 → late Feb 2018:** about 1.3 lakh profit on 10 lakh per leg, using a stated 3–5 lakh of capital. The captions garble the prices.
- **Mar → Jul 2020:** about 12% net, despite a losing long leg, through the crash.

*Not specified*
- Lookback window for the mean and SD.
- Stop if the ratio keeps diverging.
- Leg sizing.

## 3. How a program would test it

**1. Synthetic long vs cash**

*Test:* For liquid large caps, compare two portfolios, each rebalanced monthly, over 10+ years:
- **Synthetic:** long front-month futures, rolled one day before expiry, with the remaining capital at a liquid-fund rate.
- **Cash:** the same stock held in cash, dividends reinvested.

Report the return difference, Sharpe and drawdown, net of roll slippage, fees and taxes, and the dividend-vs-interest gap separately [PBQXg7M_NVg, BFZXz79936Y].

*Data:*
- daily futures settlement prices for near and next months;
- spot prices and dividends;
- a money-market yield series;
- transaction costs.

**2. Index basis at start of month**

*Test:* Is the average front-month Nifty premium over spot, measured on the first trading day after expiry, positive and about 40–50 points? Does a monthly-rolled short earn about that when spot is unchanged? Express the result also as an annualised percentage, so it can be compared with rates minus dividend yield [w5oNCUY0Yyg].

*Data:* daily futures and spot closes, expiry calendar, risk-free rate, index dividend yield.

**3. Ratio-band pairs**

*Test:* For same-sector pairs:
- trade short A / long B when the A/B ratio closes above mean + 2 SD;
- trade long A / short B when it closes below mean − 2 SD;
- exit at the mean.

Report hit rate, holding time, the share of trades that never revert, and P&L per unit of margin. Run it over several lookbacks (e.g., 60/120/250 days), with and without a divergence stop, and with equal-notional legs [gFcyYYtgnOE].

*Data:* daily adjusted prices or futures for sector pairs, plus F&O eligibility history.

**4. Fundamental pairs**

*Test:* In same-sector, similar-cap pairs (cap ratio below, say, 2), go long the firm with higher trailing 3-year sales and profit growth and short the lower-growth firm, equal notional. Measure the 1–3 year relative return and its beta to the index. Compare against pairs with large cap ratios [E5rCsOzaXyY].

*Data:* point-in-time fundamentals (sales, profit, market share where available), sector classification, market caps, prices.

**5. Range-edge / consolidation index longs**

*Test:* When the index trades inside a multi-week range after an uptrend, compare two entry methods:
- buy at the lower edge;
- buy inside the range with a stop on a daily close below the range low.

Compare forward 1–2 week returns with entries at random range positions [xV7EMUdSL4w, DLJ0AmH6hS0].

*Data:* daily index OHLC, plus an objective range definition (e.g., N-week high/low with a maximum width).

**6. Weekly breakout retest**

*Test:* Take stocks with a weekly close above a range high that held at least 52 weeks. Measure forward 4–12 week returns with a stop just below the level, and check whether the level holds on retest [urrT8zpnGus].

*Data:* weekly OHLC for the stock universe.

**7. VIX filter for longs**

*Test:* After a VIX spike above 25, compare long index futures entered while VIX is still above 25 with longs entered only once VIX closes below 25. Use 1–4 week forward returns and drawdown [PiY8pG6Jox4].

*Data:* India VIX daily, index futures.

**8. Rollover build-up as signal / 3 pm overnight system**

*Rollover signal:* Does net long vs short OI build-up in next-month futures during expiry week predict the index direction over the next week [cVxwgVbHYKY]?
- *Data:* futures OI by contract, plus price change to classify build-up.

*3 pm system:* Separately, test the 3 pm long future + short ATM call, closed the next day, from 2012 onward, after costs [sk4_m8Yfzu0].
- *Data:* intraday futures and option prices at 3 pm and the next session.

## 4. Transfer

**Applies largely as is (logic only)**
- Pair-trading logic: same sector, similar size, ratio bands, fundamental selection.
- Breakout and range-edge rules.
- Daily-close and weekly-close stops.
- Staged covering of shorts.
- Basis convergence at expiry.
- The payoff comparison of futures against ITM options.
- Rollover/OI as a sentiment read.
- The dividend-vs-interest trade-off.

All of these need only long/short access to related instruments.

**Needs adapting**

*Single-stock futures*
- Liquid stock futures are an Indian feature.
- In the US, single-stock futures are limited (the notes flag this).
- In Canada, Montreal Exchange lists some share futures, but liquidity should be verified.
- The synthetic long would more practically use deep ITM calls or synthetic options, margin accounts or swaps. Each has a different carry.

*Index instruments*
- Use S&P 500 (ES/MES) or S&P/TSX 60 (SXF) futures instead of Nifty/Bank Nifty.
- Sector pairs need US or Canadian sector peers. Canadian banks, energy and rails give natural same-sector pairs.

*Basis*
- Basis is roughly interest rate minus dividend yield. The 30–80 point Nifty figure does not transfer.
- TSX's higher dividend yield implies a smaller premium.
- US and Canadian index futures are quarterly, so rolls are less frequent than India's monthly.

*Margin, costs, cash yield*
- Margin rates and exchange or broker rules differ.
- The liquid-fund proxy becomes T-bills or a money-market fund.
- Taxes differ (e.g., US 60/40 treatment on index futures).

*Volatility filter*
- The VIX threshold of 25 is for India VIX. Calibrate separately for VIX or VIXC.

*Event trades*
- Union Budget trades map only loosely to Fed or federal budget events, or to earnings.

**India-only**
- The old end-of-day margin reporting and 10–40x intraday leverage [AGATtid-fhU].
- The specific stocks, F&O list and turnover figures [PBQXg7M_NVg, 0qmiy0rYOfE].
- The specific historical positions and price levels.

**Note on delivery**
- Physical delivery risk [p1Rt5u412Ec] matters in US commodity futures but not in cash-settled index futures.

## 5. Gaps and doubts

**Thin evidence**
- Nearly every idea rests on one video.
- Many items are descriptions of live positions, not rules.
- The pair examples are explicitly hindsight or cherry-picked, with garbled captions [gFcyYYtgnOE, E5rCsOzaXyY].

**Unclear numbers**
- Synthetic-long margin of 15–25% and capital of about Rs 10 lakh [PBQXg7M_NVg].
- Navin Fluorine stop about 4,500 against a 4,200 breakout level [urrT8zpnGus].
- The call strike offset in the 3 pm system [sk4_m8Yfzu0].
- "87 lakh notional" in the pair math, where the legs appear unequal [E5rCsOzaXyY].

**Unreconciled**
- Basis is called income for a short hedge [w5oNCUY0Yyg] but negligible as a cost for a long roller [BFZXz79936Y].
- The VIX < 25 rule against longs [PiY8pG6Jox4] sits beside scaling into longs during declines [0xrgICpggJI].
- Futures are called a cushion for the options book [5_i637lBoPU], yet several days show them as the main loss driver [tKlpB5RfdFA, XLBtvv6Jq18, d-_3zxoHyEg].

**Not covered in these notes**
- How to size a hedge ratio or beta-adjust index hedges.
- Rules for putting on or lifting the portfolio hedge.
- Stops, divergence limits or time limits for pair trades.
- Lookback choice for ratio bands.
- Quantified roll costs or a calendar-spread analysis.
- Contango/backwardation outside the oil episode.
- A thresholded rollover/OI signal.
- Position sizing beyond one "about 2% of capital" worst case [urrT8zpnGus] and one about Rs 10 lakh combined-MTM comfort level [XLBtvv6Jq18].

**Performance evidence**
- No backtest results are reported for any futures idea.
- The roughly 10% a year expectation [BFZXz79936Y] and the roughly 15% return on margin from pairs [E5rCsOzaXyY] are assumptions, not measured outcomes.
